# The hook standard (every script, every state)

The creator, 11 Nov 2026: *"please make hook so good, no one can leave… here hook is the main factor for everything."*

The hook is the first 30–40 seconds. It decides whether anyone watches the other 25 minutes, so it gets more time than any other part of the script. Every script in this repo meets this standard before it is handed over. It overrides the shorter hook notes in `docs/PLAYBOOK.md` where they differ.

## The shape (seven beats, about 90–120 spoken words)

| # | Beat | Job | Alabama (final) | Florida |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Familiar thing** | First words name something the viewer has used, drunk, sung or watched. No greeting, no "today we…", no title read. | "Sweet Home Alabama." | "The Gatorade in your gym bag?" |
| 2 | **Twist** | One line that flips it. | The band was from Florida. | Invented for a college football team called the Gators. |
| 3 | **Second familiar thing → twist** | Same move, bigger surprise. | Mardi Gras started in Mobile. | Disney World was bought in secret with fake company names. |
| 4 | **Third → the unbelievable one** | The fact people will repeat to a friend. | The Moon rocket was designed here. | America's oldest European city is in Florida, 55 years before the Mayflower. |
| 5 | **The turn (stakes)** | The dark or impossible fact that creates a contrast. Delivered slower. | People beaten on a bridge for trying to vote. | In 1900 the whole state had fewer people than the city of Boston. |
| 6 | **The question** | One sentence that the whole film answers. It is the title and thumbnail's promise. | "So how does one state do BOTH?" | "So how does a swamp almost nobody wanted become the third-biggest state in America?" |
| 7 | **The specific promise** | A concrete, slightly odd image that the answer starts with. Not "find out", not "stay". | "The answer is hiding on one street. Five blocks long." | "It starts with a doctor, a block of ice… and a mosquito." |

## The rules

1. **First three words are the hook.** Open on the familiar thing. No intro, no greeting, no channel name.
2. **Every line opens a loop before the last one closes.** Use "but", "and", "so", "?" to chain. If a sentence could be deleted without the next one breaking, delete it.
3. **Escalate.** Fun → surprising → unbelievable → dark. Never put the best fact first or last-but-one.
4. **One contrast defines the film** (rocket vs bridge; swamp vs superstate). The thumbnail shows it, the title names it, the hook asks it, the ending pays it off.
5. **Specific beats general.** Numbers, names, places, dates: "fewer people than Boston" beats "very few people".
6. **Short lines.** 6–14 words. One idea per line. Easy for Gemini to read in one breath.
7. **Body by 40 s.** Hook ≤ 120 words. The first line after the hook is already the story ("Let's go back…").
8. **Banned:** "stay to the end", "watch until the end", "keep watching", "in this video", "let's dive in", "buckle up", "you won't believe", "before we start", "make sure to subscribe", chapter names.
9. **True.** Every hook fact is fact-checked with a source in the project README, worded so it stays true (e.g. "oldest European city on the mainland", not "oldest city").
10. **Re-hooks every 2–3 minutes in the body:** a small open loop ("Remember that doctor? Hold that thought…" is banned; instead: "And the ice machine? It's about to matter."), a callback to a hook fact, or a new question.
11. **The ending pays off the question and the promise image** (the five blocks, the mosquito), then ends on a high note.

## How to write one

1. List 10 candidate familiar things for the state (products, songs, films, foods, phrases, places people visit).
2. Keep the four with the strongest true twist.
3. Find the contrast (then vs now, good vs bad, tiny vs huge) that the whole state's story answers.
4. Write three different hooks. Score each against the checklist below. Keep the best; steal lines from the others.
5. Read it aloud at speed. Any line where attention drops gets cut.

## Checklist (all must be yes)

- [ ] First three words name something familiar
- [ ] At least three familiar → twist facts, escalating
- [ ] A dark or impossible turn, read slower
- [ ] One question the whole film answers, matching title and thumbnail
- [ ] A concrete promise image, no "stay to the end"
- [ ] ≤ 120 words, body starts by ~40 s
- [ ] Every fact sourced in the README table
- [ ] Gemini TTS: only supported tags, angle brackets, numbers spelled out
