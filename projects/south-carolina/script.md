## Hook

The first golf club in America? <short pause> Charleston, South Carolina. And a South Carolina town calls itself the birthplace of sweet tea.

The first shots of the Civil War were fired in Charleston Harbour.

<long pause> Before the Civil War, most of the people living in South Carolina were enslaved. More than half.

And Charleston was one of the richest cities in America. Built on a grain of rice.

<short pause> So how does one small state become one of the richest colonies in America... and then start the deadliest war in American history?

<whispers> It starts with a handful of seed rice from the other side of the world.

## Part 1: First Peoples

South Carolina rises from flat salt marshes and sea islands, through pine forests, to the Blue Ridge Mountains.

For thousands of years, it's home to many nations. The Catawba, the Cherokee, the Yamasee, the Cusabo, and others. On the coast, people pile up oyster shells into giant rings.

In fifteen sixty-six, the Spanish build a town called Santa Elena on what is now Parris Island. It lasts about twenty years.

## Part 1B

In sixteen seventy, English settlers arrive. Many come from Barbados, a small Caribbean island where planters have grown rich on sugar worked by enslaved Africans.

They found Charles Town, named after King Charles the Second. Today, Charleston.

And they bring the Barbados way of doing things with them.

## Part 2: Carolina Gold

At first, the colony struggles. Then, around sixteen eighty-five, as the story goes, a ship's captain blown off course from Madagascar gives a Charleston settler a small bag of seed rice.

The rice grows beautifully in the warm, wet lowlands. It's so profitable that people call it "Carolina Gold".

But growing rice in swamps is brutal, dangerous, skilled work. Clearing cypress swamps. Building banks and floodgates. Flooding and draining the fields at exactly the right time.

## Part 2B

And the planters know who has that skill.

People from the rice-growing coast of West Africa. Today's Senegal, Gambia, Sierra Leone.

So the planters buy enslaved Africans by the tens of thousands. Many experts believe that African knowledge is what made Carolina rice work.

Charleston becomes the busiest port for enslaved people in North America. Historians estimate that around four in ten enslaved Africans brought into what became the United States came through Charleston.

By the seventeen-hundreds, enslaved Africans outnumber white settlers in the colony.

## Part 2C

On the sea islands, isolated from the mainland, enslaved people keep much of their African culture. Their language, their food, their stories, their basket weaving.

It becomes the Gullah Geechee culture. It's still alive today.

## Part 2D (quiet style)

In seventeen thirty-nine, near the Stono River, about twenty enslaved men rise up. They march south, toward Spanish Florida, which promises freedom. More people join them.

The militia catches them. Dozens are killed.

It's the largest slave revolt in the British colonies. Afterward, the colony passes even harsher laws.

## Part 2E

In the seventeen-forties, a teenager named Eliza Lucas, left in charge of her father's plantations, experiments with indigo, a plant that makes blue dye. It works. Indigo joins rice.

By the time of the Revolution, the Charleston area's planters are some of the richest people in British America.

## Part 3: Palmetto State

In June seventeen seventy-six, British warships attack a little fort on Sullivan's Island, made of sand and soft palmetto logs.

The cannonballs sink into the spongy palmetto wood, and don't shatter it. The fort holds. The British sail away.

That's why there's a palmetto tree on South Carolina's flag.

Later, a soldier named Francis Marion hides in the swamps and ambushes the British. They call him the "Swamp Fox".

In seventeen eighty-eight, South Carolina becomes the eighth state.

## Part 3B

And in seventeen eighty-six, a group of Charleston merchants form the South Carolina Golf Club. The first golf club in America.

## Part 4: Secession

But everything in South Carolina rests on slavery. And as the North grows against it, South Carolina's planters grow afraid.

Their leader is John C. Calhoun. He argues a state can cancel federal laws it doesn't like. In eighteen thirty-two, South Carolina tries it, over a tax on imports. President Andrew Jackson, born right on the Carolina border himself, threatens to send in the army. South Carolina backs down. For now.

## Part 4B

By eighteen sixty, more than half the people in South Carolina are enslaved.

In November, Abraham Lincoln is elected president. He wants to stop slavery from spreading west.

Six weeks later, on December twentieth, eighteen sixty, South Carolina becomes the first state to leave the Union. Its declaration says it plainly. It is leaving to protect slavery.

## Part 4C

In Charleston Harbour, there's a federal fort, still flying the American flag. Fort Sumter.

At four-thirty in the morning, on April twelfth, eighteen sixty-one, Confederate cannons open fire on it.

The fort surrenders the next day. Amazingly, nobody is killed by the bombardment.

<short pause> But the Civil War has begun. It will kill more Americans than any other war.

## Part 4D

In May eighteen sixty-two, an enslaved ship's pilot named Robert Smalls waits until the white officers go ashore for the night.

Then he takes the Confederate ship Planter, picks up his family and others, and sails right past the Confederate forts, giving the correct signals. He hands the ship to the U.S. Navy.

<chuckle> He later becomes a congressman. And buys the house of the man who once enslaved him.

## Part 4E (quiet style)

In eighteen sixty-four, a Confederate submarine, the Hunley, sinks a Union warship off Charleston. The first submarine ever to sink a ship in battle. It sinks too. All eight men aboard die.

In February eighteen sixty-five, much of the capital, Columbia, burns as Sherman's army passes through.

## Part 5: Reconstruction

After the war, South Carolina becomes the first state with a legislature where most members are Black. Formerly enslaved men help write a new constitution with public schools for everyone.

It doesn't last. By the eighteen-nineties, Black South Carolinians are pushed out of voting, and Jim Crow laws take hold.

## Part 5B

But one of the cases that ends school segregation starts here. In Clarendon County, Black parents ask for a school bus for their children. They're refused. Their lawsuit, Briggs versus Elliott, becomes part of Brown versus Board of Education.

## Part 5C (quiet style)

In nineteen sixty-eight, in Orangeburg, state police fire on students protesting a segregated bowling alley. Three young men are killed.

## Part 5D (quiet style)

In June two thousand fifteen, a gunman joins a Bible study at Emanuel AME Church in Charleston. Then he opens fire. Nine Black worshippers are killed.

Weeks later, South Carolina removes the Confederate battle flag from its State House grounds.

## Part 6: Today

Today, South Carolina builds BMWs in Spartanburg, at the company's biggest factory in the world. Boeing builds jets near Charleston. And millions of tourists come to Myrtle Beach and Charleston's old streets.

## Part 6B

So how does one small state become one of the richest colonies in America... and then start the deadliest war in American history?

It starts with a handful of seed rice from the other side of the world.

Rice made the planters rich. To grow it, they enslaved tens of thousands of skilled Africans, until the enslaved were the majority. The planters built everything on slavery. And when they feared losing it, they left the Union and fired the first shots.

<short pause> The same rice fields also gave America the Gullah Geechee, Robert Smalls, and the families who helped end segregation.

<chuckle> And, somehow, America's very first golf club.
