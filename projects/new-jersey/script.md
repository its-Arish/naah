## Hook

The first practical light bulb? <short pause> New Jersey. The first drive-in movie theatre? New Jersey. The streets in Monopoly? Named after Atlantic City.

In New Jersey, it's illegal to pump your own gas.

And the first movie studio in America wasn't in Hollywood. It was in New Jersey.

<long pause> Alexander Hamilton was shot in New Jersey. And the giant airship Hindenburg exploded in flames over it.

<short pause> So how does a small state, squeezed between New York and Philadelphia, end up inventing the modern world?

<whispers> It starts with a waterfall Alexander Hamilton wanted to put to work.

## Part 1: Lenapehoking

For thousands of years, this land is Lenapehoking, home of the Lenape. They fish the rivers and the shore, farm corn, and gather oysters in such numbers that their shell piles become small hills.

In sixteen-oh-nine, Henry Hudson sails past. The Dutch claim the land as part of New Netherland.

In sixteen sixty-four, the English take it over. The new owners name it after the island of Jersey in the English Channel, where one of them, George Carteret, came from.

New Jersey.

## Part 1B

From the very start, New Jersey is in the middle. New York on one side. Philadelphia on the other.

Benjamin Franklin supposedly called it "a barrel tapped at both ends". <chuckle> Everyone drinks from it, and passes through it, on the way somewhere else.

## Part 2: Crossroads

And that's exactly what happens in the Revolution. New Jersey is right in the way.

More battles and skirmishes happen here than in almost any other state. People call it the "Crossroads of the Revolution".

In December seventeen seventy-six, the American army is losing badly. Then, on Christmas night, George Washington crosses the icy Delaware River and surprises the enemy at Trenton, New Jersey. A week later, he wins again at Princeton.

The Revolution is saved.

## Part 2B

For a few months in seventeen eighty-three, the government of the United States meets in Princeton. Then briefly in Trenton. New Jersey was, for a moment, the capital of America.

In December seventeen eighty-seven, it becomes the third state.

## Part 3: Mill Towns

Now, the waterfall.

Alexander Hamilton, America's first Treasury Secretary, has a big idea. America shouldn't just farm. It should make things. Factories, machines, industry.

In seventeen ninety-one, he and his partners choose the spot. The Great Falls of the Passaic River. A seventy-seven-foot waterfall, roaring with power.

They plan a whole city around it. Canals carry the water to mills. They name it after the governor. Paterson.

It's America's first planned industrial city.

## Part 3B (quiet style)

Hamilton doesn't live to see it grow.

On July eleventh, eighteen-oh-four, at Weehawken, New Jersey, he fights a duel with the Vice President, Aaron Burr.

Burr shoots him. Hamilton dies the next day.

## Part 3C

But Paterson works. Its mills spin cotton, build locomotives, and later weave so much silk it's called "Silk City".

And all across New Jersey, factories grow along the rivers and railroads, between the two big cities. Newark makes leather, beer and jewellery. Trenton makes pottery and steel cable. Camden builds ships.

In eighteen forty-six, in Hoboken, two teams play one of the first organised baseball games under written rules.

## Part 4: Invention Factory

In eighteen seventy-six, a young inventor named Thomas Edison moves to a quiet village called Menlo Park, New Jersey.

He builds something nobody has built before. Not just an invention. A factory for inventions. A laboratory, with machinists, chemists and engineers, all working on new ideas.

He promises a small invention every ten days, and a big one every six months.

In eighteen seventy-seven, he records his own voice on a cylinder wrapped in tin foil, reciting "Mary Had a Little Lamb". The phonograph. People call him "the Wizard of Menlo Park".

Two years later, after testing thousands of materials, he makes an electric light bulb that burns for hours. And he lights up Menlo Park.

## Part 4B

Then Edison moves to a bigger lab in West Orange. There, his team builds one of the first movie cameras. And in eighteen ninety-three, they build the first movie studio in the world. A black tar-paper shack that rotates to follow the sun. They call it the Black Maria.

For years, Fort Lee, New Jersey, is the movie capital of America. Then filmmakers move to California, for the sunshine. <chuckle> And to get away from Edison's lawyers.

## Part 5: Shore and Sky

Meanwhile, on the coast, Atlantic City builds the first boardwalk in America, in eighteen seventy, so people stop tracking sand into the hotels.

It becomes America's playground. Saltwater taffy. The Miss America pageant. And in the nineteen-thirties, a board game uses Atlantic City's street names. Boardwalk. Park Place. Monopoly.

In nineteen thirty-three, in Camden, a man named Richard Hollingshead opens the first drive-in movie theatre.

## Part 5B (quiet style)

On May sixth, nineteen thirty-seven, the giant German airship Hindenburg arrives at Lakehurst, New Jersey.

As it comes in to land, it bursts into flames. In about half a minute, it's gone.

Thirty-six people are killed. A radio reporter cries on the air, "Oh, the humanity!"

## Part 5C

A year later, on Halloween eve, a radio play called "The War of the Worlds" tells listeners that Martians have landed in Grover's Mill, New Jersey.

<chuckle> Some listeners believe it. Newspapers make the panic sound much bigger than it really was. But for one night, New Jersey was being invaded by aliens.

## Part 6: Labs

After the war, New Jersey keeps inventing.

At Bell Labs, in Murray Hill, scientists invent the transistor in nineteen forty-seven. The tiny switch inside every computer and phone today.

In nineteen sixty-four, two Bell Labs scientists in Holmdel hear a strange hiss in their giant antenna. They think it's pigeon droppings. They clean it out. The hiss stays.

It turns out to be the leftover heat of the Big Bang. They win the Nobel Prize.

And in Princeton, Albert Einstein spends the last twenty-two years of his life.

## Part 6B (quiet style)

In July nineteen sixty-seven, after police beat a Black taxi driver in Newark, the city erupts. Twenty-six people die.

## Part 6C

New Jersey also gives America the New Jersey Turnpike, more diners than almost anywhere, and Bruce Springsteen.

Today, it's the most densely populated state in the country. And it's still a giant centre for medicine, science and chemistry.

<chuckle> And you still can't pump your own gas. It's been the law since nineteen forty-nine.

## Part 6D

So how does a small state, squeezed between New York and Philadelphia, end up inventing the modern world?

It starts with a waterfall Alexander Hamilton wanted to put to work.

Hamilton made Paterson the first planned factory city. New Jersey filled with mills, machines and skilled workers, right between two of the biggest markets in America. So when Edison wanted to build a factory for inventions, he built it here. And the labs that followed him built the electronic age.

<short pause> New Jersey was always the place people passed through.

<chuckle> It just turns out a lot of the future passed through it first.
