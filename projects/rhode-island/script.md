## Hook

Rhode Island is the smallest state. <short pause> But until twenty twenty, it had the longest official name. "The State of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations".

And most of Rhode Island isn't even on an island.

Mr. Potato Head? Made by a Rhode Island toy company.

It was the first colony to declare independence from Britain. And the very last to join the United States.

<long pause> And Rhode Island merchants ran most of America's slave-trading voyages.

<short pause> So how does a colony founded on freedom become the heart of America's slave trade?

<whispers> It starts with a man thrown out of Massachusetts, in the middle of winter.

## Part 1: First Peoples

Rhode Island is wrapped around a big, island-dotted bay. Narragansett Bay.

It's the land of the Narragansett, one of the most powerful nations in New England, along with the Wampanoag, the Niantic and others.

In fifteen twenty-four, the Italian explorer Giovanni da Verrazzano sails past and describes an island about the size of the Greek island of Rhodes. Later, the Dutch call one island "Roodt Eylandt", the red island. Either way, the name sticks.

## Part 2: Liberty of Conscience

Now, the man.

Roger Williams is a young Puritan minister in Massachusetts. And he says things the leaders really don't like.

That the government shouldn't punish people for their religion. That church and state should be separate. And that the king had no right to give away Native land without buying it.

In sixteen thirty-five, Massachusetts banishes him. They plan to ship him back to England.

So in January sixteen thirty-six, in deep snow, he slips away into the forest. He spends weeks with the Narragansett and Wampanoag, who know him and shelter him.

## Part 2B

In the spring, he buys land from the Narragansett leaders, at the head of the bay. He names his settlement after God's care for him. Providence.

And in Providence, he makes a rule that's almost unheard of in the world. Anyone can worship however they want. Or not at all. The government stays out of it.

He calls it "liberty of conscience".

## Part 2C

People who don't fit anywhere else come to Rhode Island. Anne Hutchinson, banished from Massachusetts for preaching. Baptists. Quakers. And Jewish families, who build a synagogue in Newport. The Touro Synagogue. Today it's the oldest synagogue building in America.

Years later, George Washington writes to that congregation. America, he says, gives "to bigotry no sanction, to persecution no assistance".

<chuckle> Neighbouring colonies call it "Rogue's Island". A dumping ground for troublemakers. Rhode Island takes it as a compliment.

## Part 2D (quiet style)

In sixteen seventy-five, King Philip's War spreads into Rhode Island. In December, colonial soldiers attack a Narragansett fort in the Great Swamp. Hundreds of Narragansett, many women and children, are killed.

## Part 3: Rum and Ships

Rhode Island has thin, rocky soil. But a great harbour at Newport.

So Rhode Islanders turn to the sea. And to trade. And in a colony where the government doesn't tell people what to do... merchants do whatever makes money.

## Part 3B (quiet style)

Rhode Island distilleries turn molasses from the Caribbean into rum. Ships carry the rum to West Africa. There, it's traded for captive African people.

Those people are carried across the Atlantic, in chains, and sold in the Caribbean and the American South. Then the ships bring back molasses. And the circle starts again.

Rhode Island merchants make around a thousand of these voyages. Most of all the slave-trading voyages that leave from what becomes the United States. More than a hundred thousand Africans are carried on them.

Families like the Browns of Providence and the DeWolfs of Bristol grow rich.

Rhode Island even passed a law against slavery, back in sixteen fifty-two. Nobody enforced it.

## Part 4: Rogue Island

And Rhode Islanders really don't like being told what to do by Britain either.

In seventeen seventy-two, a British customs ship, the Gaspee, chasing smugglers, runs aground near Providence. That night, Rhode Islanders row out, shoot its captain, and burn the ship.

On May fourth, seventeen seventy-six, two months before the Declaration of Independence, Rhode Island becomes the first colony to formally renounce its loyalty to the king.

## Part 4B

But after the war, Rhode Island doesn't trust a strong national government either. It refuses to even send anyone to the Constitutional Convention.

When the other states form the United States, Rhode Island stays out. For more than a year, it's basically its own country.

<chuckle> Finally, the United States threatens to tax its goods like a foreign country. In May seventeen-ninety, Rhode Island joins. The last of the original thirteen. By two votes.

## Part 5: Mills

In seventeen ninety-three, a young Englishman named Samuel Slater arrives. Britain has banned anyone from taking its textile machine designs out of the country. So Slater has memorised them.

In Pawtucket, Rhode Island, he builds a water-powered cotton mill. It's considered the beginning of the factory age in America.

<chuckle> In England, they call him "Slater the Traitor".

Soon, mill villages line Rhode Island's rivers. They spin cotton picked by enslaved people in the South.

## Part 5B

In the Gilded Age, America's richest families build giant summer mansions in Newport. They call them "cottages". One, The Breakers, has seventy rooms.

Newport hosts the first national tennis championships in eighteen eighty-one. And for decades, the America's Cup sailing races.

## Part 5C

In nineteen fifty-two, a Rhode Island toy company called Hasbro starts selling a set of plastic face parts you stick into a real potato. Mr. Potato Head. It's one of the first toys ever advertised on television.

And Rhode Islanders drink something nobody else does. Coffee milk. It's the official state drink.

## Part 5D (quiet style)

In February two thousand three, during a rock concert at the Station nightclub in West Warwick, fireworks set the walls on fire.

One hundred people are killed.

## Part 5E

In two thousand six, Brown University, named after the Brown family, publishes a report on its own connection to the slave trade.

And in twenty twenty, Rhode Island's voters remove the words "Providence Plantations" from the state's official name, because of what the word "plantations" brings to mind.

## Part 5F

So how does a colony founded on freedom become the heart of America's slave trade?

It starts with a man thrown out of Massachusetts, in the middle of winter.

Roger Williams built a place where no government would tell anyone what to believe. That freedom drew rebels, thinkers and outcasts. But it also meant almost no one told the merchants what they couldn't do. And the merchants chose rum, ships and human beings.

<short pause> The smallest state holds both of America's stories. The freedom, and the chains.

<chuckle> And a potato with a moustache.
