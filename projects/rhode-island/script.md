# The Entire History of Rhode Island

Narration script, written to be voiced with **Gemini TTS** and then animated. About 2,300 spoken words, so roughly 12 to 13 minutes when recorded (California ran 2,600 words to 13:50).

How to use it (the full rules are in `docs/PLAYBOOK.md` and `docs/HOOKS.md`):

- Each `##` heading is **one Gemini generation**. Paste only the lines under it, never the heading. Headings are paste breaks: never spoken, never shown. The film has no chapter cards.
- Paste the matching style line (table below) into Gemini's style box. Parts marked "quiet style" use the quiet line.
- Tags are angle brackets and only from Gemini's supported list: `<short pause>` `<long pause>` `<breath>` `<sigh>` `<exhales>` `<gasp>` `<chuckle>` `<laugh>` `<giggle>` `<phew>` `<tsk>` `<groan>` `<whispers>`. Never two tags side by side. CAPITALS stress a word; three dots add a hesitation; years and numbers are spelled out.

| Part | Style line for the style box |
| --- | --- |
| Hook | Quick, amused for the first four facts; slower and serious from "And Rhode Island merchants"; leaning in, almost whispering, on the last line. |

## Hook

Rhode Island is the smallest state. <short pause> But until twenty twenty, it had the longest official name. "The State of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations".

<chuckle> And most of Rhode Island isn't even on an island. Mr. Potato Head? Made by a Rhode Island toy company.

It was the first colony to declare independence from Britain. And the very last to join the United States.

<long pause> And Rhode Island merchants ran most of America's slave-trading voyages.

<short pause> So how does a colony founded on freedom become the heart of America's slave trade?

<whispers> It starts with a man thrown out of Massachusetts, in the middle of winter.

## Part 1: First Peoples

Rhode Island is wrapped around a big, island-dotted bay. Narragansett Bay. <chuckle> The whole state is so small you can drive across it in under an hour. But it has about four hundred miles of coastline.

It's the land of the Narragansett, one of the most powerful nations in New England, along with the Wampanoag, the Niantic and others. <short pause> They grow corn, catch fish and dig clams. They make wampum from quahog shells, and Narragansett wampum is prized across the region.

In fifteen twenty-four, the Italian explorer Giovanni da Verrazzano sails past and describes an island about the size of the Greek island of Rhodes. Later, the Dutch call one island "Roodt Eylandt", the red island. <chuckle> Nobody's completely sure which explanation is right. Either way, the name sticks.

<whispers> In a park in Newport stands a mysterious round stone tower, on eight stone arches. <chuckle> For two hundred years, people have claimed it was built by Vikings, or by lost Portuguese sailors, or by Knights Templar. <short pause> Scientists who tested its mortar say it's from the sixteen-hundreds. Most likely, it's the windmill of a colonial governor.

## Part 2: Liberty of Conscience

Now, the man.

Roger Williams is a young Puritan minister in Massachusetts. <tsk> And he says things the leaders really don't like.

That the government shouldn't punish people for their religion. That church and state should be separate, with a wall between them. And that the king had no right to give away Native land without buying it.

In sixteen thirty-five, Massachusetts banishes him. They plan to ship him back to England.

<whispers> So in January sixteen thirty-six, in deep snow, he slips away into the forest. He spends weeks with the Narragansett and Wampanoag, who know him and shelter him. <short pause> He had learned their language. Later, he writes one of the first English guides to a Native American language.

<sigh> Years later, he remembers that winter. For fourteen weeks, he writes, he didn't know what bread or bed meant.

## Part 2B

In the spring, he buys land from the Narragansett leaders, at the head of the bay. He names his settlement after God's care for him. Providence.

<gasp> And in Providence, he makes a rule that's almost unheard of in the world. Anyone can worship however they want. Or not at all. The government stays out of it.

He calls it "liberty of conscience".

In sixteen sixty-three, the king grants Rhode Island a charter that promises exactly that. <short pause> A "lively experiment". The first government in the world built on religious freedom.

## Part 2C

People who don't fit anywhere else come to Rhode Island. Anne Hutchinson, banished from Massachusetts for preaching. Baptists, who build the first Baptist church in America. Quakers. <short pause> And Jewish families, who build a synagogue in Newport. The Touro Synagogue. Today it's the oldest synagogue building in America.

Years later, George Washington writes to that congregation. America, he says, gives "to bigotry no sanction, to persecution no assistance".

<chuckle> Neighbouring colonies call it "Rogue's Island". A dumping ground for troublemakers. <laugh> Rhode Island takes it as a compliment.

<sigh> Anne Hutchinson later moves on to the Dutch colony near New York. There, in sixteen forty-three, she and most of her children are killed in a war between the Dutch and the local Native people.

## Part 2D (quiet style)

Mary Dyer, a Quaker from Rhode Island, keeps going back to Boston to preach, even though Quakers are banned there on pain of death.

<breath> In sixteen sixty, Massachusetts hangs her on Boston Common.

## Part 2E (quiet style)

In sixteen seventy-five, King Philip's War spreads into Rhode Island. The Narragansett try to stay out of it.

<breath> In December, colonial soldiers from the other colonies march through the snow, and attack a Narragansett fort in the Great Swamp.

<long pause> Hundreds of Narragansett, many women and children, are killed. Their homes and winter food are burned.

## Part 3: Rum and Ships

Rhode Island has thin, rocky soil. But a great harbour at Newport.

So Rhode Islanders turn to the sea. And to trade. <short pause> And in a colony where the government doesn't tell people what to do... merchants do whatever makes money.

<chuckle> Newport becomes one of the richest towns in the colonies. Smugglers dodge British taxes all the time.

<short pause> Newport's cabinet makers, the Townsend and Goddard families, build some of the finest furniture in colonial America. <gasp> In nineteen eighty-nine, one of their mahogany desks sells at auction for more than twelve million dollars.

## Part 3B (quiet style)

Rhode Island distilleries turn molasses from the Caribbean into rum. Ships carry the rum to West Africa. There, it's traded for captive African people.

<breath> Those people are carried across the Atlantic, in chains, and sold in the Caribbean and the American South. Then the ships bring back molasses. And the circle starts again.

<long pause> Rhode Island merchants make around a thousand of these voyages. Most of all the slave-trading voyages that leave from what becomes the United States. More than a hundred thousand Africans are carried on them.

Families like the Browns of Providence and the DeWolfs of Bristol grow rich.

<breath> Rhode Island had even passed a law against slavery, back in sixteen fifty-two. Nobody enforced it.

## Part 3C

Even one family is split down the middle. <short pause> Two Brown brothers, John and Moses.

John Brown is a slave trader. He's put on trial for breaking America's new law against the trade. <tsk> And he still defends it.

His brother Moses frees the people he enslaved, becomes a Quaker, and fights to end the slave trade. He also helps bring Samuel Slater to Rhode Island. More on him in a moment.

## Part 4: Rogue Island

And Rhode Islanders really don't like being told what to do by Britain either.

<gasp> In seventeen seventy-two, a British customs ship, the Gaspee, chasing smugglers, runs aground near Providence. That night, Rhode Islanders, led by John Brown, row out, shoot its captain, and burn the ship. <short pause> The British offer a big reward. Nobody talks.

On May fourth, seventeen seventy-six, two months before the Declaration of Independence, Rhode Island becomes the first colony to formally renounce its loyalty to the king.

## Part 4B

In the war, Rhode Island raises a regiment that includes enslaved Black men, promised their freedom if they serve. The First Rhode Island Regiment. <short pause> At the Battle of Rhode Island, in seventeen seventy-eight, they hold their ground against charge after charge.

<short pause> And a Quaker iron-maker's son from Rhode Island, Nathanael Greene, who taught himself war from books, becomes Washington's most trusted general. <chuckle> His own Quaker meeting throws him out for joining the army.

<gasp> In seventeen eighty, a French army of more than five thousand men lands at Newport, to help the Americans. A year later, they march south to Yorktown, and help win the war.

## Part 4C

But after the war, Rhode Island doesn't trust a strong national government either. <tsk> It refuses to even send anyone to the Constitutional Convention.

When the other states form the United States, Rhode Island stays out. <chuckle> For more than a year, it's basically its own country.

Finally, the United States threatens to tax its goods like a foreign country. In May seventeen-ninety, Rhode Island joins. The last of the original thirteen. <laugh> By two votes.

<short pause> Before that, in seventeen eighty-four, Rhode Island passes a law that every child born to an enslaved mother from then on will be free. <sigh> Slowly, slavery ends in the state. But Rhode Island's ships keep carrying captives for years, legally and illegally.

## Part 5: Mills

In seventeen eighty-nine, a young Englishman named Samuel Slater sails for America. Britain has banned anyone from taking its textile machine designs out of the country. <whispers> So Slater has memorised them.

In Pawtucket, Rhode Island, with Moses Brown's money, he builds a water-powered cotton mill. It's considered the beginning of the factory age in America.

<chuckle> In England, they call him "Slater the Traitor".

<sigh> Soon, mill villages line Rhode Island's rivers. They spin cotton picked by enslaved people in the South. Children as young as seven work the machines.

<chuckle> Providence also becomes the jewellery capital of America. Factories turn out rings, pins and cheap, shiny costume jewellery by the ton. And the Gorham company makes silverware for the White House.

## Part 5B

Rhode Island's old charter still only lets men who own land vote. <tsk> By the eighteen-forties, most men in the state can't vote.

So in eighteen forty-two, a lawyer named Thomas Dorr holds his own election, and becomes a rival governor. <laugh> Rhode Island has two governors at once. Dorr's followers try to capture the state arsenal. Their old cannons don't fire. The Dorr Rebellion collapses. <short pause> But soon after, Rhode Island lets far more men vote.

## Part 5C

In the Gilded Age, America's richest families build giant summer mansions in Newport. <chuckle> They call them "cottages". One, The Breakers, built for the Vanderbilts, has seventy rooms.

Newport hosts the first national tennis championships in eighteen eighty-one. And for decades, the America's Cup sailing races.

<short pause> In nineteen sixty-five, at the Newport Folk Festival, Bob Dylan plugs in an electric guitar. <groan> Some folk fans boo. Music history is made anyway.

<short pause> And in eighteen ninety-six, at a horse track in Cranston, a handful of noisy early cars race around the oval. <chuckle> One of the first car races ever held on a track in America. The winning car averages about twenty-six miles an hour.

## Part 5D (quiet style)

In September nineteen thirty-eight, a hurricane slams into Rhode Island with almost no warning.

<breath> A storm surge pushes the sea up Narragansett Bay. Downtown Providence is flooded under several feet of water. Beach houses are swept away with families inside.

<long pause> Hundreds of people in Rhode Island are killed.

## Part 5E

<gasp> Long before that, in Newport harbour, a lighthouse keeper's daughter named Ida Lewis rows out into storms, again and again, to save people from drowning. Soldiers, fishermen, even a sheep. <short pause> She's credited with saving at least eighteen lives. <chuckle> She becomes so famous that a president comes to visit her. Her lighthouse is now a yacht club, named after her.

## Part 5F

In nineteen fifty-two, a Rhode Island toy company called Hasbro starts selling a set of plastic face parts you stick into a real potato. <laugh> Mr. Potato Head. It's one of the first toys ever advertised on television. Hasbro later makes G.I. Joe, Monopoly and Transformers.

<chuckle> Rhode Islanders drink something nobody else does. Coffee milk. It's the official state drink. And on the highway in Providence, a giant blue termite, fifty-eight feet long, sits on a pest control company's roof. The Big Blue Bug.

<laugh> Rhode Island's state bird is a chicken. The Rhode Island Red, bred on farms here in the eighteen-hundreds, now laying eggs all over the world. <chuckle> Wash it down with a Del's frozen lemonade, and some clam cakes.

<short pause> And on summer nights in Providence, bonfires are lit on braziers in the middle of the city's rivers, with music playing across the water. WaterFire.

<short pause> The horror writer H.P. Lovecraft lives almost his whole life in Providence. His gravestone says: "I am Providence".

## Part 5G (quiet style)

In February two thousand three, during a rock concert at the Station nightclub in West Warwick, fireworks set the soundproofing foam on the walls on fire.

<long pause> One hundred people are killed.

## Part 5H

In two thousand six, Brown University, named after the Brown family, publishes a report on its own connection to the slave trade.

Brown was founded in seventeen sixty-four. Some of its first buildings were raised with help from enslaved workers.

<short pause> And in twenty twenty, Rhode Island's voters remove the words "Providence Plantations" from the state's official name, because of what the word "plantations" brings to mind.

<short pause> In nineteen eighty-three, after centuries of being told they no longer existed as a nation, the Narragansett win official recognition from the federal government.

## Part 5I

So how does a colony founded on freedom become the heart of America's slave trade?

<whispers> It starts with a man thrown out of Massachusetts, in the middle of winter.

Roger Williams built a place where no government would tell anyone what to believe. That freedom drew rebels, thinkers and outcasts. <short pause> But it also meant almost no one told the merchants what they couldn't do. And the merchants chose rum, ships and human beings.

<breath> The smallest state holds both of America's stories. The freedom, and the chains.

<chuckle> And a potato with a moustache.
