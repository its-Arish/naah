## Hook

New Hampshire's motto is "Live Free or Die". <short pause> It's on every licence plate. And the licence plates are made by prisoners.

New Hampshire has four hundred state representatives. One of the biggest legislatures in the English-speaking world. They're paid a hundred dollars a year.

For decades, the fastest wind ever recorded on Earth was measured on a New Hampshire mountain. Two hundred and thirty-one miles an hour.

<long pause> In nineteen eighty-six, a New Hampshire schoolteacher, Christa McAuliffe, set out to become the first teacher in space. Seventy-three seconds after launch, the shuttle broke apart.

<short pause> So how does a small state of rocky farms end up with one of the biggest governments on Earth, and the first say in choosing a president?

<whispers> It starts with a town meeting.

## Part 1: First Peoples

New Hampshire is granite. Mountains, forests, lakes, and lots and lots of rocks. That's why it's called the Granite State.

For thousands of years, it's home to the Abenaki and the Pennacook. They fish at the falls of the Merrimack River, and hunt in the White Mountains.

## Part 2: Town Meetings

In sixteen twenty-three, English fishermen settle at Portsmouth and Dover, on the short stretch of New Hampshire coast. One of the colony's founders, John Mason, names it after his home county in England. Hampshire.

The settlers live in small, separate towns. Each one runs itself.

Once a year, every man in town gathers in the meeting house. They argue. They vote. On roads, schools, taxes, and whose pigs are running loose.

The town meeting. No king, no governor, just neighbours deciding together.

<chuckle> Many New Hampshire towns still do it today. Every March.

## Part 2B

For a while, New Hampshire is ruled by Massachusetts. In sixteen seventy-nine, it becomes its own royal colony.

## Part 3: Live Free or Die

In December seventeen seventy-four, months before Lexington and Concord, New Hampshire men raid a British fort near Portsmouth, Fort William and Mary, and carry away its gunpowder. One of the very first acts of rebellion.

In January seventeen seventy-six, New Hampshire becomes the first colony to set up its own government, completely independent of Britain.

## Part 3B

One of its heroes is a tough frontier soldier named John Stark. He fights at Bunker Hill. And in seventeen seventy-seven, he wins the Battle of Bennington.

Years later, old General Stark is invited to an anniversary celebration. He's too sick to go. So he sends a toast to be read out.

"Live free or die. Death is not the worst of evils."

<short pause> In nineteen forty-five, New Hampshire makes it the state motto.

## Part 3C

In seventeen eighty-eight, the new U.S. Constitution needs nine states to approve it before it can take effect. Eight have said yes.

On June twenty-first, New Hampshire votes yes. The ninth state. The one that makes the Constitution real.

## Part 3D

And when New Hampshire writes its own government, every town wants its own voice in the state House of Representatives. Even the smallest.

So the House just keeps growing. Today it has four hundred members. For a state of about one point four million people. That's roughly one lawmaker for every three and a half thousand people.

And they get paid a hundred dollars a year. <chuckle> Not a hundred dollars a day. A year.

## Part 4: Mills and Mountains

In the eighteen hundreds, New Hampshire's rivers power giant mills. In Manchester, the Amoskeag mills grow into one of the biggest cotton-textile factories in the world.

Franklin Pierce, from New Hampshire, becomes president in eighteen fifty-three. Daniel Webster, born in New Hampshire, becomes one of the greatest speakers in American history.

## Part 4B

In the White Mountains, high on a cliff, there's a rock formation that looks like the profile of an old man's face. The Old Man of the Mountain. It becomes the state's symbol.

And on top of Mount Washington, the highest peak in the Northeast, weather can be the worst in the world.

On April twelfth, nineteen thirty-four, scientists at the summit observatory measure a gust of two hundred and thirty-one miles an hour. For sixty-two years, it's the fastest wind ever recorded on Earth.

<chuckle> They had to tie the building to the mountain with chains.

## Part 4C

In nineteen forty-four, leaders from forty-four countries meet at a grand old hotel in Bretton Woods, New Hampshire. They plan how the world's money will work after the war. They create the International Monetary Fund and the World Bank.

## Part 5: Granite State

In nineteen sixty-one, a Navy pilot from Derry, New Hampshire, Alan Shepard, becomes the first American in space.

## Part 5B (quiet style)

In nineteen eighty-five, NASA chooses a social studies teacher from Concord, New Hampshire, to be the first teacher in space. Christa McAuliffe. She plans to teach lessons from orbit.

On January twenty-eighth, nineteen eighty-six, millions of students watch the launch of the space shuttle Challenger.

Seventy-three seconds after liftoff, it breaks apart. All seven astronauts are killed.

## Part 5C (quiet style)

In May two thousand three, the Old Man of the Mountain, the face that had watched over the valley for thousands of years, collapses into rubble.

It's still on New Hampshire's road signs. And its quarter.

## Part 6: First Primary

Now, the town meeting, again.

In nineteen-sixteen, New Hampshire holds its first presidential primary, so voters can choose the parties' candidates. To save money, it's held on the same day as the March town meetings. By nineteen-twenty, that makes it the first presidential primary in the country.

And New Hampshire loves being first. So much that it passes a law. The primary must be held before any other similar contest. If another state moves earlier... New Hampshire moves earlier too.

## Part 6B

So candidates come to New Hampshire. And they have to do things the New Hampshire way. Small diners. Living rooms. Town halls. Answering hard questions from people who are used to arguing at town meetings.

In the tiny village of Dixville Notch, everyone votes at the stroke of midnight, and the results are announced minutes later.

## Part 6C

So how does a small state of rocky farms end up with one of the biggest governments on Earth, and the first say in choosing a president?

It starts with a town meeting.

For four hundred years, New Hampshire's towns have governed themselves, face to face. Every town wanted its own voice in the State House, so the House grew to four hundred. And when America started choosing presidents with primaries, New Hampshire made sure its town-meeting voters got to ask first.

<short pause> Live free or die. And, apparently, vote first.

<chuckle> Even at midnight.
