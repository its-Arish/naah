## Hook

The Ouija board? <short pause> Patented in Baltimore.

Washington, D.C., was carved out of land Maryland gave up.

And until twenty twenty-one, Maryland's own state song called Abraham Lincoln a despot.

<long pause> The bloodiest single day in American history happened in Maryland. At Antietam, about twenty-three thousand soldiers were killed, wounded or went missing. In one day.

And America's national anthem was written by a man stuck on a ship, watching his own country get bombed. Set to the tune of a British drinking song.

<short pause> So how does one night's battle over one Maryland city give the whole country its anthem?

<whispers> It starts with a flag sewn so big, the enemy couldn't miss it.

## Part 1: First Peoples

Maryland wraps around the Chesapeake Bay, the biggest estuary in the United States. Fresh river water meets salt sea water. It's full of oysters, fish and blue crabs.

For thousands of years, the Piscataway, the Nanticoke, the Susquehannock and other nations fish, farm and trade around the bay.

In sixteen-oh-eight, Captain John Smith of Jamestown explores the Chesapeake and makes a map of it.

## Part 2: Lord Baltimore

In England, Catholics are persecuted. So an English Catholic nobleman, George Calvert, Lord Baltimore, asks the king for a colony where Catholics can live in peace.

His son, Cecil, gets it. They name it after the queen, Henrietta Maria. Maryland.

In sixteen thirty-four, two little ships, the Ark and the Dove, land the first settlers. Catholics and Protestants together.

In sixteen forty-nine, Maryland passes the Toleration Act. Christians of different churches must not be harassed for their faith. It's one of the first laws of religious toleration in the American colonies.

## Part 2B

But Maryland grows rich the same way as Virginia. Tobacco. Worked by indentured servants, then by enslaved Africans.

## Part 2C

And Maryland has a long fight with its neighbour Pennsylvania about where the border is.

So in the seventeen-sixties, two English surveyors, Charles Mason and Jeremiah Dixon, spend years marking it out across forests and mountains with stone markers.

The Mason-Dixon Line.

<chuckle> They were just settling a property dispute. But later, their line becomes the famous border between the free North and the slave South.

## Part 3: Revolution

In seventeen eighty-three, Congress meets in Annapolis, Maryland, for a while. There, George Washington hands back his commission as commander of the army, and goes home. A general giving up his power. The world is amazed.

In seventeen eighty-eight, Maryland becomes the seventh state.

And in seventeen ninety, Maryland and Virginia give up land along the Potomac River for a new capital city. Washington, D.C.

## Part 3B

Now, the flag.

In the War of eighteen twelve, Britain strikes back at America. In August eighteen fourteen, British troops march into Washington and burn the White House and the Capitol.

Next target: Baltimore. A rich port full of privateers who've been raiding British ships.

Guarding Baltimore's harbour is a star-shaped fort. Fort McHenry.

Its commander, Major George Armistead, wants a flag so big that the British will have no trouble seeing it from far away.

So a Baltimore flag maker, Mary Pickersgill, with her daughter and others, sews one. Thirty feet by forty-two feet. Each star is about two feet across.

## Part 3C

On September thirteenth, eighteen fourteen, British warships start bombarding Fort McHenry. Rockets. Giant exploding shells. For twenty-five hours.

A Maryland lawyer named Francis Scott Key is on a ship with the British fleet. He'd come to win the release of a doctor friend they'd captured. Now the British won't let him leave until the attack is over.

All night, he watches the rockets and bombs over the fort. In the dark, he can't tell who's winning.

At dawn, he looks across the water. And the giant flag is still flying.

Baltimore has held.

## Part 3D

Key writes a poem on the back of a letter. "O say can you see, by the dawn's early light..."

It's printed in Baltimore days later, and sung to the tune of a popular British song from a gentlemen's club in London. A drinking song.

<chuckle> It's catchy. And it's very hard to sing.

In nineteen thirty-one, Congress makes it the national anthem. "The Star-Spangled Banner".

## Part 4: Railroads and Freedom

In eighteen twenty-seven, Baltimore starts the Baltimore and Ohio. One of America's first railroads for passengers and freight.

In eighteen thirty, its little steam engine, Tom Thumb, races a horse-drawn car. The engine is winning... until a belt slips. <chuckle> The horse wins.

## Part 4B

Maryland is a slave state. And two of the greatest freedom fighters in American history are born enslaved in Maryland.

In eighteen thirty-eight, a young man in Baltimore borrows a free Black sailor's papers, dresses as a sailor, and boards a train north. He becomes Frederick Douglass.

In eighteen forty-nine, a young woman on the Eastern Shore escapes to Pennsylvania. Then she comes back. Again and again. About thirteen trips, guiding around seventy people to freedom.

Harriet Tubman.

## Part 5: Civil War

When the war comes, Maryland is split. And it surrounds Washington, D.C. on three sides. If Maryland joins the Confederacy, the capital is cut off.

In April eighteen sixty-one, a pro-Southern mob in Baltimore attacks Union soldiers. Some of the first deaths of the war. Lincoln sends troops and arrests suspected secessionists to hold Maryland in the Union.

A Maryland poet writes "Maryland, My Maryland", calling Lincoln a despot and urging Maryland to fight him. Decades later, it becomes the state song. And stays the state song... until twenty twenty-one.

## Part 5B (quiet style)

On September seventeenth, eighteen sixty-two, near the little town of Sharpsburg, along Antietam Creek, the Union and Confederate armies collide.

In about twelve hours, roughly twenty-three thousand soldiers are killed, wounded or missing.

It's the bloodiest single day in American history.

Lee's army retreats. Five days later, Lincoln announces the Emancipation Proclamation.

## Part 5C

In eighteen sixty-four, Maryland ends slavery with its own new constitution.

## Part 6: Today

In eighteen ninety-one, a Baltimore company patents a "talking board". The Ouija board.

Baltimore also gives the world Babe Ruth, Thurgood Marshall, the first Black Supreme Court justice, and the great Johns Hopkins Hospital. Edgar Allan Poe dies there, mysteriously, in eighteen forty-nine.

And Marylanders put a spice mix called Old Bay on just about everything. Especially blue crabs.

## Part 6B (quiet style)

Early on March twenty-sixth, twenty twenty-four, a giant cargo ship loses power and hits the Francis Scott Key Bridge in Baltimore's harbour. The bridge collapses into the water.

Six road workers on the bridge are killed.

## Part 6C

So how does one night's battle over one Maryland city give the whole country its anthem?

It starts with a flag sewn so big, the enemy couldn't miss it.

Major Armistead wanted the British to see it. Mary Pickersgill made it huge. So when Francis Scott Key looked across the water at dawn, after a whole night of bombs, he could still see it. And he wrote about what he saw.

<short pause> That flag still exists. It's in the Smithsonian in Washington. On land that used to be Maryland.

<chuckle> And every time a crowd tries to hit the high note on "land of the free"... you can thank Baltimore.
