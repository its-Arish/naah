## Hook

In Montana, there are more cows than people. <short pause> More than two cows for every person.

Until nineteen ninety-nine, many of Montana's highways had no daytime speed limit. Just "reasonable and prudent".

The first Tyrannosaurus rex skeleton ever found came out of Montana.

And Montana elected the first woman ever to sit in Congress. Four years before women could vote across America.

<long pause> But in the city of Butte, there's a lake so toxic that when a giant flock of geese landed on it, thousands of them died.

<short pause> So how does a lonely state with more cows than people end up wiring up all of America?

<whispers> It starts with a hill everyone thought was worn out.

## Part 1: First Peoples

Montana is huge. Mountains in the west. Endless grass plains in the east. That's why it's called Big Sky Country.

For thousands of years, the plains are full of buffalo. Millions of them.

The Blackfeet, the Crow, the Assiniboine, the Gros Ventre, the Cheyenne and the Lakota follow the herds. In the mountains live the Salish, the Kootenai and the Pend d'Oreille.

## Part 1B

Before horses, hunters drive buffalo herds toward cliffs, and over the edge. Buffalo jumps. One near Great Falls is among the largest in North America, used for hundreds of years.

Then, in the seventeen hundreds, horses arrive from the south. The plains nations become some of the greatest horse riders in the world.

## Part 2: Explorers

In eighteen-oh-five, Lewis and Clark travel up the Missouri River across Montana. It takes them almost a month to carry their boats around the Great Falls of the Missouri. Their guide and translator is a young Shoshone woman, Sacagawea, carrying her baby on her back.

Fur traders follow. Then steamboats, all the way up the Missouri to Fort Benton.

## Part 2B

And then, gold.

In eighteen sixty-two, prospectors find gold in a creek called Grasshopper Creek. The town of Bannack appears almost overnight. Then Virginia City. Then a place where four tired prospectors decide to give it one "last chance". Last Chance Gulch.

They strike it rich. The camp becomes Helena, the capital. At one point, it's said to have more millionaires for its size than almost any city in the world.

## Part 2C

The gold camps are lawless. Robbers attack stagecoaches. And in Bannack, people suspect the sheriff himself, Henry Plummer, is leading a gang.

<chuckle> So in eighteen sixty-four, a secret vigilante group hangs him. Some say on gallows he'd built himself.

## Part 3: Last Battles

The miners, ranchers and railroads push onto Native land.

In June eighteen seventy-six, near the Little Bighorn River, Lieutenant Colonel George Custer attacks a huge camp of Lakota, Cheyenne and Arapaho people.

Led by warriors like Crazy Horse, they defeat him completely. Custer and every soldier with him are killed.

It's the greatest victory of the Plains nations. And it's the last.

## Part 3B (quiet style)

In eighteen seventy-seven, the Nez Perce, chased by the army for more than a thousand miles, are trapped in the Bear Paw Mountains. About forty miles from safety in Canada.

Chief Joseph surrenders, saying, "From where the sun now stands, I will fight no more forever."

## Part 3C (quiet style)

And the buffalo are destroyed. Hunters shoot them by the millions, for hides, and to starve the plains nations onto reservations.

By the eighteen-eighties, the great herds are gone. The nations who depended on them face starvation.

## Part 4: Copper

Now, the hill.

In southwest Montana, there's a mining camp called Butte. The gold runs out. The silver isn't great either. Most people think Butte's hill is worn out.

But an Irish immigrant named Marcus Daly buys a small silver mine there called the Anaconda.

In eighteen eighty-two, his miners dig deeper. And they hit a vein of copper. Not a small one. One of the richest copper veins anyone has ever seen.

## Part 4B

And the timing is perfect.

Thomas Edison has just invented his electric light. America is about to put electricity into every city and every home. And electricity runs on copper wire.

Butte digs. Thousands of miners, from Ireland, Cornwall, Finland, Serbia, Italy and China, work deep underground in hot, dangerous tunnels. Butte becomes the biggest city between Minneapolis and Seattle.

For a while, Butte is one of the biggest copper producers in the world. People call it "the Richest Hill on Earth". Its copper carries power and telephone calls across America.

## Part 4C

The men who own the mines are called the Copper Kings. And they fight each other like kings.

One of them, William Clark, wants to be a United States senator. Back then, state lawmakers chose senators. So he pays them. Lots of them.

<chuckle> The Senate investigates, and he has to resign. Scandals like his help convince America to let voters choose their senators directly. The Seventeenth Amendment.

## Part 4D

In November eighteen eighty-nine, Montana becomes the forty-first state.

## Part 4E (quiet style)

In June nineteen seventeen, a fire breaks out deep in Butte's Granite Mountain mine.

Smoke and gas fill the tunnels. One hundred and sixty-eight miners die. It's the deadliest hard-rock mining disaster in American history.

## Part 5: Big Sky

In nineteen sixteen, Montana elects Jeannette Rankin to Congress. The first woman ever to serve there.

She votes against America entering the First World War. And in nineteen forty-one, she's the only member of Congress to vote against entering the Second World War. "As a woman, I can't go to war," she says, "and I refuse to send anyone else."

## Part 5B

In nineteen-oh-two, a fossil hunter named Barnum Brown digs in the badlands of eastern Montana and finds the first skeleton of Tyrannosaurus rex.

In nineteen-ten, Glacier National Park is created, with more than a hundred glaciers. Today, only a couple dozen remain.

## Part 5C

In nineteen fifty-five, the Anaconda company starts digging Butte's hill from the top. An open pit, more than a mile wide. The Berkeley Pit.

In nineteen eighty-two, the mining stops, and the pumps are switched off.

Groundwater floods into the pit. It becomes a giant lake. Bright orange-red, full of acid and heavy metals.

## Part 5D (quiet style)

In November two thousand sixteen, during a snowstorm, a huge flock of snow geese lands on the Berkeley Pit.

Thousands of them die.

Today, the water is pumped and treated, and workers scare birds away with noise and lasers.

## Part 6: Today

Montana is still big and empty. Ranches, mountains, trout rivers. Yellowstone's northern gateway. And people looking for space.

## Part 6B

So how does a lonely state with more cows than people end up wiring up all of America?

It starts with a hill everyone thought was worn out.

Under Butte's tired old hill was copper. And just as America was about to switch on the lights, Montana had the metal to carry the power. Butte's miners dug the copper that lit America's cities.

<short pause> But the hill paid a price. Its poisoned lake is still there.

<chuckle> Meanwhile, the cows are doing fine. All two and a half million of them.
