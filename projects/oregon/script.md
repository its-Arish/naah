## Hook

"You have died of dysentery." <short pause> If you ever played The Oregon Trail, you know that line.

Nike? Started in Oregon. With a waffle iron.

The city of Portland got its name from a coin toss.

And Oregon's flag is the only state flag with a different picture on each side.

<long pause> But when Oregon became a state, its constitution banned Black people from moving there.

<short pause> So why would hundreds of thousands of people walk two thousand miles, across deserts and mountains, to get to Oregon?

<whispers> It starts with a hat.

## Part 1: First Peoples

People have lived in Oregon for at least fourteen thousand years. In a cave in the high desert, archaeologists have found some of the oldest shoes in the world. Sandals woven from sagebrush bark, around nine or ten thousand years old.

<chuckle> Oregon was making shoes long before Nike.

## Part 1B

On the Columbia River, at Celilo Falls, salmon leap up the rapids by the million. People fish from wooden platforms with long dip nets.

For thousands of years, it's one of the greatest trading places in North America. Nations come from hundreds of miles away. They trade so much that they invent a shared trade language, Chinook Jargon.

On the coast live the Chinook, the Tillamook, the Coos and many others. Inland, the Klamath, the Paiute, the Nez Perce and more.

## Part 2: Beaver Country

Now, the hat.

In the seventeen and eighteen hundreds, every fashionable man in Europe wants one thing. A tall felt hat. And the best felt in the world is made from beaver fur.

Europe's beavers have almost all been hunted out. But North America has millions.

And on the Pacific Northwest coast, there are beavers and sea otters, and nobody from Europe has claimed the land for sure.

## Part 2B

In eighteen-oh-five, Lewis and Clark reach the mouth of the Columbia. They spend a soggy winter at a little fort on the coast. It rains almost every day.

Then, in eighteen eleven, a New York fur merchant named John Jacob Astor sends men to build a trading post at the mouth of the Columbia. Astoria. The first American settlement on the Pacific coast.

His business fails. But in eighteen twelve, a group of his fur traders, heading back east, stumbles onto something precious.

A wide, gentle pass through the Rocky Mountains. South Pass. A way wagons could cross.

They don't realise how important it is. Not yet.

## Part 2C

For the next thirty years, Britain's Hudson's Bay Company runs the fur trade from a giant fort on the Columbia. Mountain men trap beavers in every stream.

Then, around eighteen-forty, fashion changes. Men in Europe start wearing silk hats. The beaver hat goes out of style.

<chuckle> The fur trade collapses. But the trails the trappers found are still there.

## Part 3: The Trail

And Americans in the East are hearing wonderful stories about Oregon. Rich soil. Mild winters. Free land.

In eighteen forty-three, around nine hundred people gather in Missouri with covered wagons and head west, through South Pass. It's the first big wagon train on the Oregon Trail.

Over the next twenty-five years, hundreds of thousands follow on the trails west.

The trip is about two thousand miles. It takes four to six months. Most people walk the whole way, beside their wagons, to save the oxen.

## Part 3B (quiet style)

Thousands die on the way. A few in accidents and river crossings. A few in conflicts. But most die of disease. Cholera. And yes, dysentery.

Graves line the trail.

## Part 3C

Britain and America both claim Oregon. In eighteen forty-six, they split it along the forty-ninth parallel.

And in eighteen fifty, Congress passes a law giving free land in Oregon. Three hundred and twenty acres for a single white man. Six hundred and forty for a married couple.

## Part 3D

In eighteen forty-five, two men own the land where a new town will grow. One is from Boston. The other is from Portland, Maine. Each wants to name it after his hometown.

So they flip a coin. Best two out of three.

<chuckle> The man from Maine wins. Otherwise, it would be Boston, Oregon.

## Part 4: Statehood

But the free land is only for white settlers. And the new settlers want Oregon to be white too.

## Part 4B (quiet style)

When Oregon becomes a state, on February fourteenth, eighteen fifty-nine, it is a free state. No slavery.

But its constitution also bans Black people from moving there, living there, or owning property.

It's the only free state ever admitted to the Union with a ban like that. The words are only fully removed from the constitution in two thousand two.

## Part 4C (quiet style)

As settlers flood in, Native nations are pushed onto small reservations. In the Rogue River Wars, many are killed, and the survivors are marched far north, away from their homelands.

## Part 4D

In nineteen-oh-two, Oregon becomes one of the first states to let ordinary voters write their own laws and vote on them directly. People call it the "Oregon System".

And Oregon has a deep blue lake, in the crater of a volcano that blew itself apart almost eight thousand years ago. Crater Lake. The deepest lake in the United States.

## Part 5: Timber and Weird

For decades, Oregon's biggest business is timber. Giant trees. Huge logging camps.

## Part 5B (quiet style)

During the Second World War, workers building ships move into a new town near Portland called Vanport. Many are Black families.

In nineteen forty-eight, a dike breaks. The whole town floods in an afternoon. At least fifteen people die, and thousands lose their homes.

## Part 5C

In nineteen seventy, a dead whale washes up on a beach in Florence. The highway department decides to blow it up. With half a ton of dynamite.

<chuckle> Chunks of whale rain down on the crowd. One crushes a car. It's still one of the most famous news clips ever filmed.

## Part 5D

And then, the shoes.

A University of Oregon track coach named Bill Bowerman wants lighter shoes for his runners. One morning, he looks at his wife's waffle iron, and pours rubber into it.

The waffle sole.

He and a former runner, Phil Knight, start a company. Knight sells shoes out of the trunk of his car. They name it after the Greek goddess of victory.

Nike.

## Part 5E

Oregon keeps trying new things first. A deposit on bottles and cans, in nineteen seventy-one. Voting by mail, for every election. And there's no sales tax.

Today, the state builds computer chips, grows wine, and Portland is famous for coffee, bikes and being proudly weird.

## Part 5F

So why would hundreds of thousands of people walk two thousand miles, across deserts and mountains, to get to Oregon?

It starts with a hat.

Europe wanted beaver hats. The hunt for beaver brought fur traders to the Pacific. One of them found the pass through the mountains. And when the beaver hats went out of fashion, that pass became a road for families, chasing the promise of free land.

<short pause> And that's why there's a beaver on the back of Oregon's flag.

<chuckle> One fashion trend. Two thousand miles. A whole state.
