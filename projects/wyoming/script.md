## Hook

Yellowstone? <short pause> The first national park in the world. Mostly in Wyoming.

Devils Tower? America's first national monument. Also Wyoming.

Wyoming has fewer people than any other state. Fewer than Washington, D.C.

<long pause> In eighteen eighty-five, a mob of miners in Rock Springs, Wyoming, killed at least twenty-eight Chinese workers.

And yet, this rough, wild territory of cowboys and miners was the first place in America to give women the vote for good. Fifty years before the rest of the country.

<short pause> So why would the Wild West be the first to let women vote?

<whispers> It starts with a territory that had six men for every woman.

## Part 1: First Peoples

Wyoming is high country. Mountains, sagebrush plains, and wind. Lots of wind.

For thousands of years, hunters follow buffalo and pronghorn here. High in the Bighorn Mountains, someone lays out a giant circle of stones with twenty-eight spokes. The Medicine Wheel.

Later, the land is home to the Shoshone, the Crow, the Arapaho, the Cheyenne and the Lakota.

The Lakota have a sacred place, a giant tower of stone rising out of the plains, grooved like claw marks. They call it Bear Lodge. Settlers later call it Devils Tower.

## Part 2: Mountain Men

In eighteen-oh-seven, a fur trapper named John Colter wanders alone through the mountains of northwest Wyoming. When he comes back, he talks about boiling mud, steaming ground, and water shooting into the sky.

<chuckle> Nobody believes him. They call it "Colter's Hell".

He's describing Yellowstone.

For the next twenty years, mountain men trap beavers all over Wyoming. Every summer, they gather with Native traders for a giant party and market called the Rendezvous. Trading furs, gambling, racing horses, and telling tall tales.

## Part 2B

Then come the trails. The Oregon Trail, the California Trail and the Mormon Trail all cross Wyoming, through South Pass.

Pioneers try to reach a giant granite rock by the Fourth of July. If they're there by then, they're on schedule to beat the snow. They carve their names into it. Independence Rock.

## Part 3: Equality

In eighteen sixty-seven, the transcontinental railroad arrives. Tent towns pop up along the tracks overnight. One of them, Cheyenne, grows so fast it's called "the Magic City of the Plains".

In eighteen sixty-eight, Wyoming becomes a territory.

And here's the problem. It's full of men. Railroad workers. Soldiers. Miners. Cowboys. In the first census, there are about six adult men for every adult woman.

Wyoming needs families. It needs women.

## Part 3B

So, in eighteen sixty-nine, Wyoming's first legislature passes a law.

Women in Wyoming may vote. And hold office.

Why? There are a few reasons. Some lawmakers truly believe it's right. Some hope the news will bring women and families to Wyoming. And some, people said, just want to embarrass the governor, who they thought would veto it.

<chuckle> He signs it.

Wyoming becomes the first place in America where women have a lasting right to vote. And among the very first in the world.

## Part 3C

The next year, Esther Morris becomes a justice of the peace. One of the first women judges in America.

In Laramie, women serve on juries for the first time in the world. Newspapers across America laugh. The juries do just fine.

## Part 3D

In eighteen seventy-two, Congress makes Yellowstone the world's first national park. Colter's Hell is real after all.

## Part 3E

In eighteen ninety, Wyoming asks to become a state. Some in Congress say: fine, but only if you drop women's voting.

Wyoming's lawmakers send a telegram back.

"We will remain out of the Union a hundred years, rather than come in without the women."

<short pause> Congress gives in. On July tenth, eighteen ninety, Wyoming becomes the forty-fourth state. And it picks a nickname. The Equality State.

## Part 4: Cowboys

Wyoming becomes cattle country. Giant herds graze the open range. Rich ranchers from the East, and even Britain, build fancy clubs in Cheyenne.

In eighteen ninety-two, big ranchers hire gunmen to kill small ranchers they accuse of stealing cattle. The Johnson County War. The army has to stop it.

And outlaws like Butch Cassidy hide in a canyon called Hole-in-the-Wall.

## Part 4B (quiet style)

In September eighteen eighty-five, in the coal town of Rock Springs, white miners, angry that the coal company hires Chinese workers for lower pay, and that they won't join a strike, attack the Chinese part of town.

They burn it down. At least twenty-eight Chinese miners are killed.

## Part 4C

In nineteen-oh-six, President Theodore Roosevelt makes Devils Tower America's first national monument.

## Part 5: Oil and Coal

Under Wyoming lies oil. And coal. A lot of it.

In the nineteen-twenties, a government official secretly leases a federal oil field in Wyoming, named after a rock shaped like a teapot, to his friends. In exchange for bribes.

The Teapot Dome scandal. He becomes the first cabinet member ever sent to prison.

## Part 5B

Also in the nineteen-twenties, Wyoming's governor dies. His widow, Nellie Tayloe Ross, runs for the job. And wins.

In nineteen twenty-five, she becomes the first woman governor in American history. Later, she runs the U.S. Mint. Her face is even on a medal.

## Part 5C (quiet style)

During the Second World War, Japanese American families are held at Heart Mountain, a camp in northern Wyoming.

## Part 5D

Today, the Powder River Basin produces a huge share of America's coal. Wind turbines spin across the plains. Tourists flood into Yellowstone and Grand Teton.

But Wyoming still has fewer than six hundred thousand people. The fewest of any state.

## Part 5E (quiet style)

In nineteen ninety-eight, near Laramie, a gay university student named Matthew Shepard is beaten and left tied to a fence. He dies days later. His death helps lead to a federal hate crimes law.

## Part 5F

So why would the Wild West be the first to let women vote?

It starts with a territory that had six men for every woman.

Wyoming needed women. Some of its lawmakers thought it was right. Some thought it was good advertising. Some thought it was a prank. But once women could vote, Wyoming refused to give it up. Even for statehood.

<short pause> The Equality State didn't get everything right. But it got that right first.

<chuckle> And it was willing to wait a hundred years to prove it.
