# The Entire History of Delaware

Narration script, written to be voiced with **Gemini TTS** and then animated. About 2,100 spoken words, so roughly 11 to 12 minutes when recorded (California ran 2,600 words to 13:50).

How to use it (the full rules are in `docs/PLAYBOOK.md` and `docs/HOOKS.md`):

- Each `##` heading is **one Gemini generation**. Paste only the lines under it, never the heading. Headings are paste breaks: never spoken, never shown. The film has no chapter cards.
- Paste the matching style line (table below) into Gemini's style box. Parts marked "quiet style" use the quiet line.
- Tags are angle brackets and only from Gemini's supported list: `<short pause>` `<long pause>` `<breath>` `<sigh>` `<exhales>` `<gasp>` `<chuckle>` `<laugh>` `<giggle>` `<phew>` `<tsk>` `<groan>` `<whispers>`. Never two tags side by side. CAPITALS stress a word; three dots add a hesitation; years and numbers are spelled out.

| Part | Style line for the style box |
| --- | --- |
| Hook | Quick, amused for the first four facts; slower and serious from "But Delaware kept slavery"; leaning in, almost whispering, on the last line. |
| Part 1 | Calm, marshy storyteller, with a wink. |
| Part 2 | Galloping urgency, then proud. |
| Part 3 | Industrious, then honest and heavy. |
| Part 4 | Sly, clever, a little conspiratorial. |
| Part 5 | Bright and inventive, small-town proud; smile on the very last line. |
| Lettered parts | Use the style line of their number. |
| Any part marked "quiet style" | Quiet, slow and respectful. No smile in the voice. Long gaps between sentences. |

On-screen timeline labels (the only sign of a new era): First Peoples 10,000 BC to 1638 · New Sweden 1638 to 1664 · First State 1664 to 1787 · Powder and Slavery 1802 to 1901 · Corporate Capital 1899 to 1945 · Today 1945 to now.

## Hook

Delaware was the first state. <short pause> The very first to approve the Constitution.

<chuckle> There are more companies registered in Delaware than there are people living there. About twice as many.

A single office building in Wilmington is the official address of hundreds of thousands of companies. And nylon? Invented by a Delaware company.

<long pause> But Delaware kept slavery right to the end of the Civil War. And it didn't approve the amendment that ended slavery until nineteen-oh-one.

<short pause> So how does the second-smallest state become the legal home of most of America's biggest companies?

<whispers> It starts with a mistake made by its neighbour.

## Part 1: First Peoples

Delaware is small, flat and low. A strip of land between the Delaware River and Bay on one side, and the Chesapeake on the other. <chuckle> It's so flat that its highest point is a gentle bump about four hundred and fifty feet above the sea, next to a road. It's the lowest state in America, on average.

<gasp> Every spring, on Delaware Bay's beaches, the biggest gathering of horseshoe crabs on Earth crawls out of the water to lay eggs. Hundreds of thousands of them. <short pause> They're older than the dinosaurs. And their bright blue blood is used to test vaccines and medicines for germs.

It's home to the Lenape and the Nanticoke.

<short pause> The Lenape call themselves "the real people". They fish for shad in the rivers, hunt deer, and farm corn, beans and squash. <sigh> As settlers take their land, most Lenape are pushed west, again and again, as far as Oklahoma and Canada. But the Nanticoke and the Lenape still hold gatherings in Delaware today.

## Part 1B

In sixteen-ten, an English captain names the bay after the governor of Virginia, Lord De La Warr.

<chuckle> Lord De La Warr never visits. But the bay, the river, the state, and even the Lenape people get called "Delaware" after him.

## Part 1C (quiet style)

In sixteen thirty-one, Dutch settlers build a little whaling post called Zwaanendael, at today's town of Lewes.

<breath> After a dispute with the local people, the settlement is attacked.

<long pause> All thirty-two settlers are killed. When a Dutch ship returns, it finds only burned ruins.

## Part 1D

In sixteen thirty-eight, Swedes and Finns arrive and build Fort Christina, where Wilmington is today. New Sweden.

<chuckle> Their leader is Peter Minuit. A few years earlier, working for the Dutch, he's the man who famously bought Manhattan. <short pause> Now he's switched sides, and he's working for Sweden. He dies in a storm in the Caribbean on the way home.

They bring something with them from the forests back home. A simple house made of logs, notched at the corners.

<short pause> The log cabin. It's thought to have been introduced to America here. Two centuries later, it becomes the symbol of the American frontier.

<chuckle> The Swedes also build a stone church in Wilmington, in sixteen ninety-nine. Old Swedes Church. It's still used today.

## Part 1E

<chuckle> Delaware's coast is also a favourite stop for pirates. <short pause> In seventeen hundred, the famous pirate Captain William Kidd anchors off Lewes, and townspeople row out to trade with him. They're later punished for it. <whispers> Legends say treasure is still buried in the dunes.

## Part 1F

The Dutch take over New Sweden in sixteen fifty-five. The English take it from the Dutch in sixteen sixty-four. Then it's given to William Penn, as the "Lower Counties" of Pennsylvania. Penn first steps onto American soil here, at New Castle, in sixteen eighty-two.

<laugh> And Delaware's border with Pennsylvania is drawn as a perfect curve. A circle, twelve miles out from the courthouse in New Castle. It's the only state border in America shaped like an arc.

In seventeen-oh-four, the Lower Counties get their own assembly. They're on their way to being their own colony.

<short pause> Thomas Jefferson later calls Delaware a jewel among the states, because it's small but valuable. <chuckle> That's why it's nicknamed the Diamond State.

## Part 2: First State

In July seventeen seventy-six, the Continental Congress is about to vote on independence. Delaware's two delegates in Philadelphia are split. <tsk> One yes, one no.

Its third delegate, Caesar Rodney, is eighty miles away, at home. He's sick with cancer, and wears a green silk scarf to hide his face.

<gasp> He gets the message. And he rides all night, through thunder and rain, to Philadelphia. He arrives, still in his boots and spurs, just in time to vote yes.

<phew> Delaware votes for independence. Rodney is on Delaware's quarter, galloping on his horse.

## Part 2B

<chuckle> In the war, Delaware's soldiers are said to carry fighting gamecocks with them, descended from a famous blue hen. They fight so fiercely that people call them the "Blue Hen's Chickens". <laugh> The Blue Hen is still Delaware's state bird.

<short pause> In seventeen seventy-seven, at Cooch's Bridge, Delaware's only Revolutionary War battle is fought. Some say it's where the new Stars and Stripes was first carried into battle.

## Part 2C

On December seventh, seventeen eighty-seven, Delaware becomes the first state to approve the new Constitution of the United States. Unanimously.

<chuckle> It's never let anyone forget it. It's on the licence plates. "The First State".

## Part 2D

<short pause> Enslaved as a boy on a farm in Delaware, a young man named Richard Allen buys his freedom, and moves to Philadelphia. <gasp> When white church leaders pull Black worshippers off their knees during a prayer, he walks out. He founds a new church. The African Methodist Episcopal Church. <short pause> It becomes one of the biggest Black churches in the world.

## Part 3: Powder and Slavery

In eighteen-oh-two, a French immigrant named Éleuthère Irénée du Pont builds a gunpowder mill on the Brandywine River, near Wilmington. <short pause> In France, he had learned chemistry from the great scientist Lavoisier. And he'd noticed that American gunpowder was terrible.

The mill buildings have thick stone walls on three sides, and a thin wooden roof facing the river. <gasp> So when one explodes, the blast goes up and out over the water, not into the next building.

## Part 3B (quiet style)

Explosions are common anyway.

<breath> In eighteen eighteen, a huge blast kills about forty workers.

The du Pont family lives right next to the mills, to share the danger with their workers.

## Part 3C

But the business grows. DuPont powder supplies American armies for more than a century, and DuPont becomes one of the biggest companies in America.

<chuckle> The du Pont family grows so rich that one of them, Henry Francis du Pont, turns his country house, Winterthur, into a museum with a hundred and seventy-five rooms of American furniture.

## Part 3D

<gasp> In the War of Eighteen-Twelve, British warships bombard the little town of Lewes for a whole day in eighteen thirteen, demanding food and water. <short pause> The town refuses. <laugh> The only casualties, according to the story, are a chicken, and a pig with a broken leg. One British cannonball is still stuck in the wall of a house in Lewes. The Cannonball House.

## Part 3E

Delaware is a slave state. But most enslaved people in Delaware are freed over time. By eighteen sixty, only around eighteen hundred people are still enslaved.

<short pause> A Quaker in Wilmington named Thomas Garrett helps thousands of people escape slavery. Harriet Tubman often passes through his house.

<tsk> In eighteen forty-eight, he's taken to court and fined so heavily that he loses almost everything he owns. <gasp> After the verdict, he tells the courtroom: if anyone knows a fugitive who needs help, send them to him. He keeps going for another seventeen years.

<tsk> And Delaware hangs on to old punishments for a very long time. Public whipping posts stand outside its jails until well into the twentieth century. <sigh> The last whipping happens in nineteen fifty-two.

## Part 3F (quiet style)

In the Civil War, Delaware stays in the Union.

<breath> On an island in the Delaware River, the Union builds Fort Delaware, a prison camp for captured Confederate soldiers. Thousands are packed in. <long pause> More than two thousand of them die there, mostly of disease.

## Part 3G (quiet style)

Delaware refuses to give up slavery on its own. Slavery in Delaware only ends when the Thirteenth Amendment makes it illegal across America, in December eighteen sixty-five.

<breath> Delaware's lawmakers vote against the amendment. They only approve it, symbolically, in nineteen-oh-one.

## Part 4: Corporate Capital

Now, the neighbour.

At the end of the eighteen hundreds, New Jersey is the place to start a company. Its laws are friendly to big business. <chuckle> Huge corporations register there.

In eighteen ninety-nine, Delaware copies New Jersey's laws, hoping to get some of that business.

Then, in nineteen thirteen, New Jersey's governor, Woodrow Wilson, about to become president, pushes through tough new laws against big monopolies. People call them the "Seven Sisters".

<laugh> Companies don't like them. So they pack up their paperwork, and move it across the river. To Delaware.

## Part 4B

And Delaware has a secret weapon.

<whispers> It has a special old court, from seventeen ninety-two, called the Court of Chancery. <short pause> No juries. Just expert judges who decide business arguments, fast, and write long explanations of why.

Companies love it. They know exactly what the rules are.

<gasp> Today, about two-thirds of the biggest companies in America are incorporated in Delaware. Even if they've never had an office there.

## Part 4C

And so, in Wilmington, there's an ordinary-looking office building at twelve-oh-nine North Orange Street. It's the registered address of hundreds of thousands of companies.

<chuckle> Their mail goes there. Their people don't.

<sigh> And corporate fees pay for a big part of Delaware's state budget.

## Part 4D

<chuckle> Meanwhile, one of the du Ponts, T. Coleman du Pont, decides Delaware needs a proper road. So he builds one, with his own money. <gasp> A modern highway, a hundred miles long, from one end of the state to the other. In nineteen twenty-four, he gives it to the state. The DuPont Highway.

<tsk> Not every company loves Delaware's court. In twenty twenty-four, the Court of Chancery strikes down a giant pay package for Elon Musk at Tesla. <short pause> Some big companies move their legal homes to Texas and Nevada in protest. Delaware rewrites some of its business laws to keep the rest. <short pause> In December twenty twenty-five, Delaware's Supreme Court reverses the ruling and restores the pay package. But the fight over who gets to be home for America's companies goes on.

## Part 5: Today

Meanwhile, DuPont's scientists keep inventing. In nineteen thirty-five, a chemist named Wallace Carothers invents nylon.

<gasp> In nineteen-forty, nylon stockings go on sale, and women line up around the block. During the Second World War, nylon goes into parachutes instead. <laugh> When stockings come back after the war, there are riots in the shops.

<short pause> In nineteen thirty-eight, a DuPont chemist opens a gas cylinder that should be full... and finds a slippery white powder inside. By accident, he's made Teflon. Later, DuPont invents Lycra and Kevlar too.

<short pause> Kevlar is invented in nineteen sixty-five by a DuPont chemist named Stephanie Kwolek, who notices a strange, cloudy liquid her colleagues want to throw away. <gasp> Spun into fibre, it's five times stronger than steel, for its weight. Today, it's in bulletproof vests.

## Part 5B

In nineteen twenty-three, a woman named Cecile Steele in Ocean View, Delaware, orders fifty chicks for her eggs. <chuckle> The company sends five hundred by mistake.

So she raises them for meat instead. They sell so well, she does it again. And again.

<gasp> It's the start of the modern chicken industry. Today, the Delmarva Peninsula raises hundreds of millions of chickens a year.

## Part 5C

<laugh> Delaware has some odd traditions. For years, in southern Delaware, people built giant catapults and air cannons, to see who could fling a pumpkin the furthest. Punkin Chunkin. Some pumpkins flew almost a mile.

<short pause> And two days after every election, in the town of Georgetown, the winners and losers ride through town together, in horse-drawn carriages. Then they bury a hatchet in a box of sand. Return Day.

<chuckle> And in the little town of Bridgeville, people hold a whole festival for scrapple. A breakfast loaf made of pork scraps and cornmeal, sliced and fried. <laugh> Apple Scrapple.

<short pause> And there's no sales tax. <chuckle> So shoppers from nearby states drive over to buy their televisions and computers. And in summer, they head to the beaches of Rehoboth.

## Part 5D

In the nineteen eighties, Delaware changes its banking laws, and credit card companies move in.

<short pause> And in twenty twenty-one, a man who'd represented Delaware in the Senate for thirty-six years, Joe Biden, becomes president. The first from Delaware.

## Part 5E (quiet style)

At Dover Air Force Base, in the centre of the state, every American service member who dies overseas is first brought home.

<breath> Families wait on the airfield, as the flag-covered cases are carried off the plane, one by one.

## Part 5F

So how does the second-smallest state become the legal home of most of America's biggest companies?

<whispers> It starts with a mistake made by its neighbour.

New Jersey made the rules tougher. Delaware made its rules clear, built a court that understood business, and waited. <short pause> Companies moved their paperwork across the river. And they never left.

<breath> The First State became the first choice.

<chuckle> Even if most of the companies there are just a name on a mailbox.
