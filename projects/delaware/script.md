## Hook

Delaware was the first state. <short pause> The very first to approve the Constitution.

There are more companies registered in Delaware than there are people living there. About twice as many.

A single office building in Wilmington is the official address of hundreds of thousands of companies.

Nylon? Invented by a Delaware company.

<long pause> But Delaware kept slavery right to the end of the Civil War. And it didn't approve the amendment that ended slavery until nineteen-oh-one.

<short pause> So how does the second-smallest state become the legal home of most of America's biggest companies?

<whispers> It starts with a mistake made by its neighbour.

## Part 1: First Peoples

Delaware is small, flat and low. A strip of land between the Delaware River and Bay on one side, and the Chesapeake on the other.

It's home to the Lenape and the Nanticoke.

In sixteen-ten, an English captain names the bay after the governor of Virginia, Lord De La Warr.

<chuckle> Lord De La Warr never visits. But the bay, the river, the state, and even the Lenape people get called "Delaware" after him.

## Part 1B

In sixteen thirty-eight, Swedes and Finns arrive and build Fort Christina, where Wilmington is today. New Sweden.

They bring something with them from the forests back home. A simple house made of logs, notched at the corners.

The log cabin. It's thought to have been introduced to America here.

The Dutch take over New Sweden in sixteen fifty-five. The English take it from the Dutch in sixteen sixty-four. Then it's given to William Penn, as the "Lower Counties" of Pennsylvania.

In seventeen-oh-four, the Lower Counties get their own assembly. They're on their way to being their own colony.

## Part 2: First State

In July seventeen seventy-six, the Continental Congress is about to vote on independence. Delaware's two delegates in Philadelphia are split. One yes, one no.

Its third delegate, Caesar Rodney, is eighty miles away, at home.

He gets the message. And he rides all night, through thunder and rain, to Philadelphia. He arrives just in time to vote yes.

Delaware votes for independence. Rodney is on Delaware's quarter, galloping on his horse.

## Part 2B

On December seventh, seventeen eighty-seven, Delaware becomes the first state to approve the new Constitution of the United States. Unanimously.

<chuckle> It's never let anyone forget it. It's on the licence plates. "The First State".

## Part 3: Powder and Slavery

In eighteen-oh-two, a French immigrant named Éleuthère Irénée du Pont builds a gunpowder mill on the Brandywine River, near Wilmington.

Explosions are common. But the business grows. DuPont powder supplies American armies for more than a century, and DuPont becomes one of the biggest companies in America.

## Part 3B

Delaware is a slave state. But most enslaved people in Delaware are freed over time, and by eighteen sixty, only around eighteen hundred people are still enslaved.

A Quaker in Wilmington named Thomas Garrett helps thousands of people escape slavery. Harriet Tubman often passes through his house.

## Part 3C (quiet style)

In the Civil War, Delaware stays in the Union. But it refuses to give up slavery on its own. Slavery in Delaware only ends when the Thirteenth Amendment makes it illegal across America, in December eighteen sixty-five.

Delaware's lawmakers vote against the amendment. They only approve it, symbolically, in nineteen-oh-one.

## Part 4: Corporate Capital

Now, the neighbour.

At the end of the eighteen hundreds, New Jersey is the place to start a company. Its laws are friendly to big business. Huge corporations register there.

In eighteen ninety-nine, Delaware copies New Jersey's laws, hoping to get some of that business.

Then, in nineteen thirteen, New Jersey's governor, Woodrow Wilson, about to become president, pushes through tough new laws against big monopolies. People call them the "Seven Sisters".

<chuckle> Companies don't like them. So they pack up their paperwork, and move it across the river. To Delaware.

## Part 4B

And Delaware has a secret weapon.

It has a special old court, from seventeen ninety-two, called the Court of Chancery. No juries. Just expert judges who decide business arguments, fast, and write long explanations of why.

Companies love it. They know exactly what the rules are.

Today, about two-thirds of the biggest companies in America are incorporated in Delaware. Even if they've never had an office there.

## Part 4C

And so, in Wilmington, there's an ordinary-looking office building at twelve-oh-nine North Orange Street. It's the registered address of hundreds of thousands of companies.

<chuckle> Their mail goes there. Their people don't.

## Part 5: Today

Meanwhile, DuPont's scientists keep inventing. In nineteen thirty-five, a chemist named Wallace Carothers invents nylon. In nineteen-forty, nylon stockings go on sale, and women line up around the block.

Later, DuPont invents Teflon, Lycra and Kevlar.

## Part 5B

In nineteen twenty-three, a woman named Cecile Steele in Ocean View, Delaware, orders fifty chicks for her eggs. The company sends five hundred by mistake.

So she raises them for meat instead. They sell so well, she does it again. And again.

It's the start of the modern chicken industry. Today, the Delmarva Peninsula raises hundreds of millions of chickens a year.

## Part 5C

In the nineteen eighties, Delaware changes its banking laws, and credit card companies move in.

And in twenty twenty-one, a man who'd represented Delaware in the Senate for thirty-six years, Joe Biden, becomes president. The first from Delaware.

## Part 5D

So how does the second-smallest state become the legal home of most of America's biggest companies?

It starts with a mistake made by its neighbour.

New Jersey made the rules tougher. Delaware made its rules clear, built a court that understood business, and waited. Companies moved their paperwork across the river. And they never left.

<short pause> The First State became the first choice.

<chuckle> Even if most of the companies there are just a name on a mailbox.
