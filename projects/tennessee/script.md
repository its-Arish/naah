## Hook

Mountain Dew? <short pause> Invented in Tennessee. And back then, "mountain dew" was a nickname for moonshine.

Jack Daniel's whiskey? It's made in a "dry" county.

American women got the right to vote because of one twenty-four-year-old in Tennessee. And a letter from his mom.

<long pause> In the nineteen forties, Tennessee built a secret city of seventy-five thousand people that wasn't on any map.

And inside it, they made the fuel for the first atomic bomb ever used in war.

<short pause> So how does a poor mountain state, famous for moonshine, end up building the atomic bomb?

<whispers> It starts with a river that kept flooding.

## Part 1: First Peoples

The Tennessee River is a strange river. It flows south into Alabama, then turns around and flows north, all the way back across Tennessee, to the Ohio.

People have lived along it for more than twelve thousand years. Mound builders raise great earthen platforms. Later, the Cherokee build towns in the eastern mountains. The Chickasaw hunt the west. The name "Tennessee" comes from a Cherokee town called Tanasi.

## Part 2: Frontier

In the seventeen sixties and seventies, settlers start crossing the Appalachian Mountains. Long hunters, who disappear into the woods for months. Daniel Boone. Families from Virginia and North Carolina.

Britain has told them not to cross. They cross anyway.

In seventeen seventy-two, settlers along the Watauga River write their own rules and govern themselves. On land that isn't theirs. They lease it from the Cherokee.

## Part 2B

After the Revolution, the land belongs to North Carolina. But North Carolina is far away, and doesn't help.

So in seventeen eighty-four, the settlers in the east just... declare a new state. They call it Franklin, after Benjamin Franklin, hoping he'll like it.

<chuckle> Congress never accepts it. Franklin has its own governor, its own courts, and for a while, people pay taxes in animal skins. After four years, it falls apart.

In seventeen ninety-six, Tennessee becomes the sixteenth state. The first one made from federal territory.

## Part 2C

Tennessee's frontiersmen love a fight.

Andrew Jackson, a Nashville lawyer, duellist and general, wins the Battle of New Orleans and later becomes president.

Davy Crockett, a congressman from Tennessee, goes to Texas and dies at the Alamo.

And in eighteen forty-six, when the government asks Tennessee for about twenty-six hundred volunteers for the war with Mexico, around thirty thousand men show up.

That's why it's called the Volunteer State.

## Part 3: Volunteers

But Jackson, as president, signs the Indian Removal Act.

The Cherokee have farms, schools, a constitution, and their own alphabet, invented by Sequoyah, who was born in Tennessee.

## Part 3B (quiet style)

In eighteen thirty-eight, many Cherokee are gathered in camps in southeastern Tennessee. From there, they are forced west.

Thousands die on the Trail of Tears.

## Part 4: Civil War

In eighteen sixty-one, Tennessee is the last state to leave the Union. The mountain people of the east mostly stay loyal to the Union. The cotton counties of the west want to leave.

## Part 4B (quiet style)

In April eighteen sixty-two, near a little church called Shiloh, the armies fight for two days.

More than twenty-three thousand men are killed, wounded or missing. More than all of America's earlier wars put together.

Tennessee sees more battles than almost any state except Virginia.

## Part 4C

After the war, Tennessee is the first Confederate state let back into the Union.

## Part 4D (quiet style)

But in eighteen sixty-five, in Pulaski, Tennessee, a group of former Confederate soldiers starts a secret society. The Ku Klux Klan. It spreads terror against Black citizens across the South.

## Part 5: Monkeys and Votes

In nineteen twenty, America is one state away from letting women vote. Thirty-five states have approved the Nineteenth Amendment. They need thirty-six.

It comes down to Tennessee. And the vote is tied.

A twenty-four-year-old legislator named Harry Burn is wearing a red rose. Red means "vote no". But in his pocket is a letter from his mother. "Hurrah, and vote for suffrage," she wrote. "Be a good boy."

He votes yes.

<short pause> And that's how women won the vote in America. Because a young man listened to his mom.

## Part 5B

Five years later, Tennessee is in the news again.

A new law bans teaching evolution in public schools. In the little town of Dayton, a teacher named John Scopes is put on trial for it. Reporters come from everywhere. The newspapers call it the Monkey Trial.

<chuckle> Scopes is found guilty and fined a hundred dollars. The town got a lot more famous than the teacher.

## Part 6: The Valley

But Tennessee has a bigger problem. It's poor. Really poor.

The Tennessee River floods again and again, washing away farms. Most farms have no electricity. The soil is worn out.

In nineteen thirty-three, during the Great Depression, President Franklin Roosevelt creates the Tennessee Valley Authority. The TVA.

It builds dam after dam on the Tennessee River and its streams. The dams stop the floods. And they make cheap electricity. Lots of it.

For the first time, farm families get electric lights.

## Part 6B

And then, the Second World War.

The United States needs to build an atomic bomb. To do it, it needs to separate a rare kind of uranium. That takes giant factories, and an enormous amount of electricity.

Where can you find that much power? In a quiet, hidden valley in East Tennessee, near the new TVA dams.

In nineteen forty-two, the army buys up the farms. Thousands of people are given a few weeks to leave.

And they build a city from nothing. Houses, schools, shops and giant plants. One of them, K-25, is the largest building in the world.

They call it Site X. Then Oak Ridge. By nineteen forty-five, seventy-five thousand people live there. It's not on any map. Most workers have no idea what they're making.

## Part 6C (quiet style)

On August sixth, nineteen forty-five, a uranium bomb is dropped on Hiroshima, Japan.

The uranium inside it was made in Oak Ridge.

Only then do the people of Oak Ridge find out what they had been building.

## Part 7: Music City

After the war, Tennessee becomes famous for something much louder.

In Nashville, the Grand Ole Opry radio show, which started in nineteen twenty-five, turns country music into a national sound.

In Memphis, in nineteen fifty-four, at a little studio called Sun Records, a truck driver named Elvis Presley records "That's All Right". Rock and roll is born. Johnny Cash, Jerry Lee Lewis and Carl Perkins follow.

And in Memphis, Stax Records makes soul music, and the blues fill Beale Street.

## Part 7B

In nineteen sixty, Black college students in Nashville sit down at whites-only lunch counters and refuse to leave. They are arrested, beaten and insulted. They keep coming back. Within months, Nashville's lunch counters integrate.

## Part 7C (quiet style)

In April nineteen sixty-eight, Martin Luther King Jr. comes to Memphis to support striking sanitation workers.

On April fourth, he is shot and killed on the balcony of the Lorraine Motel.

The motel is now the National Civil Rights Museum.

## Part 7D

And from the Smoky Mountains comes a girl who grew up with eleven brothers and sisters in a one-room cabin. Dolly Parton.

She becomes one of the biggest stars in country music, builds a theme park called Dollywood, and sends free books to millions of children.

## Part 7E

So how does a poor mountain state, famous for moonshine, end up building the atomic bomb?

It starts with a river that kept flooding.

To stop the floods, the TVA built the dams. The dams made cheap power. And when America needed more electricity than anywhere could give, to build a weapon nobody had ever built... it came to the valley that the river had made.

<short pause> Tennessee has always been a place where ordinary people end up changing history. A boy who listened to his mother. A truck driver with a guitar. A secret city of people who didn't know what they were making.

<chuckle> And a soft drink named after moonshine.
