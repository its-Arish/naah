## Hook

The Frisbee? <short pause> Named after a Connecticut pie company.

A little lunch counter in New Haven says it served the first hamburger sandwich, in nineteen hundred. <chuckle> It still won't give you ketchup.

America's first phone book was printed in Connecticut. Fifty names. No numbers.

<long pause> In sixteen thirty-seven, English soldiers surrounded a Pequot village at Mystic, and set it on fire. Hundreds of people inside were killed.

And Hartford, Connecticut, calls itself the insurance capital of the world.

<short pause> So how does one of the smallest states end up insuring the world?

<whispers> It starts with ships that never came home.

## Part 1: First Peoples

Connecticut is named after its great river. In the Algonquian language of the people who lived there, "Quinetucket" means "the long tidal river".

The Mohegan, the Pequot, the Niantic, the Quinnipiac and others fish its rivers and coast, farm corn, and make wampum, beads carved from shells, that are used for trade and treaties.

## Part 2: Puritan River Towns

In sixteen fourteen, a Dutch explorer named Adriaen Block sails up the river. Dutch traders build a little fort where Hartford is today.

Then the English arrive. In sixteen thirty-six, a Puritan minister named Thomas Hooker leads about a hundred people from Massachusetts, with their cattle, through the woods to the Connecticut River. They found Hartford.

## Part 2B

In sixteen thirty-nine, the river towns write down how they'll govern themselves. The Fundamental Orders.

It's one of the first written constitutions in history. The power comes from the people, not from a king.

That's why Connecticut is called the Constitution State.

## Part 2C (quiet style)

But the English and their allies are at war with the powerful Pequot nation.

In May sixteen thirty-seven, English soldiers, with Mohegan and Narragansett allies, surround a fortified Pequot village near the Mystic River before dawn. They set it on fire. People who try to escape are shot.

Somewhere between four hundred and seven hundred Pequot are killed, most of them women, children and elders.

After the war, the English try to wipe out even the Pequot name. But the Pequot survive.

## Part 2D

In sixteen eighty-seven, a royal governor arrives to take Connecticut's charter away. During the meeting, the candles go out. When they're relit, the charter is gone.

Legend says it was hidden in a giant hollow oak tree. The Charter Oak. It's on Connecticut's state quarter.

## Part 3: Revolution

In seventeen seventy-six, a young Connecticut schoolteacher named Nathan Hale volunteers to spy on the British. He's caught and hanged. His famous last words: "I only regret that I have but one life to lose for my country."

But Connecticut also produces America's most famous traitor. Benedict Arnold, from Norwich. In seventeen eighty-one, fighting for the British, he burns New London, a town just a few miles from where he grew up.

## Part 3B

In seventeen eighty-seven, at the Constitutional Convention, big states and small states are deadlocked. Should states get votes by population, or equal votes?

Connecticut's delegates, Roger Sherman and Oliver Ellsworth, propose both. One house by population. One house where every state gets two senators.

The Connecticut Compromise. It's still how Congress works.

## Part 4: Yankee Peddlers

Connecticut has rocky soil. So its people turn to trade, and to making things.

Connecticut peddlers travel the country selling clocks, tin pots and buttons. They're so clever that legends say some even sold fake nutmegs, carved from wood.

<chuckle> That's why Connecticut is called the Nutmeg State.

In New Haven, Eli Whitney builds muskets with interchangeable parts. In Hartford, Samuel Colt builds revolvers in a giant factory.

## Part 4B

In eighteen thirty-nine, Africans kidnapped into slavery take over a Spanish ship called the Amistad. It ends up off Long Island, and they're jailed in Connecticut.

Their case goes all the way to the Supreme Court. Former president John Quincy Adams argues for them. And they win their freedom.

## Part 5: Insurance City

Now, the ships.

In the seventeen hundreds, Connecticut merchants send ships to the Caribbean, carrying timber, horses and food. Storms sink some. Pirates take others. A merchant could lose everything with one ship.

So merchants start meeting in coffee houses and taverns to share the risk. Each one promises to pay a small part if a ship is lost. They sign their names underneath the list of cargo.

They're called underwriters. And it's the start of the insurance business in Hartford.

## Part 5B

In seventeen ninety-four, Hartford's first fire insurance company is founded. Then more.

In eighteen thirty-five, a giant fire burns much of New York City. Many insurance companies go bust and can't pay.

But the president of a Hartford insurance company travels to New York through the snow, and promises to pay every claim in full. Even if it means risking his own money.

Overnight, Hartford's insurance is famous for keeping its promises. Customers pour in.

## Part 5C

In eighteen sixty-four, a Hartford company called Travelers sells America's first accident insurance policy. The price: two cents. For a man walking home from the post office.

Later, Travelers writes some of the first car insurance. And Aetna, Travelers, and dozens of others make Hartford the insurance capital of the world.

## Part 5D

Hartford is also home to Mark Twain, who writes Tom Sawyer and Huckleberry Finn in his house there. His next-door neighbour is Harriet Beecher Stowe.

In eighteen seventy-eight, New Haven prints the first telephone directory. It has fifty names, and no numbers. <chuckle> You just asked the operator.

Yale students throw empty pie tins from the Frisbie Pie Company to each other. Later, a toy company calls a plastic flying disc the Frisbee.

And in nineteen fifty-four, in Groton, the Navy launches the world's first nuclear-powered submarine. The Nautilus.

## Part 5E (quiet style)

On December fourteenth, twenty twelve, a gunman attacks Sandy Hook Elementary School in Newtown, Connecticut. Twenty children and six adults are killed.

## Part 5F

So how does one of the smallest states end up insuring the world?

It starts with ships that never came home.

Connecticut's merchants couldn't afford to lose a ship. So they shared the risk. That habit grew into fire insurance, then a reputation for paying when others wouldn't. And Hartford became the city that promises to pay when things go wrong.

<short pause> The Constitution State built its fortune on keeping its word.

<chuckle> And on the occasional wooden nutmeg.
