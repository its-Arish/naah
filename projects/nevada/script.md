## Hook

Las Vegas? It started as a railroad stop. In nineteen oh five.

Area 51? The government only admitted it existed... in twenty thirteen.

In the nineteen fifties, tourists in Las Vegas threw parties on hotel roofs to watch atomic bombs go off on the horizon.

And Nevada became a state by telegram. The longest, most expensive telegram anyone had ever sent.

<long pause> Because in eighteen sixty-four, Nevada didn't have nearly enough people to be a state. Washington let it in anyway.

<short pause> So how does an empty desert, rushed into the Union with too few people, become a place forty million people visit every year?

<whispers> It starts with that telegram.

## Part 1: First Nevadans

Nevada is a land of basins and ranges. Mountain, desert valley, mountain, desert valley, over and over, hundreds of times. Rivers here don't reach the sea. They just sink into the sand.

People have lived here for more than ten thousand years. The Northern Paiute, the Southern Paiute, the Western Shoshone, the Washoe around Lake Tahoe.

In a cave near Lovelock, archaeologists found duck decoys made of reeds and feathers, about two thousand years old. Some of the oldest decoys in the world. People were hunting ducks in the desert... with fake ducks.

## Part 2: Silver

For most outsiders, Nevada was something to get across. Wagon trains on the way to California dreaded it. The Forty Mile Desert. No water. Bones of oxen along the trail.

Then, in eighteen fifty-nine, miners near a place that becomes Virginia City find a strange blue-grey mud clogging their gold pans. They curse it and throw it away.

It's silver. The richest silver strike in American history. The Comstock Lode.

Virginia City explodes. Mansions, opera houses, saloons, mines going down thousands of feet. Hot water floods the tunnels, so miners work in heat that can kill.

And a young newspaper reporter in Virginia City signs a story with a new pen name, in eighteen sixty-three.

Mark Twain.

## Part 3: Battle Born

Then the Civil War.

President Lincoln wants to pass the Thirteenth Amendment, to end slavery. He needs votes in Congress. And he faces a tough re-election.

Nevada Territory is solidly on his side. But it only has around forty thousand people. Far fewer than a state normally needs.

Washington lets it in anyway.

There's just one problem. The new state constitution has to reach Washington, fast. So in October eighteen sixty-four, an operator in Carson City taps out the entire constitution on the telegraph. Sixteen thousand words. Hour after hour.

It costs thousands of dollars. The longest and most expensive telegram ever sent, up to that time.

On October thirty-first, eighteen sixty-four, Nevada becomes the thirty-sixth state. Battle Born. It's still on the flag.

A few months later, Nevada's new congressman votes for the Thirteenth Amendment.

## Part 3B

But here's the catch.

When the silver runs out in the eighteen eighties, Nevada is a state with almost nobody in it. By nineteen hundred, only about forty thousand people. Some in Congress even suggest taking away its statehood.

How does a tiny, empty state survive?

It does what bigger states won't. It makes things legal.

First, prize fighting. In eighteen ninety-seven, Carson City hosts a world heavyweight championship that most states had banned. Then, in nineteen ten, Reno hosts the "Fight of the Century".

Then, divorce. Most states make divorce very hard. Nevada makes it easy. In nineteen thirty-one, you only need to live in Nevada for six weeks. Reno becomes the Divorce Capital of the World. Rich people from New York come out to stay at "divorce ranches".

And that same year, nineteen thirty-one... Nevada legalises gambling.

## Part 4: Anything Goes

Las Vegas began in nineteen oh five, when the railroad auctioned off plots of land in the desert. A water stop.

In nineteen thirty-one, the federal government starts building a giant dam on the Colorado River nearby. Thousands of workers pour in. They have paychecks... and Las Vegas has casinos.

Hoover Dam is finished in nineteen thirty-six. It lights up the whole Southwest. And Las Vegas.

After the war, gangsters notice. In nineteen forty-six, a mobster named Bugsy Siegel opens the Flamingo hotel on a lonely stretch of highway. The Strip begins.

## Part 4B

And then, the bombs.

In January nineteen fifty-one, the government opens the Nevada Test Site, sixty-five miles from Las Vegas. Over the next decade, it sets off a hundred nuclear explosions above ground.

Las Vegas turns it into a tourist attraction. Hotels advertise the best views. People throw dawn "atomic parties". A showgirl is crowned Miss Atomic Bomb.

Later, the tests move underground. More than eight hundred of them, until nineteen ninety-two.

Nearby, on a dry lake bed, the government builds a secret base to test spy planes. For decades, it officially doesn't exist.

Area fifty-one.

## Part 4C (quiet style)

The winds carried the fallout east.

Into Utah. Into small towns in Nevada and Arizona.

Over the following years, many families there developed cancers. They became known as the downwinders.

In nineteen ninety, Congress passed a law to compensate some of them.

## Part 5: Vegas

In the nineteen sixties, Las Vegas means Frank Sinatra and his friends on stage. Then Elvis.

Then a billionaire changes everything. Howard Hughes moves into the Desert Inn in nineteen sixty-six. When the hotel asks him to leave, he buys it. Then he buys more casinos. Big companies follow him, and slowly the mob is pushed out.

Then came the mega-resorts. Pyramids, castles, a fake Venice, a fake Paris. And Las Vegas becomes one of the fastest-growing cities in America.

## Part 5B (quiet style)

On October first, twenty seventeen, a gunman fires from a hotel window onto a crowd at a country music festival on the Las Vegas Strip.

Sixty people are killed.

It is the deadliest mass shooting by a single gunman in American history.

The city held vigils for weeks. The phrase on the signs was Vegas Strong.

## Part 6: Today

Today, Las Vegas has major league teams. Hockey's Golden Knights. Football's Raiders. And a giant glowing sphere that's become the city's newest landmark.

Around forty million people visit every year.

Near Reno, the desert now holds giant battery factories and data centres.

And here's one more strange Nevada fact. About eighty percent of the land in Nevada is owned by the federal government. More than any other state.

## Part 6B

So how does an empty desert, rushed into the Union with too few people, become a place forty million people visit every year?

It starts with that telegram. Nevada became a state before it had the people to be one. When the silver ran out, it had to find a way to survive. So it said yes to everything other states said no to. Boxing. Divorce. Gambling.

<short pause> The state that was born in a hurry... learned to make a living out of other people's fun.

<chuckle> And if you ever lose at the tables, just remember. Nevada's been betting on itself since eighteen sixty-four.
