## Hook

Cheerios? <short pause> Minnesota. Spam? Minnesota. Scotch tape and Post-it notes? Both Minnesota.

"The Land of ten thousand lakes"? It actually has more than eleven thousand.

The biggest shopping mall in America is in Minnesota. With a theme park inside it.

<long pause> In eighteen sixty-two, the United States hanged thirty-eight Dakota men in Minnesota, on a single day. The largest mass execution in American history.

<short pause> So how does a frozen state, far from any ocean, end up feeding America its breakfast?

<whispers> It starts with the only great waterfall on the Mississippi River.

## Part 1: First Peoples

When the glaciers of the last Ice Age melted, they left thousands of holes in the ground. They filled with water. Lakes. Everywhere.

The Mississippi River starts here, as a little stream flowing out of Lake Itasca. You can walk across it on stepping stones.

For thousands of years, this is the land of the Dakota. Later, the Ojibwe move in from the east. They gather wild rice from the lakes in canoes, and tap maple trees for sugar.

## Part 1B

Where the Minnesota River flows into the Mississippi, there's a place the Dakota call Bdote. It's sacred. For many Dakota, it's where their people began.

And a few miles upstream, the Mississippi drops over a wide ledge of rock. A roaring waterfall.

In sixteen eighty, a French priest named Louis Hennepin sees it and names it after his patron saint. The Falls of Saint Anthony.

## Part 2: Fur and Forts

French and British fur traders paddle through the lakes. Voyageurs, in giant canoes, singing as they paddle.

In eighteen nineteen, the U.S. Army builds Fort Snelling on the bluff above Bdote. Among the people who live there for a while: an enslaved man named Dred Scott. Years later, he'll point to his time at Fort Snelling, in free territory, when he sues for his freedom.

In eighteen fifty-one, the Dakota are pressured to sign treaties giving up most of their land in exchange for promised money and food. In eighteen fifty-eight, Minnesota becomes the thirty-second state.

## Part 2B

When the Civil War begins, Minnesota's governor happens to be in Washington. He's among the very first to offer troops to President Lincoln.

At Gettysburg, the First Minnesota is ordered to charge a much bigger Confederate force to buy a few minutes of time. They do it. Most of the men who charge are killed or wounded.

## Part 2C (quiet style)

In the summer of eighteen sixty-two, the Dakota are starving. The government's promised payments are late. Traders refuse to give them food on credit. One trader is quoted saying, if they're hungry, "let them eat grass".

In August, some Dakota warriors attack. War spreads across southern Minnesota. Hundreds of settlers are killed.

When the war ends, military trials last just minutes each. More than three hundred Dakota men are sentenced to death. President Lincoln reduces the list to thirty-nine. One more is spared.

On December twenty-sixth, eighteen sixty-two, in Mankato, thirty-eight Dakota men are hanged together.

Around sixteen hundred Dakota, mostly women and children, are held in a camp below Fort Snelling through the winter. Many die. Then the Dakota are expelled from Minnesota.

## Part 3: Mill City

Now, the waterfall.

St. Anthony Falls is the only great natural waterfall on the whole Mississippi. And falling water means power.

At first, it powers sawmills. Minnesota's huge pine forests are cut down and floated to the falls. Then, flour mills.

Minnesota's farms grow mountains of wheat. And the mills at the falls grind it into flour. Lots of it. By the eighteen-eighties, Minneapolis is the flour-milling capital of the world.

People call it the Mill City.

## Part 3B (quiet style)

But flour dust can explode. In eighteen seventy-eight, the giant Washburn "A" Mill blows up. The blast is heard for miles. Eighteen workers are killed.

## Part 3C

The mills rebuild, safer and bigger. Two rival companies grow giant. One run by the Pillsbury family. The other, Washburn-Crosby, becomes General Mills.

And they start making breakfast.

General Mills invents a cooking expert to answer customers' letters. She doesn't exist. <chuckle> Her name is Betty Crocker.

Then cereals. Wheaties, "the Breakfast of Champions", in nineteen twenty-four. And in nineteen forty-one, a little puffed oat "O". Today we call them Cheerios.

## Part 4: Iron and Ideas

In the north, miners discover a giant range of iron ore. The Mesabi Range. Its ore is shipped across Lake Superior to steel mills. During the Second World War, much of America's iron comes from Minnesota.

## Part 4B

In nineteen-oh-two, five businessmen in the town of Two Harbors start a company to mine a hard mineral for making sandpaper. The Minnesota Mining and Manufacturing Company.

The mineral turns out to be worthless.

<chuckle> So they figure out how to make sandpaper anyway. Then, in nineteen twenty-five, a young employee named Richard Drew watches car painters struggle with tape that ruins their paint. He invents masking tape. Then clear, sticky Scotch tape. Later, the company invents Post-it notes.

Today, you know the company as 3M.

## Part 4C

In nineteen thirty-seven, the Hormel company in Austin, Minnesota, launches a canned meat. Spam. During the Second World War, it feeds millions of soldiers around the world.

In Rochester, a doctor and his two sons build a small clinic into one of the most famous hospitals on Earth. The Mayo Clinic.

## Part 5: Today

Minnesota also gives the world Bob Dylan, Prince, Judy Garland, and the pilot Charles Lindbergh.

In nineteen ninety-two, the Mall of America opens near Minneapolis. More than five hundred stores. And a theme park with roller coasters, indoors.

<chuckle> In Minnesota, you need an indoor roller coaster. It's minus twenty outside.

## Part 5B (quiet style)

In August two thousand seven, the I-thirty-five-W bridge over the Mississippi in Minneapolis collapses during rush hour. Thirteen people are killed.

## Part 5C (quiet style)

In May two thousand twenty, in Minneapolis, a police officer kneels on George Floyd's neck for more than nine minutes. He dies. Protests spread around the world.

## Part 5D

So how does a frozen state, far from any ocean, end up feeding America its breakfast?

It starts with the only great waterfall on the Mississippi River.

The falls powered the mills. The mills turned Minnesota's wheat into the world's flour. And the flour companies turned into General Mills and Pillsbury, putting cereal in bowls across America.

<short pause> And the waterfall is still there, in the middle of Minneapolis, next to the ruins of the old mills.

<chuckle> Probably surrounded by people eating Cheerios.
