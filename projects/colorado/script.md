## Hook

Chipotle? <short pause> Started in Denver. And a man in Denver once trademarked the word "cheeseburger".

Colorado looks like a perfect rectangle. It isn't. It has almost seven hundred sides.

The song "America the Beautiful" was written after a trip to the top of a Colorado mountain.

<long pause> In eighteen sixty-four, soldiers attacked a peaceful camp at Sand Creek. Most of the dead were women, children and elders.

And Colorado's famous gold rush? It was named after a mountain that didn't have any gold.

<short pause> So how does a gold rush named after the wrong mountain build one of the fastest-growing states in America?

<whispers> It starts with a mountain an explorer swore no one would ever climb.

## Part 1: Cliff Dwellers

About eight hundred years ago, in the canyons of southwest Colorado, people build entire villages inside the cliffs.

At Mesa Verde, the Ancestral Puebloans fit stone houses, towers and round ceremonial rooms called kivas under huge overhanging rock. One of them, Cliff Palace, has around a hundred and fifty rooms.

They climb in and out on toeholds cut into the rock.

And then, around thirteen hundred, they leave. Probably because of long droughts. They move south, and their descendants live among the Pueblo peoples of New Mexico and Arizona today.

## Part 1B

The mountains become the home of the Ute. Out on the eastern plains, the Cheyenne, the Arapaho, the Comanche and the Kiowa follow the buffalo, on horses that came north from Spanish New Mexico.

## Part 2: Explorers

In eighteen-oh-three, the United States buys a huge chunk of the West from France. The Louisiana Purchase. The eastern half of Colorado is part of it.

In eighteen-oh-six, an army explorer named Zebulon Pike sees a giant mountain on the horizon. He tries to climb it. In November. With his men wearing summer uniforms.

<chuckle> He doesn't make it. He writes that no human being could ever reach the top.

The mountain is named after him anyway. Pikes Peak.

A few years later, another explorer crosses the plains and labels them on his map as "the Great American Desert". Not a place anyone would want to live.

## Part 3: Gold Rush

Then, in eighteen fifty-eight, a small party finds gold where Cherry Creek meets the South Platte River.

The news spreads east. And because nobody back home has heard of Cherry Creek, the newspapers name the rush after the one landmark everyone knows. Pikes Peak. About eighty miles away.

<chuckle> The Pikes Peak Gold Rush. Not at Pikes Peak.

In eighteen fifty-nine, around a hundred thousand people head west, many with "Pikes Peak or Bust!" painted on their wagons.

Lots of them find very little gold. Some go home with a new slogan painted on their wagons. "Busted, by God."

## Part 3B

The camp at Cherry Creek becomes a town. Its founders name it after the governor of Kansas Territory, James Denver, hoping he'll help them.

He'd already quit. <chuckle> They keep the name anyway.

Denver.

The real gold turns up in the mountains west of town, at places like Central City. In eighteen sixty-one, Colorado becomes a territory. Its border is drawn as a neat rectangle on paper.

But surveyors in the eighteen hundreds make lots of small mistakes on the ground. So the real border zigzags. Today it has six hundred and ninety-seven sides.

## Part 3C (quiet style)

The gold rush brings miners onto land promised to the Cheyenne and Arapaho.

In November eighteen sixty-four, a Cheyenne and Arapaho camp at Sand Creek is flying an American flag and a white flag. Their leader, Black Kettle, believes they are under the army's protection.

At dawn, Colonel John Chivington's soldiers attack.

Around two hundred and thirty people are killed. Most of them women, children and elders.

Some officers refuse to fire. One of them, Captain Silas Soule, testifies about what he saw. Congress condemns the massacre.

## Part 3D (quiet style)

Over the next years, the Cheyenne and Arapaho are pushed out of Colorado. In eighteen eighty-one, most of the Ute are forced off their mountain lands too.

## Part 4: Silver

In eighteen seventy-six, a hundred years after the Declaration of Independence, Colorado becomes a state. The Centennial State.

And then... silver.

In Leadville, high in the mountains, miners strike silver. A shopkeeper named Horace Tabor helps two prospectors with supplies in exchange for a share of what they find. They find a fortune.

Tabor becomes one of the richest men in America. He builds an opera house. He scandalises everyone by marrying a young divorcée named Baby Doe.

In eighteen ninety-three, the price of silver collapses. Tabor loses everything.

## Part 4B

That same summer, a teacher named Katharine Lee Bates rides a wagon to the top of Pikes Peak.

From the top, she sees the plains stretching east, and the mountains all around. She writes a poem.

"America the Beautiful."

<short pause> The mountain that Pike said no human would ever climb... now has a road, a railway, and a gift shop on top.

## Part 4C

Also in eighteen ninety-three, Colorado's men vote to give women the right to vote. It's the first state where men vote for women's suffrage at the ballot box.

## Part 5: Hard Times

In the coal mines of southern Colorado, miners work long, dangerous hours. In nineteen thirteen, they go on strike and move into tent colonies.

## Part 5B (quiet style)

In April nineteen fourteen, at Ludlow, guards and soldiers attack the tent colony.

A fire spreads through the tents. In a pit under one tent, two women and eleven children are found dead.

It becomes known as the Ludlow Massacre.

## Part 5C (quiet style)

During the Second World War, Japanese American families are held at a camp called Amache, on the plains of southeast Colorado.

## Part 6: Ski Country

Also during the war, the army needs soldiers who can fight in the mountains of Europe. On skis.

At Camp Hale, high in the Colorado Rockies, it trains the Tenth Mountain Division. Skiers, climbers, and mountain men.

After the war, many of them come back. And they build ski resorts. Veterans of the Tenth help create places like Vail and Arapahoe Basin, and help Aspen become a ski town.

Colorado discovers its real treasure. Snow.

## Part 6B

In the Cold War, the military digs a command centre deep inside Cheyenne Mountain, near Colorado Springs. It watches the skies over North America. And every Christmas Eve, it tracks Santa.

## Part 6C (quiet style)

In April nineteen ninety-nine, two students attack Columbine High School, near Denver. Thirteen people are killed.

## Part 6D

Today, Denver is booming. People pour in for the mountains, the sunshine, and the jobs. Colorado has more fourteen-thousand-foot peaks than any other state. And in two thousand twelve, it became one of the first two states to legalise marijuana for adults.

And in nineteen ninety-three, a young chef opened a burrito shop near the University of Denver. Chipotle.

## Part 6E

So how does a gold rush named after the wrong mountain build one of the fastest-growing states in America?

It starts with a mountain an explorer swore no one would ever climb.

That mountain gave the gold rush its name. The gold rush built Denver. The silver made it rich, then broke it. And then people came back, again and again, not for what was under the mountains... but for the mountains themselves.

<short pause> Colorado's real gold was never in the ground.

<chuckle> It was in the view. And maybe the burritos.
