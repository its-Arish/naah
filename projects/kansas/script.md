## Hook

Pizza Hut? <short pause> Started in Kansas. White Castle, the first fast-food hamburger chain? Also Kansas.

Scientists once measured it. Kansas really is flatter than a pancake.

And Wichita builds so many airplanes, it calls itself the Air Capital of the World.

<long pause> But before the Civil War, Kansas had its own war. Neighbours murdered neighbours. Towns were burned. People called it Bleeding Kansas.

<short pause> So how does a flat, quiet prairie become the place where the Civil War really began?

<whispers> It starts with crates of rifles, labelled "Bibles".

## Part 1: First Peoples

Kansas is grass. An ocean of tall grass and short grass, rolling to the horizon. And millions of buffalo.

The Kansa people, who give the state its name, the Osage, the Pawnee and the Wichita live along its rivers. The Cheyenne, the Arapaho, the Kiowa and the Comanche hunt on its western plains.

## Part 1B

In fifteen forty-one, Coronado marches all the way to Kansas, still hunting for a city of gold called Quivira.

<chuckle> He finds grass houses, and no gold.

In the eighteen-twenties, the Santa Fe Trail crosses Kansas, carrying wagon trains of goods to New Mexico.

And the government moves eastern nations, like the Shawnee, the Delaware and the Potawatomi, onto reservations in Kansas. Promising it will be theirs forever. Within a few decades, most are moved again.

## Part 2: Bleeding Kansas

Now, the rifles.

In eighteen fifty-four, America is splitting over slavery. Every new state tips the balance in Congress. Slave or free.

So Congress passes the Kansas-Nebraska Act. It says: let the settlers decide. Let the people of Kansas vote on whether to allow slavery.

And immediately, both sides race to fill Kansas with their own voters.

## Part 2B

From New England, anti-slavery groups pay for families to move to Kansas. They found towns like Lawrence and Topeka.

And they send them guns. Sharp's rifles. Packed into crates, sometimes labelled "Bibles".

A famous preacher, Henry Ward Beecher, says a rifle might do more good in Kansas than a Bible. So people start calling the rifles "Beecher's Bibles".

## Part 2C

From next-door Missouri, a slave state, armed men called "Border Ruffians" ride into Kansas on election days. They vote. Then they ride home.

In one election, Kansas has fewer than three thousand eligible voters. More than six thousand votes are counted.

<chuckle> Kansas ends up with two governments. One for slavery. One against. Each says the other is fake.

## Part 2D (quiet style)

In eighteen fifty-six, a pro-slavery posse rides into Lawrence and burns its hotel and newspaper offices.

Days later, an anti-slavery man named John Brown and his sons drag five pro-slavery settlers from their homes along Pottawatomie Creek, and kill them with swords.

Raids and revenge killings spread. Dozens of people die.

## Part 2E

Bleeding Kansas is the Civil War before the Civil War.

In January eighteen sixty-one, as Southern states are leaving the Union, Kansas finally joins it. As the thirty-fourth state. A free state.

## Part 3: War (quiet style)

In August eighteen sixty-three, a Confederate guerrilla named William Quantrill leads about four hundred raiders into Lawrence at dawn.

They go house to house. They kill around a hundred and fifty men and boys, and burn the town.

## Part 4: Cowtowns

After the war, Texas cowboys drive giant herds of cattle north, to the new railroads in Kansas. Abilene. Wichita. Dodge City.

Cowtowns. Saloons. Gunfights. Lawmen like Wyatt Earp and Wild Bill Hickok.

<chuckle> Dodge City gets so rowdy, it buries people on a hill. Boot Hill. Because they died with their boots on.

## Part 4B

In eighteen seventy-nine, thousands of Black families leave the South, where Reconstruction has ended, and head to Kansas. The free state John Brown fought for.

They're called Exodusters. Some join Black towns like Nicodemus, which still stands today.

## Part 4C

And Mennonite farmers from Russia bring a tough kind of wheat called Turkey Red. It survives Kansas winters.

Kansas becomes the breadbasket of America.

## Part 4D

Kansas is also stubborn about alcohol. In eighteen eighty-one, it becomes the first state to ban alcohol in its constitution.

And a six-foot-tall woman named Carry Nation walks into saloons with a hatchet... and smashes them to pieces.

## Part 5: Wheat and Wings

In the early nineteen-hundreds, Wichita starts building airplanes. Clyde Cessna. Walter Beech. Lloyd Stearman.

In the Second World War, Wichita's factories build thousands of bombers. After the war, small planes for the whole world. The Air Capital.

And a girl from Atchison, Kansas, Amelia Earhart, becomes the first woman to fly alone across the Atlantic.

## Part 5B (quiet style)

In March nineteen eighteen, at Camp Funston, an army camp in Kansas, soldiers begin falling sick with the flu.

It's one of the first recorded outbreaks of the nineteen eighteen flu pandemic, which goes on to kill tens of millions of people around the world.

## Part 5C

In nineteen twenty-one, a Wichita cook opens a little hamburger stand. White Castle. Often called the first fast-food chain.

In nineteen fifty-eight, two brothers in Wichita borrow six hundred dollars from their mother and open a small pizza place. Its sign only has room for eight letters. Pizza Hut.

And a boy who grew up in Abilene, Kansas, Dwight Eisenhower, leads the Allies to victory in Europe, then becomes president.

## Part 6: Brown

In Topeka, in the nineteen-fifties, a little girl named Linda Brown has to walk past a white school near her home and cross railroad tracks to get to her bus to a Black school.

Her father, and other Black parents, sue the Topeka school board.

In nineteen fifty-four, the Supreme Court rules on Brown versus Board of Education. Separate schools for Black and white children are unconstitutional.

## Part 6B

So how does a flat, quiet prairie become the place where the Civil War really began?

It starts with crates of rifles, labelled "Bibles".

Congress let Kansas decide for itself about slavery. So both sides poured in, with votes and with guns. The fighting in Kansas showed America that the argument over slavery could only end in war.

<short pause> And a century later, it was a little girl from Topeka whose name went on the case that ended segregation in America's schools.

<chuckle> Kansas may be flatter than a pancake. But it has never been boring.
