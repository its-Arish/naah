# The Entire History of Ohio

Narration script, written to be voiced with **Gemini TTS** and then animated. About 2,300 spoken words, so roughly 12 to 13 minutes when recorded (California ran 2,600 words to 13:50).

How to use it (the full rules are in `docs/PLAYBOOK.md` and `docs/HOOKS.md`):

- Each `##` heading is **one Gemini generation**. Paste only the lines under it, never the heading. Headings are paste breaks: never spoken, never shown. The film has no chapter cards.
- Paste the matching style line (table below) into Gemini's style box. Parts marked "quiet style" use the quiet line.
- Tags are angle brackets and only from Gemini's supported list: `<short pause>` `<long pause>` `<breath>` `<sigh>` `<exhales>` `<gasp>` `<chuckle>` `<laugh>` `<giggle>` `<phew>` `<tsk>` `<groan>` `<whispers>`. Never two tags side by side. CAPITALS stress a word; three dots add a hesitation; years and numbers are spelled out.

| Part | Style line for the style box |
| --- | --- |
| Hook | Quick, amused for the first three facts; slower and serious from "In nineteen seventy"; leaning in on the last line. |
| Part 1 | Calm, mysterious storyteller. |
| Part 2 | Frontier tension, then wry. |
| Part 3 | Hopeful and brave. |
| Part 4 | Busy, inventive, upbeat. |
| Part 5 | Excited, airborne. |
| Part 6 | Serious, then proud. |
| Part 7 | Soaring and warm, with a smile on the very last line. |
| Lettered parts | Use the style line of their number. |
| Any part marked "quiet style" | Quiet, slow and respectful. No smile in the voice. Long gaps between sentences. |

On-screen timeline labels (the only sign of a new era): Mound Builders 1000 BC to 1700 · Ohio Country 1700 to 1803 · Free State 1803 to 1865 · Inventors 1865 to 1900 · First Flight 1878 to 1910 · Industry 1910 to 1970 · Space 1962 to 1998 · Today 1970 to now.

## Hook

Seven American presidents were born in Ohio.

Ohio's flag isn't a rectangle. It's the only state flag shaped like a swallow's tail.

<chuckle> And technically, Congress didn't finish the paperwork making Ohio a state... until nineteen fifty-three. A hundred and fifty years late.

<long pause> In nineteen seventy, soldiers fired on students at Kent State University. Four were killed.

And yet, the first airplane was built in Ohio. And the first man to walk on the Moon came from Ohio.

<short pause> So how does one flat state, hundreds of miles from any ocean, launch people into the sky, and all the way to the Moon?

<whispers> It starts with a toy helicopter.

## Part 1: Mound Builders

Two thousand years ago, Ohio is a land of giant earthworks.

The Adena and Hopewell peoples build burial mounds, circles and perfect squares of earth, hundreds of them.

<gasp> At Newark, the Hopewell build a giant octagon of earth walls, joined to a perfect circle. Its corners line up with the rising and setting of the Moon, in a cycle that takes eighteen and a half years to repeat. <short pause> Today, part of it is a golf course. <chuckle> Golfers play around a two-thousand-year-old observatory.

## Part 1B

And on a ridge in southern Ohio, someone shapes the earth into a giant snake. More than thirteen hundred feet long, its body curling, its mouth open around an oval. <short pause> The Great Serpent Mound.

Nobody is completely sure who built it, or why. Its head points toward the sunset on the longest day of the year.

Later, the land is home to the Shawnee, the Miami, the Wyandot, the Lenape and others. The word "Ohio" comes from the Seneca word for "great river".

<short pause> In between, around a thousand years ago, people of the Fort Ancient culture build farming villages along the rivers. At one of them, near Dayton, they set up a tall cedar pole in the middle of the village. <chuckle> Its shadow tells them when to plant, and when to hold festivals. A giant sundial. Today it's called SunWatch.

## Part 2: Ohio Country

In the seventeen hundreds, France and Britain both want the "Ohio Country". Their fight over it helps start the French and Indian War.

After the Revolution, the new United States takes it as the Northwest Territory. And in seventeen eighty-seven, it makes a rule that shapes Ohio forever. <short pause> In the Northwest Territory, slavery is banned.

In seventeen eighty-eight, the first American settlers float down the Ohio River on a flatboat and found Marietta.

## Part 2B

But the land belongs to Native nations, and they fight for it.

<gasp> At first, they win. In seventeen ninety-one, led by the Miami war chief Little Turtle and the Shawnee leader Blue Jacket, they crush an American army. Around six hundred soldiers are killed. It's one of the worst defeats the U.S. Army ever suffered.

<short pause> Then, in seventeen ninety-four, at Fallen Timbers, an American army wins. The next year, many nations are pressured to sign away most of Ohio.

A Shawnee leader born in Ohio, Tecumseh, tries to unite all the nations, from the Great Lakes to the Gulf, to resist. <sigh> He's killed in battle in eighteen thirteen.

<short pause> His brother, a holy man called the Prophet, had preached that the nations should give up the settlers' goods, their whiskey and their ways, and go back to the old life. <sigh> After Tecumseh dies, the dream of a great Native alliance dies with him. Within a few decades, the nations of Ohio are forced west, across the Mississippi.

## Part 2C

Settlers pour in. <chuckle> And wandering among them, barefoot, wearing a tin pot for a hat, so the stories say, is a man named John Chapman. He plants apple nurseries all over the Ohio frontier, and sells the trees to settlers.

<short pause> People call him Johnny Appleseed.

## Part 2D

In eighteen-oh-three, Ohio becomes a state. The seventeenth.

<chuckle> Except... Congress never passes the final formal resolution admitting it. And nobody notices.

For a hundred and fifty years.

In nineteen fifty-three, getting ready for Ohio's hundred-and-fiftieth birthday, people discover the mistake. Ohio sends a new request for statehood to Washington. <laugh> On horseback. For old times' sake.

President Eisenhower signs it. Ohio is officially a state. Backdated to eighteen-oh-three.

## Part 2E

Ohio fills up fast. Canals are dug to link Lake Erie to the Ohio River, so farmers can ship grain north or south.

<chuckle> Cincinnati becomes the pork capital of America. So many hogs are driven through its streets to the slaughterhouses that people call it Porkopolis. Pigs wander the streets, eating the garbage.

<short pause> Two Cincinnati men, a candle maker and a soap maker, marry two sisters, and their father-in-law suggests they go into business together. Procter and Gamble.

## Part 3: Free State

Ohio is a free state. Across the river is Kentucky, a slave state. <short pause> The Ohio River is the line between slavery and freedom.

In the town of Ripley, a minister named John Rankin keeps a lantern burning in his window on the hill, so escaping people can find his house from across the river. Ohio becomes one of the busiest routes on the Underground Railroad.

<short pause> Down the hill from Rankin lives a man named John Parker. Born enslaved, he bought his own freedom, and now owns an iron foundry in Ripley. <gasp> At night, he rows across the river, into Kentucky, and leads people back. Hundreds of them. With a price on his head.

In Cincinnati, a young writer named Harriet Beecher Stowe hears stories of escapes across the frozen river. Years later, she writes Uncle Tom's Cabin.

## Part 3B

And in Oberlin, a college becomes one of the first in America to admit Black students, and the first to let women study alongside men.

<gasp> In eighteen fifty-eight, slave catchers seize a young man named John Price near Oberlin, and take him to a hotel in the next town, Wellington. Hundreds of students and townspeople surround the hotel... and rescue him. He escapes to Canada.

<chuckle> Some of the rescuers are put on trial. They become heroes.

## Part 3C

In the Civil War, Ohio sends more than three hundred thousand soldiers to the Union. And its three most famous generals, Ulysses S. Grant, William Tecumseh Sherman and Philip Sheridan, all come from Ohio.

After the war, Ohio keeps sending men to the White House. Grant. Hayes. Garfield. Harrison. McKinley. Taft. Harding. <short pause> Seven presidents born in Ohio.

<short pause> And in eighteen seventy-two, a woman born in the little town of Homer, Ohio, Victoria Woodhull, runs for president. Fifty years before women can vote nationally. <chuckle> She spends election day in jail.

<short pause> From a farm in western Ohio comes a girl named Annie Oakley, who learns to shoot to feed her family. She becomes the star of Buffalo Bill's Wild West Show. She can shoot a playing card held edge-on. From thirty paces.

## Part 4: Inventors

Ohio becomes a state of tinkerers.

Thomas Edison is born in Milan, Ohio. In Cleveland, John D. Rockefeller starts Standard Oil and becomes the richest man in America.

<short pause> He starts as a bookkeeper. He buys refinery after refinery, until Standard Oil controls about nine out of every ten barrels of oil refined in America. <tsk> In nineteen eleven, the Supreme Court breaks it into more than thirty pieces. <chuckle> Rockefeller gets even richer. He owns shares in all of them.

Akron makes so many tyres it's called the Rubber Capital of the World. <chuckle> Goodyear even builds giant blimps there.

In eighteen sixty-nine, Cincinnati starts the first all-professional baseball team. The Red Stockings. They don't lose a game all season.

## Part 4B

And in Dayton, a saloon keeper invents the cash register, to stop his bartenders stealing. The company that sells it, National Cash Register, becomes a factory of inventors.

One of them, Charles Kettering, invents the electric starter for cars. <laugh> Before that, you had to start a car by cranking a handle at the front. It could kick back and break your arm.

In Cleveland, in nineteen fourteen, the first electric traffic light in America goes up.

## Part 5: First Flight

<short pause> Now, the toy.

In eighteen seventy-eight, a bishop named Milton Wright comes home with a present for his two youngest sons, Wilbur and Orville. A little helicopter made of cork, bamboo and paper, powered by a twisted rubber band.

<chuckle> The boys play with it until it breaks. Then they build their own.

<short pause> They never forget it.

<short pause> And one of Orville's high-school classmates in Dayton is a young Black poet named Paul Laurence Dunbar. The Wright brothers even print his first newspaper for him. <short pause> He becomes one of the first African American poets famous across the country.

## Part 5B

They grow up. They open a bicycle shop in Dayton. Bicycles teach them about balance, steering and light, strong machines.

<short pause> Their sister, Katharine, a teacher, helps run the business, writes their letters, and later travels with them in Europe, where she becomes as famous as they are.

And they start reading everything about flying. They study birds. They build gliders. <gasp> They build their own wind tunnel out of a wooden box and a fan, and test more than two hundred tiny wing shapes. They even build their own engine.

On December seventeenth, nineteen-oh-three, at Kitty Hawk, North Carolina, chosen for its steady winds, Orville flies for twelve seconds. The first powered airplane flight.

<short pause> The plane was built in Dayton, Ohio.

Then they go home, and in a cow pasture outside Dayton called Huffman Prairie, they learn to turn, circle and stay up for more than half an hour. <chuckle> Passengers on the passing trolley stare out of the windows. The real airplane is born in Ohio.

## Part 5C (quiet style)

In March nineteen thirteen, after days of rain, the rivers flood Dayton and much of Ohio.

<breath> Hundreds of people are killed. The Wright brothers' house floods too. <short pause> Their photographs of the first flight survive in a shed.

## Part 6: Industry

In the twentieth century, Ohio's cities make steel, cars, tyres and glass. Toledo is called the Glass City.

<chuckle> Ohio got Toledo, remember, by winning a war against Michigan in the eighteen-thirties. The only casualty was a man stabbed with a penknife.

<short pause> In nineteen twenty, in a car showroom in Canton, a group of team owners meet to form a professional football league. It becomes the NFL. That's why the Pro Football Hall of Fame is in Canton.

## Part 6B

In nineteen thirty-five, at a college track meet in Michigan, an Ohio State student named Jesse Owens, the grandson of enslaved people, sets three world records and ties a fourth. <gasp> In about forty-five minutes.

The next year, in Berlin, in front of Adolf Hitler, he wins four Olympic gold medals.

## Part 6C

In nineteen sixty-nine, the Cuyahoga River in Cleveland is so polluted with oil and chemicals that it catches fire. <tsk> Again. It had burned many times before. This time, the photos shock America. Soon, the country gets the Environmental Protection Agency and the Clean Water Act.

<sigh> Like many Ohio cities, Cleveland loses factories, jobs and half its people. In nineteen seventy-eight, it becomes the first big American city since the Great Depression to default on its debts. <short pause> Today, the river that burned has fish in it again.

In Cleveland, a radio DJ named Alan Freed helps make the phrase "rock and roll" famous. That's why the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame is there today.

<short pause> And in the nineteen-thirties, two Cleveland teenagers, Jerry Siegel and Joe Shuster, dream up a superhero from another planet, who wears glasses and works at a newspaper. <gasp> Superman. They sell the rights for a hundred and thirty dollars.

## Part 6D (quiet style)

On May fourth, nineteen seventy, students at Kent State University protest the Vietnam War.

<long pause> National Guard soldiers open fire.

Four students are killed. Nine are wounded.

## Part 7: Space

<short pause> Meanwhile, the toy has kept flying.

Near Dayton, the Air Force builds one of its biggest bases, right on the field where the Wright brothers once flew. Wright-Patterson.

In nineteen sixty-two, an Ohio Marine pilot named John Glenn becomes the first American to orbit the Earth.

And a boy from Wapakoneta, Ohio, gets his pilot's licence at sixteen. <gasp> Before his driver's licence. His name is Neil Armstrong.

On July twentieth, nineteen sixty-nine, he steps onto the Moon.

<chuckle> And in nineteen ninety-eight, John Glenn flies into space again. At seventy-seven years old.

<short pause> Ohio has produced around two dozen astronauts. More than almost any other state.

<chuckle> And Ohio is famous for something else. Picking presidents. For more than a century, whoever won Ohio almost always won the White House. Candidates spent so much time there that people said: as Ohio goes, so goes the nation.

## Part 7B

Ohio also gives the world one of its greatest writers, Toni Morrison, from Lorain, who wins the Nobel Prize in nineteen ninety-three.

<laugh> And in twenty sixteen, LeBron James, from Akron, leads the Cleveland Cavaliers back from three games to one down, to win Cleveland's first major championship in fifty-two years.

<chuckle> And every summer, at Cedar Point, on Lake Erie, people line up to ride some of the tallest, fastest roller coasters in the world.

## Part 7C

So how does one flat state, hundreds of miles from any ocean, launch people into the sky, and all the way to the Moon?

<short pause> It starts with a toy helicopter.

A father brought it home. Two boys never stopped thinking about it. They built the first airplane in Dayton. Their hometown became a home for flying, and a whole state grew up looking up.

<long pause> Sixty-six years after Kitty Hawk, an Ohio boy stood on the Moon.

<short pause> And he carried a small piece of the Wright brothers' first airplane with him.

<chuckle> Not bad for a state that technically wasn't a state yet.
