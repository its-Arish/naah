## Hook

Seven American presidents were born in Ohio.

Ohio's flag isn't a rectangle. It's the only state flag shaped like a swallow's tail.

And technically, Congress didn't finish the paperwork making Ohio a state... until nineteen fifty-three. <chuckle> A hundred and fifty years late.

<long pause> In nineteen seventy, soldiers fired on students at Kent State University. Four were killed.

And yet, the first airplane was built in Ohio. And the first man to walk on the Moon came from Ohio.

<short pause> So how does one flat state, hundreds of miles from any ocean, launch people into the sky, and all the way to the Moon?

<whispers> It starts with a toy helicopter.

## Part 1: Mound Builders

Two thousand years ago, Ohio is a land of giant earthworks.

The Adena and Hopewell peoples build burial mounds, circles and perfect squares of earth, some lined up with the rising and setting of the Moon.

And on a ridge in southern Ohio, someone shapes the earth into a giant snake. More than thirteen hundred feet long, its body curling, its mouth open around an oval. The Great Serpent Mound.

Nobody is completely sure who built it, or why.

Later, the land is home to the Shawnee, the Miami, the Wyandot, the Lenape and others. The word "Ohio" comes from the Seneca word for "great river".

## Part 2: Ohio Country

In the seventeen hundreds, France and Britain both want the "Ohio Country". Their fight over it helps start the French and Indian War.

After the Revolution, the new United States takes it as the Northwest Territory. And in seventeen eighty-seven, it makes a rule that shapes Ohio forever. In the Northwest Territory, slavery is banned.

In seventeen eighty-eight, the first American settlers float down the Ohio River on a flatboat and found Marietta.

## Part 2B

But the land belongs to Native nations, and they fight for it. At first, they win. In seventeen ninety-one, they crush an American army in one of the worst defeats the U.S. Army ever suffered.

Then, in seventeen ninety-four, at Fallen Timbers, an American army wins. The next year, many nations are pressured to sign away most of Ohio.

A Shawnee leader born in Ohio, Tecumseh, tries to unite the nations to resist. He's killed in battle in eighteen thirteen.

## Part 2C

In eighteen-oh-three, Ohio becomes a state. The seventeenth.

Except... Congress never passes the final formal resolution admitting it. And nobody notices.

For a hundred and fifty years.

In nineteen fifty-three, getting ready for Ohio's hundred-and-fiftieth birthday, people discover the mistake. Ohio sends a new request for statehood to Washington. On horseback. <chuckle> For old times' sake.

President Eisenhower signs it. Ohio is officially a state. Backdated to eighteen-oh-three.

## Part 3: Free State

Ohio is a free state. Across the river is Kentucky, a slave state. The Ohio River is the line between slavery and freedom.

In the town of Ripley, a minister named John Rankin keeps a lantern burning in his window on the hill, so escaping people can find his house from across the river. Ohio becomes a busy route on the Underground Railroad.

In Cincinnati, a young writer named Harriet Beecher Stowe hears stories of escapes across the frozen river. Years later, she writes Uncle Tom's Cabin.

And in Oberlin, a college becomes one of the first in America to admit Black students, and the first to let women study with men.

## Part 3B

In the Civil War, Ohio sends more than three hundred thousand soldiers to the Union. And its three most famous generals, Ulysses S. Grant, William Tecumseh Sherman and Philip Sheridan, all come from Ohio.

After the war, Ohio keeps sending men to the White House. Grant. Hayes. Garfield. Harrison. McKinley. Taft. Harding. Seven presidents born in Ohio.

## Part 4: Inventors

Ohio becomes a state of tinkerers.

Thomas Edison is born in Milan, Ohio. In Cleveland, John D. Rockefeller starts Standard Oil and becomes the richest man in America. Akron makes so many tyres it's called the Rubber Capital of the World.

And in Dayton, a saloon keeper invents the cash register, to stop his bartenders stealing. The company that sells it becomes a factory of inventors.

## Part 5: First Flight

Now, the toy.

In eighteen seventy-eight, a bishop named Milton Wright comes home to Ohio with a present for his two youngest sons, Wilbur and Orville. A little helicopter made of cork, bamboo and paper, powered by a twisted rubber band.

The boys play with it until it breaks. Then they build their own.

<short pause> They never forget it.

## Part 5B

They grow up. They open a bicycle shop in Dayton. Bicycles teach them about balance, steering and light, strong machines.

And they start reading everything about flying. They study birds. They build gliders. They build their own wind tunnel, and their own engine.

On December seventeenth, nineteen-oh-three, at Kitty Hawk, North Carolina, chosen for its steady winds, Orville flies for twelve seconds. The first powered airplane flight.

The plane was built in Dayton, Ohio.

Then they go home, and in a cow pasture outside Dayton called Huffman Prairie, they learn to turn, circle and stay up for more than half an hour. The real airplane is born in Ohio.

## Part 5C (quiet style)

In March nineteen thirteen, after days of rain, the rivers flood Dayton and much of Ohio.

Hundreds of people are killed. The Wright brothers' house floods too. Their photographs of the first flight survive in a shed.

## Part 6: Industry

In the twentieth century, Ohio's cities make steel, cars, tyres and glass.

In nineteen sixty-nine, the Cuyahoga River in Cleveland is so polluted with oil and chemicals that it catches fire. Again. It had burned many times before. This time, the photos shock America. Soon, the country gets the Environmental Protection Agency and the Clean Water Act.

In Cleveland, a radio DJ named Alan Freed helps make the phrase "rock and roll" famous. That's why the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame is there today.

## Part 6B (quiet style)

On May fourth, nineteen seventy, students at Kent State University protest the Vietnam War.

National Guard soldiers open fire.

Four students are killed. Nine are wounded.

## Part 7: Space

Meanwhile, the toy has kept flying.

Near Dayton, the Air Force builds one of its biggest bases, at the field where the Wright brothers once flew. Wright-Patterson.

In nineteen sixty-two, an Ohio Marine pilot named John Glenn becomes the first American to orbit the Earth.

And a boy from Wapakoneta, Ohio, gets his pilot's licence at sixteen. Before his driver's licence. His name is Neil Armstrong.

On July twentieth, nineteen sixty-nine, he steps onto the Moon.

## Part 7B

So how does one flat state, hundreds of miles from any ocean, launch people into the sky, and all the way to the Moon?

It starts with a toy helicopter.

A father brought it home to Dayton. Two boys never stopped thinking about it. They built the first airplane there. Their hometown became a home for flying, and a whole state grew up looking up.

Sixty-six years after Kitty Hawk, an Ohio boy stood on the Moon.

<short pause> And he carried a small piece of the Wright brothers' first airplane with him.

<chuckle> Not bad for a state that technically wasn't a state yet.
