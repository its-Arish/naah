## Hook

Corn Flakes? <short pause> Invented in Michigan, at a health spa, by a doctor who thought bland food made people behave better.

In Michigan, you're never more than about six miles from a lake or a stream.

Michigan once fought a war with Ohio, over the city of Toledo. Michigan lost... and got a whole extra peninsula as a consolation prize.

Detroit is the city that put the whole world on wheels.

<long pause> And in two thousand thirteen, Detroit became the biggest city in American history to go bankrupt.

<short pause> So how does the city that put the world on wheels end up broke?

<whispers> It starts with a farm boy who hated farming.

## Part 1: Three Fires

Michigan is two peninsulas surrounded by four of the five Great Lakes. The lower one is shaped like a mitten. <chuckle> People here point to their own hand to show you where they live.

For thousands of years, it's home to the Anishinaabe. Three nations, the Ojibwe, the Odawa and the Potawatomi, call themselves the Three Fires. They travel the lakes in birchbark canoes, fish, gather wild rice, and tap maple trees for sugar.

## Part 2: French and British

In the sixteen hundreds, French fur traders and priests arrive. They paddle the lakes, trading for beaver skins.

In seventeen-oh-one, a French officer named Antoine de la Mothe Cadillac builds a fort on the narrow river between two lakes. The French word for "strait" is "détroit".

Detroit.

Remember his name. Cadillac.

## Part 2B

In seventeen sixty-three, after Britain takes over, an Odawa leader named Pontiac leads a war against British forts around the Great Lakes. He besieges Detroit for months.

Remember his name too. Pontiac.

Even after the American Revolution, the British keep Detroit until seventeen ninety-six. Then, in the War of eighteen twelve, the American general in Detroit surrenders it without a fight. The Americans have to win it back.

## Part 2C

In the eighteen-thirties, settlers pour in through the new Erie Canal. Michigan wants to be a state. But there's a problem. A small strip of land, with the port of Toledo, that both Michigan and Ohio claim.

In eighteen thirty-five, both sides send militias. The Toledo War.

<chuckle> The only casualty is a Michigan sheriff's deputy, stabbed with a penknife by an Ohio man. He survives.

Congress gives Toledo to Ohio. And to make up for it, Michigan gets the western Upper Peninsula. Back then, people thought it was a frozen wasteland.

In January eighteen thirty-seven, Michigan becomes the twenty-sixth state.

## Part 3: Copper and Lumber

And then, the "worthless" Upper Peninsula turns out to be full of copper. And iron. Lots of it.

Miners from Cornwall, Finland and Italy come to dig it. In eighteen fifty-five, the Soo Locks open, so ships can carry the ore around the rapids between Lake Superior and the other lakes.

Michigan's forests are cut down too. For a while, Michigan produces more lumber than any other state.

<chuckle> Ohio got Toledo. Michigan got a mountain of metal.

## Part 3B

In Battle Creek, a doctor named John Harvey Kellogg runs a famous health spa. He believes in plain, bland food. In the eighteen nineties, he and his brother Will accidentally leave some boiled wheat sitting out. They roll it flat anyway, and it comes out as flakes.

Later, they try corn. Will starts a company. Corn Flakes. And Battle Creek becomes Cereal City.

## Part 4: Motor City

Now, back to the farm boy.

Henry Ford grows up on a farm near Detroit in the eighteen sixties and seventies. He hates farm work. He loves machines. He takes apart watches and fixes them for his neighbours.

He becomes an engineer, and builds his first car in a shed in eighteen ninety-six.

In nineteen-oh-eight, he launches the Model T. Simple, tough, and cheap enough for ordinary families.

And in nineteen thirteen, he introduces the moving assembly line. Instead of workers walking around the car, the car moves past the workers. Building a Model T goes from about twelve hours to about an hour and a half.

## Part 4B

The next year, Ford does something shocking. He doubles his workers' pay, to five dollars a day.

People rush to Detroit from everywhere. From Poland, Italy, Hungary, Lebanon, Mexico. And from the American South, especially Black families escaping Jim Crow.

Other car companies grow up around Ford. General Motors. Chrysler. And the new car brands borrow Detroit's history. Cadillac, named after the city's founder. Pontiac, named after the Odawa war leader.

Detroit grows from about three hundred thousand people in nineteen hundred to almost two million by nineteen fifty. The fifth biggest city in America.

## Part 4C

In the Second World War, the car factories switch to tanks, trucks and bombers. At Willow Run, Ford's giant plant builds a B-twenty-four bomber about once an hour.

They call Detroit the "Arsenal of Democracy".

## Part 5: Motown

In nineteen fifty-nine, a former car-factory worker named Berry Gordy borrows eight hundred dollars from his family and starts a record company in a small house in Detroit.

He runs it like an assembly line for hit songs.

Motown. The Supremes, Stevie Wonder, Marvin Gaye, the Temptations, the Jackson Five.

## Part 5B

But cars do something else to Detroit.

Highways are built straight through the city, often through Black neighbourhoods. Families who can afford a car move out to new suburbs, and drive in to work. Factories move out too.

Detroit is the city that built the cars. And the cars carried people away from it.

## Part 5C (quiet style)

In July nineteen sixty-seven, after a police raid, Detroit erupts in five days of violence and fires.

Forty-three people are killed. Thousands of buildings are damaged.

Even more people leave.

## Part 5D

In the nineteen seventies, oil prices jump. Small, cheap Japanese cars start to sell. Detroit's car companies lose ground. Factories close. Jobs disappear.

The city shrinks. And shrinks.

## Part 5E (quiet style)

By two thousand thirteen, Detroit has lost more than half its people. Tens of thousands of houses stand empty. It owes around eighteen billion dollars.

In July, it files for bankruptcy. The largest city bankruptcy in American history.

## Part 5F (quiet style)

A year later, in two thousand fourteen, to save money, the city of Flint switches its water supply. The new water corrodes old lead pipes.

Lead gets into the drinking water of a whole city, and its children.

## Part 6: Today

But Detroit doesn't disappear.

It comes out of bankruptcy in about a year and a half. Downtown fills up again. Old factories become offices and lofts. And car companies race to build electric cars.

## Part 6B

So how does the city that put the world on wheels end up broke?

It starts with a farm boy who hated farming.

Henry Ford wanted to free people from the farm. His cheap cars and five-dollar wages built Detroit into a giant. But Detroit put almost everything into one industry. And the cars it built let people leave. Out to the suburbs. Out of the city.

<short pause> The car built Detroit. And the car drove away from it.

<chuckle> Meanwhile, on Mackinac Island, in the middle of the lakes, cars have been banned since eighteen ninety-eight. They still get around by horse and bicycle.
