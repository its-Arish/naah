## Hook

Basketball? <short pause> Invented in Massachusetts. Volleyball? Also Massachusetts. Four years later, just up the road.

The chocolate chip cookie was invented at an inn in Massachusetts.

In nineteen nineteen, a giant tank burst in Boston and a wave of molasses swept through the streets.

<long pause> And in sixteen ninety-two, Massachusetts hanged its own neighbours for witchcraft.

<short pause> So how does a colony that hanged people for being witches become the place the whole world sends its smartest kids?

<whispers> It starts with a law about the Devil.

## Part 1: First Peoples

Long before anyone in Europe had heard of it, this land belonged to the Wampanoag, the Massachusett, the Nipmuc, the Pocumtuc and other peoples.

They fish the coast, hunt the forests, and grow corn, beans and squash together in the same fields. The Three Sisters.

The state's name comes from the Massachusett people. It means something like "at the great hill".

## Part 1B (quiet style)

Then, between sixteen sixteen and sixteen nineteen, a terrible sickness spreads along the coast, probably brought by European sailors and traders.

Whole villages die. In some places, almost nobody is left.

One of the empty villages is called Patuxet.

## Part 2: Pilgrims

In sixteen twenty, a small ship called the Mayflower sails from England. On board are about a hundred passengers, many of them religious separatists who want to worship their own way. We call them the Pilgrims.

They're supposed to land near the Hudson River. But storms and dangerous shoals push them north, to Cape Cod. They're outside their legal area. So before they even get off the ship, the men sign an agreement to govern themselves. The Mayflower Compact.

They settle on the site of the empty village of Patuxet. They call it Plymouth.

The first winter is brutal. About half of them die.

Then a man walks into their settlement and says, in English, "Welcome."

His name is Samoset. Soon after, he brings a man called Tisquantum. Squanto. Squanto had been kidnapped years earlier, taken to Europe, and found his way home... only to discover his whole village, Patuxet, had died.

He teaches the Pilgrims how to plant corn. And the Wampanoag leader Massasoit makes an alliance with them.

In the autumn of sixteen twenty-one, they share a harvest feast that lasts three days. About fifty Pilgrims. About ninety Wampanoag men.

Today, we call it the first Thanksgiving.

## Part 2B

Ten years later, a much bigger wave arrives. The Puritans. Thousands of them.

Their leader, John Winthrop, tells them they will be like a "city upon a hill". The whole world will be watching.

They found Boston in sixteen thirty. And they are obsessed with one thing. Reading the Bible.

So, in sixteen thirty-six, they found a college to train ministers. It's named after a young minister who leaves it his books. John Harvard.

And in sixteen forty-seven, they pass a law that begins, and this is real, "It being one chief project of that old deluder, Satan, to keep men from the knowledge of the Scriptures..."

The Devil, they reasoned, wants people ignorant. So every town with fifty families must hire a teacher. Every town with a hundred families must start a grammar school.

The Old Deluder Satan Act. One of the very first public school laws in America.

## Part 2C (quiet style)

But the colony keeps growing, and taking land.

In sixteen seventy-five, Massasoit's son Metacom, whom the English call King Philip, leads a war to drive the settlers out.

For its size, King Philip's War is one of the deadliest wars in American history. Thousands die. Native nations are shattered, and many survivors are sold into slavery.

## Part 2D (quiet style)

And in sixteen ninety-two, in the village of Salem, a few girls begin having strange fits.

They say they are being tortured by witches. They start naming names.

Within months, more than a hundred and fifty people are accused. Nineteen are hanged. One old man, Giles Corey, is crushed to death under heavy stones for refusing to plead.

A few years later, one of the judges stands up in church and publicly apologises. Some of the accusers later say they were wrong. It is far too late.

## Part 3: Revolution

Fast forward to the seventeen sixties.

Britain has just won a huge, expensive war. And it decides the colonies should help pay for it. Taxes on paper. Taxes on glass. Taxes on tea.

And Boston... Boston does not like that.

In seventeen seventy, a crowd throws snowballs, sticks and insults at British soldiers. The soldiers fire. Five people are killed, including a sailor of African and Native descent, Crispus Attucks. It's called the Boston Massacre.

And the lawyer who defends the soldiers in court? John Adams. A future president.

In December seventeen seventy-three, men dressed as Mohawk warriors board three ships and dump three hundred and forty-two chests of tea into Boston Harbor.

The Boston Tea Party. <chuckle> Probably the most expensive cup of tea in history.

## Part 3B

Britain shuts Boston's port. Everyone prepares to fight.

On the night of April eighteenth, seventeen seventy-five, Paul Revere and William Dawes ride out to warn that British troops are coming. No, he almost certainly didn't shout "The British are coming!" Most people back then still thought of themselves as British.

The next morning, at Lexington and Concord, someone fires. The shot heard round the world.

The American Revolution begins in Massachusetts.

Two months later, the Battle of Bunker Hill. Which was actually fought mostly on Breed's Hill. <chuckle>

## Part 3C

In seventeen eighty, John Adams writes the Massachusetts Constitution. It's still in use today, the oldest written constitution still working anywhere in the world.

It says all men are born free and equal.

So an enslaved man named Quock Walker goes to court and says: well, then, that includes me.

He wins. By seventeen eighty-three, slavery in Massachusetts is effectively over.

## Part 4: Mills and Whales

In the eighteen hundreds, Massachusetts changes how America works.

In Lowell, giant water-powered mills spin cotton into cloth. Many of the workers are young women from farm families, the Lowell mill girls. They go on strike for better pay, and publish their own magazine.

On the coast, New Bedford and Nantucket send whaling ships around the world for years at a time. Whale oil lights the lamps of America. Herman Melville sails on a whaler, and writes Moby-Dick.

And Boston becomes the centre of the fight against slavery. William Lloyd Garrison prints The Liberator. Frederick Douglass, who escaped slavery, gives his first big speech in Massachusetts.

In the Civil War, the Fifty-fourth Massachusetts becomes one of the first official Black regiments in the Union Army.

## Part 4B

And then, the inventions.

In eighteen seventy-six, in Boston, Alexander Graham Bell speaks the first words through a telephone.

In eighteen ninety-one, in Springfield, a teacher named James Naismith needs a game his students can play indoors in winter. He nails two peach baskets to a balcony.

Basketball.

Four years later, in Holyoke, William Morgan wants something gentler. Volleyball.

In nineteen thirty-eight, at the Toll House Inn in Whitman, Ruth Wakefield adds chopped chocolate to her cookie dough.

And in nineteen fifty, a man in Quincy opens a doughnut shop. Today you know it as Dunkin'.

## Part 4C (quiet style)

On January fifteenth, nineteen nineteen, in Boston's North End, a giant tank holding more than two million gallons of molasses bursts open.

A wave of syrup, taller than a house, rushes through the streets.

Twenty-one people die. Some say that on hot days, the neighbourhood smelled of molasses for decades.

## Part 5: Modern

The twentieth century belongs to a Massachusetts family.

John F. Kennedy, from Brookline, becomes president in nineteen sixty-one.

And Boston becomes a brain city. Harvard, MIT, and dozens more colleges. The first computers, radar research in the Second World War, and later biotech labs that make medicines and vaccines.

## Part 5B

In two thousand four, Massachusetts becomes the first state in America to allow same-sex couples to marry.

The same year, the Boston Red Sox win the World Series, for the first time since nineteen eighteen. Eighty-six years. Fans called it "the Curse of the Bambino", after they sold Babe Ruth to the Yankees.

## Part 5C (quiet style)

In two thousand thirteen, two bombs explode near the finish line of the Boston Marathon. Three people are killed and hundreds are hurt.

The city's answer becomes two words. Boston Strong.

## Part 5D

So how does a colony that hanged people for being witches become the place the whole world sends its smartest kids?

It starts with a law about the Devil.

The Puritans were so afraid of Satan that they made every town teach its children to read. The same fear that hanged nineteen people in Salem also built some of the first public schools in America, and its oldest college.

Out of the schools came the readers. Out of the readers came the arguers. And out of the arguers came a revolution, the fight against slavery, and a city full of universities.

<short pause> Massachusetts has always run on ideas. Some of them were terrible. Some of them changed the world.

<chuckle> And one of them was putting chocolate in a cookie.
