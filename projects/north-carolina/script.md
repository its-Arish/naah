## Hook

Pepsi? <short pause> Invented in North Carolina. Krispy Kreme? North Carolina.

North Carolina's licence plates say "First in Flight". But the Wright brothers were from Ohio.

Blackbeard, the most famous pirate who ever lived, died off North Carolina's coast. And his head was hung from a ship.

<long pause> And in fifteen eighty-seven, more than a hundred English men, women and children landed here. Three years later, every single one of them was gone. Including the first English baby born in America.

<short pause> So what happened to the Lost Colony?

<whispers> It starts with one word, carved into a post.

## Part 1: The Lost Colony

North Carolina's coast is a thin line of sandy islands, the Outer Banks, with shallow water and shifting sandbars behind them. Hundreds of ships have sunk here. Sailors call it the Graveyard of the Atlantic.

The land behind it belongs to many nations. The Cherokee in the mountains. The Tuscarora, the Catawba, and on the coast, Algonquian-speaking peoples like the Secotan and the Croatoan.

## Part 1B

In fifteen eighty-five, Sir Walter Raleigh sends English soldiers to Roanoke Island. It goes badly. They fight with the local people and go home.

So in fifteen eighty-seven, he tries again. This time with families. A hundred and fifteen men, women and children, led by an artist named John White.

A few weeks after they land, White's daughter has a baby girl. They name her Virginia. Virginia Dare. The first English child born in the Americas.

The colony needs supplies. So John White sails back to England to get them. He leaves behind his daughter, and his newborn granddaughter.

## Part 1C

He can't come back. England is at war with Spain. The Spanish Armada is coming. Every ship is needed.

It takes him three years.

In August fifteen-ninety, John White finally returns to Roanoke.

The houses are gone. Taken down. There's no sign of a fight. No bodies. No graves.

Just three letters carved into a tree. C, R, O.

And on a post at the entrance to the fort, one whole word.

CROATOAN.

## Part 1D

Before he left, White had told the colonists: if you leave, carve where you went. And if you're in danger, carve a cross.

There's no cross.

Croatoan is the name of an island to the south, now part of Hatteras, and of the friendly people who live there.

White wants to sail there. But a storm hits. His crew refuses. And he sails back to England.

<short pause> He never sees his family again.

Hold on to that word. Croatoan. We'll come back to it.

## Part 2: Pirates and Planters

Over the next century, English settlers move down from Virginia into the land they call Carolina, after King Charles.

## Part 2B (quiet style)

As settlers push onto their land, the Tuscarora fight back in seventeen eleven. They lose. Many are killed or sold into slavery. Most survivors move north, to join the Iroquois in New York.

## Part 2C

And the shallow inlets of the Outer Banks are perfect for hiding pirates.

The most famous is Edward Teach. Blackbeard. He ties smoking fuses under his hat to look terrifying. He blockades Charleston with his fleet. Then he settles in North Carolina, and some say the governor protects him.

In seventeen eighteen, at Ocracoke Inlet, a Royal Navy lieutenant from Virginia hunts him down. Blackbeard is shot and stabbed many times before he falls.

<chuckle> They hang his head from the bowsprit of the ship. Not exactly a quiet retirement.

## Part 3: Rip Van Winkle

North Carolina is a poor, stubborn colony of small farms, pine forests and tar. Its pines make tar and pitch for the ships of the Royal Navy. That's one story of where the nickname "Tar Heels" comes from.

In April seventeen seventy-six, North Carolina becomes the first colony to officially tell its delegates in Congress to vote for independence. The Halifax Resolves.

But after the war, it refuses to join the new United States until the Constitution gets a Bill of Rights. It finally joins in seventeen eighty-nine. The twelfth state.

## Part 3B

In seventeen ninety-nine, a twelve-year-old boy named Conrad Reed finds a heavy yellow rock in a creek on his family's farm.

The family uses it as a doorstop. For three years.

<chuckle> Then a jeweller tells them it's gold. Seventeen pounds of it. They sell it for three dollars and fifty cents. It was worth about a thousand times that.

It's the first documented gold find in the United States. North Carolina has America's first gold rush.

## Part 3C

But North Carolina is so slow to change that people call it the "Rip Van Winkle State". Asleep, while its neighbours get rich.

And it's a slave state. By eighteen sixty, about a third of its people are enslaved.

## Part 3D (quiet style)

In the eighteen-thirties, most Cherokee are forced out of North Carolina on the Trail of Tears.

But some hide in the mountains. And they stay. Their descendants are the Eastern Band of Cherokee, who still live in the North Carolina mountains today.

## Part 4: Civil War

In May eighteen sixty-one, North Carolina is one of the last states to join the Confederacy.

## Part 4B (quiet style)

But it sends more soldiers than almost any Confederate state. And it loses more of them. Around forty thousand North Carolinians die in the war.

## Part 4C (quiet style)

After the war, in eighteen ninety-eight, Wilmington has an elected government that includes Black and white leaders.

A mob of armed white men overthrows it. They burn a Black newspaper, kill many Black residents, and force elected officials out of town.

It's the only successful coup d'état on American soil.

## Part 5: First in Flight

And now, the Ohio brothers.

Wilbur and Orville Wright need a place with strong, steady winds and soft sand to land on. They write to the weather bureau. The answer is Kitty Hawk, on North Carolina's Outer Banks.

On December seventeenth, nineteen-oh-three, at Kill Devil Hills, the first powered airplane flies. For twelve seconds.

<chuckle> Ohio built the plane. North Carolina gave it the sky. Both states still argue about it.

## Part 5B

In eighteen ninety-three, in New Bern, a pharmacist named Caleb Bradham mixes a soda he calls "Brad's Drink". Five years later, he renames it. Pepsi-Cola.

In the mountains, George Vanderbilt builds a French-style castle with two hundred and fifty rooms. The Biltmore. Still the largest private home in America.

Tobacco and textiles make the Piedmont rich. Winston-Salem makes cigarettes. And in nineteen thirty-seven, a man opens a doughnut shop there. Krispy Kreme.

## Part 6: Sit-ins and Science

On February first, nineteen sixty, four Black college students in Greensboro sit down at a whites-only lunch counter in Woolworth's. They order coffee. They are refused. They stay.

The next day, they come back with more students. Within weeks, sit-ins spread across the South.

## Part 6B

In nineteen fifty-nine, North Carolina builds the Research Triangle Park, between three universities. As tobacco and textile jobs fade, it fills with labs and tech companies.

Charlotte becomes one of the biggest banking cities in America.

And a kid from Wilmington, cut from his high-school varsity team, becomes the greatest basketball player of all time. Michael Jordan.

## Part 6C (quiet style)

In September two thousand twenty-four, Hurricane Helene's floods tear through the mountains of western North Carolina.

More than a hundred people in the state are killed.

## Part 6D

So what happened to the Lost Colony?

It starts with one word, carved into a post. Croatoan.

For four hundred years, it was a mystery. But in the last few years, archaeologists digging on Hatteras Island, the old Croatoan, have found English objects from the fifteen hundreds. Gun parts. A slate writing tablet. A sword handle. Mixed in with the belongings of the Croatoan people.

And inland, at a site on the Albemarle Sound, they've found English pottery too.

Many researchers now think the colonists didn't vanish. They split up. And they went to live with their Native neighbours, just like the carving said.

<short pause> The answer may have been written on that post the whole time.

<chuckle> It just took four hundred years to dig it up.
