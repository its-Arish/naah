# The Entire History of North Carolina

Narration script, written to be voiced with **Gemini TTS** and then animated. About 2,700 spoken words, so roughly 14 to 15 minutes when recorded (California ran 2,600 words to 13:50).

How to use it (the full rules are in `docs/PLAYBOOK.md` and `docs/HOOKS.md`):

- Each `##` heading is **one Gemini generation**. Paste only the lines under it, never the heading. Headings are paste breaks: never spoken, never shown. The film has no chapter cards.
- Paste the matching style line (table below) into Gemini's style box. Parts marked "quiet style" use the quiet line.
- Tags are angle brackets and only from Gemini's supported list: `<short pause>` `<long pause>` `<breath>` `<sigh>` `<exhales>` `<gasp>` `<chuckle>` `<laugh>` `<giggle>` `<phew>` `<tsk>` `<groan>` `<whispers>`. Never two tags side by side. CAPITALS stress a word; three dots add a hesitation; years and numbers are spelled out.

| Part | Style line for the style box |
| --- | --- |
| Hook | Quick, amused for the first three facts; slower and serious from "And in fifteen eighty-seven"; leaning in, almost whispering, on the last line. |
| Part 1 | Mysterious, hushed storyteller, like a campfire ghost story. |
| Part 2 | Swashbuckling and playful, then serious. |
| Part 3 | Stubborn, proud, a little wry. |
| Part 4 | Heavy and steady. |
| Part 5 | Airborne, bright, full of wonder. |
| Part 6 | Determined and warm, modern; mysterious again for the final answer, with a smile on the very last line. |
| Lettered parts | Use the style line of their number. |
| Any part marked "quiet style" | Quiet, slow and respectful. No smile in the voice. Long gaps between sentences. |

On-screen timeline labels (the only sign of a new era): First Peoples 10,000 BC to 1585 · Lost Colony 1585 to 1590 · Pirates and Planters 1650 to 1775 · Rip Van Winkle 1776 to 1860 · Civil War 1861 to 1865 · First in Flight 1865 to 1945 · Sit-ins and Science 1945 to 2000 · Today 2000 to now.

## Hook

Pepsi? <short pause> Invented in North Carolina. Krispy Kreme? North Carolina. The first public university in America to open its doors? North Carolina.

<chuckle> North Carolina's licence plates say "First in Flight". But the Wright brothers were from Ohio.

Blackbeard, the most famous pirate who ever lived, died off North Carolina's coast. And his head was hung from the front of a ship.

<long pause> And in fifteen eighty-seven, more than a hundred English men, women and children landed here. Three years later, every single one of them was gone. Including the first English baby born in America.

<short pause> So what happened to the Lost Colony?

<whispers> It starts with one word, carved into a post.

## Part 1: The Lost Colony

North Carolina's coast is a thin line of sandy islands, the Outer Banks, about two hundred miles long. Behind them, shallow sounds. In front of them, shifting sandbars and storms.

<gasp> Hundreds and hundreds of ships have wrecked here. Sailors call it the Graveyard of the Atlantic.

The land behind the islands belongs to many nations. The Cherokee in the Appalachian mountains. The Catawba and the Tuscarora in the hills and forests. And on the coast, Algonquian-speaking peoples, like the Secotan and the Croatoan, living in towns of bark-covered longhouses, with fields of corn, beans and squash, and fish traps in the sounds.

<short pause> We know what their towns looked like, because an English artist painted them. His name was John White. And he's the man at the centre of this mystery.

## Part 1B

In fifteen eighty-five, Sir Walter Raleigh sends a hundred English soldiers to Roanoke Island, to build England's first colony in America. They call the whole region "Virginia", after Queen Elizabeth, the Virgin Queen.

<tsk> It goes badly. A silver cup goes missing from the English camp. They blame a nearby town. And they burn it, and its corn fields, to the ground.

The English run short of food. They start demanding it from their neighbours. Fighting breaks out. <sigh> The English commander kills a local leader named Wingina.

And when the famous sailor Francis Drake stops by in fifteen eighty-six, the soldiers jump on his ships and go home.

## Part 1C

So in fifteen eighty-seven, Raleigh tries again. This time, not soldiers. Families. A hundred and fifteen men, women and children, led by the artist John White, as governor.

They're supposed to settle further north, at the Chesapeake Bay. But the ship's pilot refuses to take them any further. <tsk> He drops them off at Roanoke.

A few weeks after they land, White's daughter Eleanor has a baby girl. They name her Virginia. Virginia Dare. <short pause> The first English child born in the Americas.

The colony is short of everything. So the colonists ask John White to sail back to England, and bring supplies.

<breath> He doesn't want to go. But he goes. He leaves behind his daughter, and his newborn granddaughter.

## Part 1D

He can't come back. England is at war with Spain. <gasp> The Spanish Armada, a hundred and thirty warships, is sailing to invade England. Every ship is needed.

It takes him three years.

In August fifteen-ninety, John White finally returns to Roanoke. <short pause> It's his granddaughter's third birthday.

His men blow a trumpet. They sing English songs. Nobody answers.

<whispers> The houses are gone. Taken down. There's no sign of a fight. No bodies. No graves.

The chests White had buried are dug up and broken open. His books torn. His pictures rotted in the rain.

And carved into a tree, three letters. C... R... O.

<long pause> And on a post at the entrance to the fort, one whole word.

CROATOAN.

## Part 1E

Before he left, White had told the colonists: if you move, carve where you went. And if you're in danger, carve a cross above it.

<short pause> There's no cross.

Croatoan is the name of an island to the south, now part of Hatteras. It's also the name of the people who live there. Friendly people. One of them, a man named Manteo, had sailed to England and back with the English.

White is relieved. He wants to sail to Croatoan the next morning.

<sigh> But a storm hits. The ships lose their anchors. The crew refuses to go on. And the ships sail back to England.

<long pause> John White never sees his family again.

Hold on to that word. Croatoan. We'll come back to it.

## Part 2: Pirates and Planters

Over the next century, English settlers push down from Virginia, into the land the King calls Carolina. Carolina, from Carolus, the Latin name for King Charles.

<chuckle> At first, North and South Carolina are one colony. But the north is so different from the south that they split apart in the seventeen-hundreds. The south has a big port at Charleston, and huge rice plantations. The north has small farms, pine forests, swamps... and a coast too dangerous for big ships.

## Part 2B (quiet style)

As settlers push onto their land, the Tuscarora fight back, in seventeen eleven.

<breath> They lose. Many are killed, or sold into slavery.

Most of the survivors walk north, all the way to New York, and join the Iroquois.

## Part 2C

And that dangerous coast, full of hidden inlets and shallow water, is perfect for one kind of person. <chuckle> Pirates.

The most famous is Edward Teach. Blackbeard. He has a huge black beard, braided with ribbons. And in battle, he ties slow-burning fuses under his hat, so smoke curls around his face. <gasp> He looks like a demon.

In seventeen eighteen, with a fleet of ships, he blockades Charleston, the richest town in the South, and holds its people hostage until they hand over... a chest of medicine.

Then his flagship, the Queen Anne's Revenge, runs aground on a sandbar near Beaufort, North Carolina. <groan> Some say he wrecked it on purpose, to get rid of some of his crew.

<short pause> Blackbeard sails to the little town of Bath, and the governor of North Carolina gives him a pardon. And some say the governor gets a share of his loot.

## Part 2D

The governor of Virginia doesn't trust any of this. So he sends a navy lieutenant, Robert Maynard, to hunt Blackbeard down.

In November seventeen eighteen, they meet at Ocracoke Inlet. Blackbeard's men fire their cannons, and Maynard hides most of his crew below deck. <gasp> Blackbeard thinks he's won. He jumps aboard.

And the sailors pour out of the hold.

Blackbeard fights on, with five gunshot wounds and about twenty sword cuts, before he falls.

<chuckle> They hang his head from the front of the ship. Not exactly a quiet retirement.

<short pause> In nineteen ninety-six, divers found the wreck of the Queen Anne's Revenge, off Beaufort. They've since brought up cannons, an anchor, and thousands of objects. Even tiny flecks of gold dust.

## Part 2E

Meanwhile, North Carolina gets rich, slowly, from its trees.

The giant longleaf pines make tar, pitch and turpentine. The sticky stuff that keeps the wooden ships of the Royal Navy watertight. <chuckle> North Carolina makes so much tar that its people end up called "Tar Heels". One story says it's because tar stuck to their feet. Another says they stood their ground in battle, as if they had tar on their heels.

## Part 3: Rip Van Winkle

In April seventeen seventy-six, North Carolina becomes the first colony to officially tell its delegates in Congress: vote for independence. The Halifax Resolves.

<short pause> A few weeks before that, at Moores Creek Bridge, Patriot militia pull up the planks of the bridge, and grease the bare logs with soap and animal fat. <laugh> Loyalist soldiers charge across, slipping and sliding, straight into gunfire. It's over in minutes.

<breath> And in seventeen eighty-one, at Guilford Courthouse, the British general Cornwallis beats the Americans. But he loses about a quarter of his army doing it. A British politician says: another victory like that will ruin us.

Cornwallis marches away, to Virginia. To a little town called Yorktown. Where he surrenders.

## Part 3B

After the war, North Carolina does something very North Carolina. <tsk> It refuses to join the new United States.

Not until the Constitution gets a Bill of Rights. A list of freedoms the government can't take away.

It finally joins in November seventeen eighty-nine. The twelfth state. <short pause> That same year, it creates the University of North Carolina. In seventeen ninety-five, it becomes the first public university in America to open its doors to students.

## Part 3C

In seventeen ninety-nine, a twelve-year-old boy named Conrad Reed skips church to go fishing in a creek on his family's farm. <short pause> He spots a heavy yellow rock in the water.

The family uses it as a doorstop. For three years.

<chuckle> Then a jeweller tells them it's gold. Seventeen pounds of it. He buys it for three dollars and fifty cents. It was worth about a thousand times that.

It's the first documented gold find in the United States. <gasp> North Carolina has America's first gold rush. For almost thirty years, nearly all the gold made into American coins comes from North Carolina. Charlotte even gets its own branch of the U.S. Mint.

## Part 3D

But apart from the gold, North Carolina changes so slowly that people call it the "Rip Van Winkle State". <chuckle> Like the man in the story who falls asleep for twenty years. Few roads, few schools, while its neighbours race ahead.

<sigh> And it's a slave state. By eighteen sixty, about a third of its people are enslaved.

In Chapel Hill, an enslaved man named George Moses Horton teaches himself to read. He makes up poems in his head, and sells love poems to university students for their girlfriends. <short pause> In eighteen twenty-nine, he publishes a book of poems. One of the first books by a Black writer in the South.

He hopes it'll buy his freedom. It doesn't. He stays enslaved for another thirty-six years.

## Part 3E (quiet style)

In Edenton, an enslaved young woman named Harriet Jacobs escapes from the man who owns her.

<breath> She hides in a tiny crawl space under her grandmother's roof. Too low to stand up in. Hot in summer, freezing in winter.

She stays there for seven years. Watching her children through a little hole in the wall.

<long pause> In eighteen forty-two, she finally escapes north. Years later, she writes her story. It's one of the most important books ever written about slavery.

## Part 3F (quiet style)

In the eighteen-thirties, the United States forces the Cherokee out of their homeland, on the Trail of Tears.

<long pause> But some hide deep in the Great Smoky Mountains of North Carolina. And they stay.

Their descendants are the Eastern Band of Cherokee, who still live in those mountains today.

## Part 4: Civil War

In May eighteen sixty-one, North Carolina is one of the last states to join the Confederacy.

<short pause> But it fights hard. At the mouth of the Cape Fear River stands Fort Fisher, a giant fortress of sand, protecting Wilmington. Fast, sleek ships slip past the Union navy at night, bringing guns and supplies from Europe.

Fort Fisher holds out until January eighteen sixty-five. <sigh> When it falls, the Confederacy loses its last big open port.

And in April eighteen sixty-five, at a farmhouse near Durham, the Confederate general Joseph Johnston surrenders to General Sherman. Almost ninety thousand soldiers. <short pause> The biggest surrender of the war.

## Part 4B (quiet style)

North Carolina sends more soldiers than almost any Confederate state.

<breath> And it loses more of them. Around forty thousand North Carolinians die in the war.

## Part 4C (quiet style)

After the war, Wilmington is the biggest city in the state. And in eighteen ninety-eight, it has an elected government that includes Black and white leaders.

<long pause> A mob of armed white men overthrows it. They burn a Black newspaper, kill many Black residents, and force elected officials out of town at gunpoint.

It's often called the only successful coup on American soil.

## Part 5: First in Flight

<chuckle> And now, those brothers from Ohio.

Wilbur and Orville Wright run a bicycle shop in Dayton. They want to build a flying machine. And they need a place with strong, steady winds, and soft sand to crash into.

They write to the weather bureau. The answer is a fishing village on North Carolina's Outer Banks. Kitty Hawk.

Year after year, they come back in the autumn and camp in the sand dunes, flying gliders, swatting mosquitoes, and fixing what breaks. <groan> Which is a lot.

## Part 5B

On the morning of December seventeenth, nineteen-oh-three, at Kill Devil Hills, it's freezing and windy. Orville lies down on the lower wing. The engine starts.

<gasp> And the airplane lifts off the sand. For twelve seconds. A hundred and twenty feet. Shorter than the wings of a jumbo jet.

But it's the first powered, controlled flight in history.

They fly three more times that day. The longest goes almost a minute. <groan> Then a gust of wind flips the plane over and smashes it.

<chuckle> Ohio built the plane. North Carolina gave it the sky. Both states still argue about it.

## Part 5C

Meanwhile, North Carolina finally wakes up.

In eighteen ninety-three, in New Bern, a pharmacist named Caleb Bradham mixes a fizzy drink he calls "Brad's Drink". <chuckle> Five years later, he gives it a better name. Pepsi-Cola.

In the mountains, the millionaire George Vanderbilt builds a French-style castle with two hundred and fifty rooms. The Biltmore. <gasp> Still the largest private home in America.

In Durham, a man named James Buchanan Duke uses a new machine that rolls cigarettes as fast as dozens of workers. He becomes one of the richest men in America. <short pause> His family's money turns a little college into Duke University.

Tobacco and cotton mills make the Piedmont rich. And in nineteen thirty-seven, in Winston-Salem, a man opens a doughnut shop. Krispy Kreme.

## Part 5D (quiet style)

But the mill workers are paid very little, and work very long hours.

In nineteen twenty-nine, workers at the Loray Mill in Gastonia go on strike.

<breath> A young mother named Ella May Wiggins writes songs for the strikers. That September, she is shot and killed on her way to a union meeting.

Nobody is ever convicted.

## Part 6: Sit-ins and Science

On February first, nineteen sixty, four Black college students in Greensboro walk into Woolworth's. Ezell Blair, Franklin McCain, Joseph McNeil and David Richmond.

They sit down at the whites-only lunch counter. They order coffee. They are refused. <short pause> They stay. Until the store closes.

The next day, they come back with more students. And the next day, more. <gasp> Within weeks, sit-ins spread to cities across the South.

That April, a civil rights organiser from North Carolina named Ella Baker brings the student protesters together in Raleigh. They form a new group, the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee. It becomes one of the most important groups of the civil rights movement.

## Part 6B

As tobacco and textile jobs fade, North Carolina makes a bet. <short pause> In nineteen fifty-nine, it builds the Research Triangle Park, between three universities in Raleigh, Durham and Chapel Hill. It fills with labs and tech companies.

Charlotte becomes the second-biggest banking city in America, after New York.

<chuckle> And in the North Carolina hills, men who used to outrun the police in fast cars full of illegal moonshine start racing each other for fun. In nineteen forty-nine, in Charlotte, NASCAR holds its first race for regular stock cars. Today, NASCAR's Hall of Fame is in Charlotte.

<laugh> And a kid from Wilmington, cut from his high school's top team, becomes the greatest basketball player of all time. Michael Jordan.

## Part 6C (quiet style)

In September two thousand twenty-four, the remains of Hurricane Helene stall over the mountains of western North Carolina.

<breath> Rivers rise higher than anyone has ever seen. Whole towns are swept away.

More than a hundred people in the state are killed.

## Part 6D

So what happened to the Lost Colony?

<whispers> It starts with one word, carved into a post. Croatoan.

For four hundred years, it was one of America's great mysteries. People blamed disease, Spanish soldiers, hurricanes, even aliens. <chuckle> It's always aliens.

But in the last few years, archaeologists digging on Hatteras Island, the old Croatoan, have found English objects from the fifteen hundreds. Pieces of guns. A piece of a slate writing tablet. The handle of a sword. Mixed in with the belongings of the Croatoan people.

<short pause> And inland, at a site on the Albemarle Sound, they've found English pottery too.

## Part 6E

Many researchers now think the colonists didn't vanish. They split up. And they went to live with their Native neighbours, and became part of their families, just like the carving said.

Somewhere out there, Virginia Dare may have grown up. Learned a new language. Had children of her own.

<short pause> The answer may have been written on that post the whole time.

<chuckle> It just took four hundred years to dig it up.
