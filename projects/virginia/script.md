## Hook

Eight American presidents were born in Virginia. <short pause> More than any other state.

The Pentagon? Virginia. The CIA? Also Virginia.

Arlington National Cemetery used to be Robert E. Lee's front yard.

<long pause> The first English settlers here got so hungry, they ate their own dead.

And in one single summer, sixteen nineteen, Virginia gave America its first elected assembly. And its first enslaved Africans.

<short pause> So how does one colony give America freedom and slavery in the same summer?

<whispers> It starts with a plant the king of England hated.

## Part 1: Powhatan

Before the English, this land is called Tsenacommacah. It's ruled by a powerful leader named Wahunsenacah, whom the English call Powhatan.

He leads an alliance of around thirty nations, maybe fifteen thousand people or more, along the rivers that flow into Chesapeake Bay.

They live in towns of bark houses, farm corn, fish the rivers, and trade copper and shell beads.

And Powhatan has a daughter. Her childhood nickname is Pocahontas.

## Part 2: Jamestown

In May sixteen-oh-seven, three English ships sail up a river they name the James, after their king. About a hundred men and boys build a fort on a swampy island.

Jamestown. The first permanent English settlement in America.

They're a company. They're here to make money. Many of them are gentlemen who have never worked with their hands. They look for gold. They find mosquitoes, bad water, and disease.

Their leader for a while, Captain John Smith, says it plainly. He who does not work, shall not eat.

## Part 2B (quiet style)

The winter of sixteen-oh-nine is called the Starving Time.

The Powhatan surround the fort. Supply ships are lost. The colonists eat horses, dogs, rats and shoe leather.

And in two thousand thirteen, archaeologists confirmed what the old reports said. Some colonists ate the bodies of the dead.

Of around five hundred people, only about sixty survive the winter.

## Part 2C

Jamestown is about to be abandoned. And then, a colonist named John Rolfe tries something.

He plants a sweeter kind of tobacco, from seeds brought from the Caribbean.

King James the First hates tobacco. He wrote a whole pamphlet against it. He called smoking "loathsome to the eye, hateful to the nose, harmful to the brain, dangerous to the lungs."

<chuckle> He was right. And it didn't matter. England goes crazy for it.

Tobacco saves Virginia. Soon it's planted everywhere, even in the streets of Jamestown.

John Rolfe marries Pocahontas in sixteen fourteen. She travels to England as a celebrity, falls ill, and dies there at about twenty-one.

## Part 2D

Now, tobacco needs two things. Land. And lots and lots of workers.

So the company makes Virginia more attractive. Come over, and you get land. And in July sixteen nineteen, it lets settlers elect their own representatives.

The General Assembly meets in the church at Jamestown. The first elected lawmaking body in English America.

## Part 2E (quiet style)

And a few weeks later, in late August sixteen nineteen, a ship called the White Lion arrives at Point Comfort.

It carries more than twenty Africans, captured from a Portuguese slave ship. They are traded for food.

They are the first recorded Africans in English Virginia.

Over the next decades, Virginia's laws turn slavery into something permanent, and passed from mother to child.

Freedom for some. Slavery for others. Both arrive in the same summer. And both, in their own way, for tobacco.

## Part 3: Revolution

By the seventeen hundreds, Virginia is the biggest and richest of the colonies. Planters build grand houses on the rivers. And they raise a generation of leaders.

In seventeen seventy-five, in a church in Richmond, Patrick Henry stands up and says, "Give me liberty, or give me death!"

Thomas Jefferson, from Virginia, writes the Declaration of Independence. "All men are created equal."

George Washington, from Virginia, leads the army.

And in seventeen eighty-one, at Yorktown, Virginia, Washington and the French trap the British army. The British surrender. The Revolution is won. In Virginia.

## Part 3B

James Madison, from Virginia, becomes the "Father of the Constitution".

Washington, Jefferson, Madison, Monroe. Four of the first five presidents are Virginians.

But here's the contradiction. Washington and Jefferson both enslave hundreds of people. The men who wrote "liberty" live on tobacco land worked by enslaved people.

## Part 4: Slavery and War

By eighteen sixty, nearly half a million people in Virginia are enslaved.

## Part 4B (quiet style)

In eighteen thirty-one, an enslaved preacher named Nat Turner leads a revolt in Southampton County. About sixty white people are killed. Then many more Black people are killed in revenge, and Virginia passes even harsher laws.

## Part 4C

In eighteen sixty-one, Virginia leaves the Union. Richmond becomes the capital of the Confederacy.

But the mountain counties in the west don't want to go. So they leave Virginia. In eighteen sixty-three, they become a new state. West Virginia.

More Civil War battles are fought in Virginia than in any other state.

During the war, the Union army takes Robert E. Lee's own home at Arlington, and starts burying soldiers on his land. So he can never go back.

And here's a strange one. The first big battle of the war, Bull Run, is fought on a farm owned by a man named Wilmer McLean. A shell lands in his kitchen. So he moves his family far away, to a quiet village called Appomattox Court House.

In April eighteen sixty-five, Lee surrenders to Grant... in Wilmer McLean's living room.

The war started in his yard. And ended in his parlour.

## Part 5: Jim Crow

After the war, Virginia passes Jim Crow laws that separate Black and white people.

When the Supreme Court orders schools to integrate in nineteen fifty-four, Virginia's leaders call for "Massive Resistance". In Prince Edward County, they close all the public schools rather than integrate them. For five years.

## Part 5B

But change comes from Virginia too.

Richard and Mildred Loving, a white man and a Black woman, marry and are arrested for it. Their case goes to the Supreme Court. In nineteen sixty-seven, it rules that every state must allow people of different races to marry.

Loving versus Virginia.

And in nineteen ninety, Virginia elects Douglas Wilder. The first elected Black governor in American history. The grandson of enslaved people, governing from Richmond, the old capital of the Confederacy.

## Part 5C

In the nineteen forties, the army builds the biggest office building in the world, in Arlington. Five sides. The Pentagon.

## Part 5D (quiet style)

On September eleventh, two thousand one, a hijacked plane crashes into the Pentagon. One hundred and eighty-four people are killed.

## Part 5E

Today, Northern Virginia is full of tech companies and giant data centres. Much of the world's internet traffic passes through them. The old tobacco state runs on cables now.

So how does one colony give America freedom and slavery in the same summer?

It starts with a plant the king of England hated.

Tobacco saved Jamestown. To grow it, Virginia needed people. To attract them, it gave land and a vote. To work the fields, it bought enslaved Africans. One plant. Two inheritances. And America has been arguing about both of them ever since.

<short pause> Virginia gave the country its first assembly, its most famous words about liberty, and the battlefields where slavery finally ended.

<chuckle> Not bad for a place that was nearly abandoned because nobody wanted to farm.
