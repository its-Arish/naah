## Hook

Microsoft? <short pause> Started in Albuquerque. Roswell, the UFO town? New Mexico.

Some Americans think New Mexico is a foreign country. People from New Mexico have actually been asked for their passports.

Its capital, Santa Fe, was a capital ten years before the Pilgrims landed.

<long pause> And on July sixteenth, nineteen forty-five, in the New Mexico desert, the world's first atomic bomb exploded. The families living nearby were never warned.

<short pause> So how does the oldest capital in America end up building the newest, most terrifying weapon on Earth?

<whispers> It starts with a boys' school on a lonely mountain mesa.

## Part 1: First Peoples

Thirteen thousand years ago, near a town now called Clovis, hunters chase mammoths with beautiful stone spear points. Archaeologists name a whole ancient culture after the town. Clovis.

## Part 1B

A thousand years ago, in a remote canyon, the Ancestral Puebloans build something astonishing. Chaco Canyon.

Giant stone "great houses", some with hundreds of rooms, several storeys tall. Straight roads running for miles across the desert. Buildings lined up with the sun and the moon.

Chaco is the centre of a world. And then, by around eleven fifty, people move away.

Their descendants are the Pueblo peoples. Today there are nineteen Pueblos in New Mexico. Taos Pueblo has been lived in for about a thousand years.

The Apache and the Navajo, and later the Comanche, also make this land their home.

## Part 2: New Spain

In fifteen forty, Coronado marches through, looking for the Seven Cities of Gold. He doesn't find them.

In fifteen ninety-eight, Juan de Oñate leads settlers north from Mexico, along a road that becomes the Camino Real. The Royal Road.

## Part 2B (quiet style)

When the people of Acoma Pueblo resist, Oñate's soldiers attack. Hundreds are killed. And Oñate orders that men from Acoma have one foot cut off.

## Part 2C

In sixteen ten, the Spanish build a new capital. Santa Fe. The "Holy Faith".

Its Palace of the Governors still stands. The oldest continuously used public building in the United States.

<chuckle> Ten years later, the Pilgrims are only just stepping off the Mayflower.

But New Mexico is very, very far from everything. Caravans from Mexico City take six months to arrive. It's the edge of the Spanish empire.

## Part 3: Pueblo Revolt

The Spanish force the Pueblo peoples to work for them, pay tribute, and give up their religion. Priests burn sacred objects. Leaders are whipped.

In sixteen eighty, a Tewa religious leader named Popé secretly organises dozens of Pueblos, speaking different languages, spread across hundreds of miles.

Runners carry knotted cords. Each day, untie one knot. When the last knot is untied... rise up.

In August sixteen eighty, they rise. All at once.

The Spanish are driven out of New Mexico completely. For twelve years.

It's one of the most successful Native uprisings against European colonisers in history.

The Spanish come back in sixteen ninety-two. But after that, they're more careful. Pueblo languages and ceremonies survive to this day.

## Part 3B

In eighteen twenty-one, Mexico wins independence. The Santa Fe Trail opens, bringing American traders and wagons from Missouri.

And in eighteen forty-six, during the war with Mexico, an American army marches into Santa Fe. New Mexico becomes American territory.

## Part 4: Territory

Then it waits. And waits.

For more than sixty years, Congress refuses to make New Mexico a state. Some say it's too wild. Many in Congress don't trust a place where most people speak Spanish and are Catholic.

Meanwhile, the Wild West happens. Cattle wars. Outlaws. A young gunman called Billy the Kid fights in the Lincoln County War. He's shot dead in eighteen eighty-one, at about twenty-one years old.

Finally, in January nineteen twelve, New Mexico becomes the forty-seventh state.

## Part 4B

Four years later, the Mexican revolutionary Pancho Villa raids the little border town of Columbus, New Mexico. The U.S. Army chases him into Mexico for months. They never catch him.

## Part 4C

In the nineteen-twenties and thirties, artists fall in love with New Mexico's light. Taos and Santa Fe become art towns. The painter Georgia O'Keeffe moves to the desert and paints its bones and red hills.

And in Roswell, a scientist named Robert Goddard launches rockets into the sky. Years before the space age.

## Part 5: Secret City

Now, the school.

In the nineteen-twenties, a young man from New York with weak health is sent to New Mexico to get stronger. He falls in love with the mountains. He rides horses across them.

His name is J. Robert Oppenheimer.

High on a mesa in the Jemez Mountains, there's a small, isolated boys' boarding school. The Los Alamos Ranch School. Boys ride horses and sleep on open porches in winter.

## Part 5B

In nineteen forty-two, the United States needs a place to design an atomic bomb. Somewhere far from everyone. Hidden. Hard to reach.

Oppenheimer, now a famous physicist, knows exactly the place.

The army buys the ranch school. And scientists from all over the world arrive in secret. Their address is just a post office box in Santa Fe. Their children's birth certificates say they were born at "Box sixteen sixty-three".

Los Alamos.

## Part 5C (quiet style)

At five-thirty in the morning, on July sixteenth, nineteen forty-five, in a desert the Spanish called the Jornada del Muerto, the Journey of the Dead, they test the first atomic bomb. Trinity.

The flash lights up the mountains. The heat is felt miles away.

Radioactive dust drifts over ranches and villages in the Tularosa Basin. Families who were never told collect rainwater from their roofs and drink it. For decades, many have said the government ignored them.

Three weeks later, atomic bombs destroy Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

## Part 6: Today

In July nineteen forty-seven, near Roswell, a rancher finds strange debris. The army announces it has recovered a "flying disc". Then says it was a weather balloon. Decades later, the Air Force says it was a secret balloon project to listen for Soviet nuclear tests.

<chuckle> Roswell has been the UFO capital of the world ever since.

In nineteen fifty, firefighters rescue a burned bear cub from a forest fire in New Mexico. He becomes the living Smokey Bear.

And in nineteen seventy-five, two young men named Bill Gates and Paul Allen start a little software company in Albuquerque. Microsoft.

Today, New Mexico has the biggest hot-air balloon festival in the world. A spaceport. And a magazine column called "One of Our Fifty Is Missing", full of stories of people who forgot it's part of America.

## Part 6B

So how does the oldest capital in America end up building the newest, most terrifying weapon on Earth?

It starts with a boys' school on a lonely mountain mesa.

For four hundred years, New Mexico was the edge of everything. Too far, too high, too empty, for empires to bother with. That isolation protected the Pueblos, and kept old Santa Fe old. And when a young scientist who had fallen in love with those lonely mountains needed a place no one would find... he remembered the school on the mesa.

<short pause> New Mexico was always the place that was hard to find.

<chuckle> Some people are still looking for it.
