## Hook

Coca-Cola? <short pause> Invented in Atlanta. As a medicine.

Georgia is "the Peach State". But it doesn't even grow the most peaches.

The world's busiest airport? Atlanta.

<long pause> In eighteen sixty-four, an army burned Atlanta and marched all the way to the sea.

But the strangest thing? Georgia began as the only colony in America that banned slavery.

<short pause> So how does the colony that banned slavery become one of the biggest slave states in the country?

<whispers> It starts with a man whose friend died in a debtors' prison.

## Part 1: Mound Builders

A thousand years ago, Georgia has towns with great earthen pyramids.

At Ocmulgee and Etowah, people of the Mississippian culture build flat-topped mounds, some taller than a six-storey building, with temples and chiefs' houses on top.

In fifteen forty, the Spanish explorer Hernando de Soto marches through, looking for gold. His army brings war and disease. The great mound towns fade.

Later, the land belongs mostly to two great nations. The Muscogee, or Creek, in the south. And the Cherokee in the mountains of the north.

## Part 2: The Experiment

In seventeen twenty-eight, in London, a man named Robert Castell is thrown into a debtors' prison. In England back then, if you couldn't pay what you owed, you could be locked up until you did.

He catches smallpox in prison. And he dies.

His friend is a member of Parliament named James Oglethorpe. Oglethorpe is furious. He leads an investigation into England's terrible prisons.

And then he has a bigger idea. A new colony in America, for England's "worthy poor". People who need a second chance.

## Part 2B

In seventeen thirty-two, King George the Second grants the charter. The colony is named after him. Georgia.

In February seventeen thirty-three, Oglethorpe lands on a bluff above the Savannah River. He makes friends with the local Yamacraw leader, Tomochichi. And he lays out the city of Savannah, a grid of streets and leafy squares. You can still walk them today.

Funny thing, though. Very few actual debtors ever come. <chuckle> The famous "debtors' colony" mostly wasn't.

## Part 2C

But Georgia has rules. Strange rules.

No big plantations. Each family gets a small farm. And from seventeen thirty-five... no slavery. Georgia is the only one of the thirteen colonies to ban it by law.

Why? Oglethorpe and the trustees want small farmers who work their own land. They want settlers who can fight, to protect South Carolina from the Spanish in Florida. And they worry that enslaved people would escape to Spanish Florida, which promised them freedom.

But next door, in South Carolina, planters are getting rich growing rice with enslaved labour. And Georgia's settlers are jealous.

They complain. They petition. They smuggle. For years.

And in seventeen fifty-one, the trustees give up. Slavery becomes legal in Georgia.

## Part 3: Colony and Revolution

Georgia becomes a royal colony, and grows rice and indigo on the coast.

In the Revolution, the British capture Savannah and hold it for years. Georgia is one of the most loyalist colonies.

In seventeen eighty-eight, Georgia becomes the fourth state.

## Part 3B

Then, in seventeen ninety-three, on a plantation near Savannah, a young man from Connecticut named Eli Whitney builds a machine that pulls the seeds out of cotton. Fast.

The cotton gin.

Suddenly, cotton is hugely profitable. Planters want more land, and more enslaved workers, to grow it. Cotton spreads across Georgia, and the whole South.

By eighteen sixty, more than four hundred and sixty thousand people in Georgia are enslaved. Almost half of everyone in the state.

## Part 3C

And the land they want belongs to the Cherokee.

The Cherokee have done everything the government told them to do. They have farms, a written constitution, and a capital at New Echota. A Cherokee man named Sequoyah invents a writing system for his language. Within a few years, most Cherokee can read and write. They print their own newspaper.

Then, in eighteen twenty-nine, gold is found near Dahlonega. One of America's first gold rushes. Thousands of miners pour into Cherokee land.

The Cherokee fight Georgia all the way to the Supreme Court. And in eighteen thirty-two, the court rules on their side.

It doesn't matter.

## Part 3D (quiet style)

In eighteen thirty-eight, soldiers force the Cherokee out of their homes at gunpoint and hold them in camps.

Then they are marched west, more than a thousand miles, to what is now Oklahoma.

Thousands die on the way. The Cherokee call it "the trail where they cried". We call it the Trail of Tears.

## Part 4: Civil War

In eighteen sixty-one, Georgia leaves the Union to protect slavery.

## Part 4B (quiet style)

Near the town of Andersonville, the Confederacy builds a prison camp for captured Union soldiers. It's overcrowded, with almost no shelter and dirty water.

Nearly thirteen thousand prisoners die there.

## Part 4C

In eighteen sixty-four, Union General William Tecumseh Sherman captures Atlanta, the South's railroad hub. When his army leaves, much of the city burns.

Then he marches sixty thousand soldiers across Georgia to the sea, destroying railroads, farms and supplies on the way.

In December, he captures Savannah, and sends President Lincoln a telegram offering him the city as a Christmas present.

<short pause> Savannah, the city Oglethorpe built, survives.

## Part 5: Atlanta Rises

Atlanta rebuilds so fast it takes a phoenix as its symbol. Its motto is one Latin word. "Resurgens". Rising again.

In eighteen eighty-six, an Atlanta pharmacist named John Pemberton invents a syrup he sells as a "brain tonic". At first, it even contains a little cocaine from the coca leaf. That's where the name comes from.

Coca-Cola.

But after the war comes Jim Crow. Segregation. Black Georgians are blocked from voting, and many face terrible violence.

## Part 5B

And in nineteen twenty-nine, in Atlanta, a baby is born in a house on Auburn Avenue. His father is a preacher at Ebenezer Baptist Church.

Martin Luther King Jr.

He grows up to lead the civil rights movement, with marches, boycotts, and a dream. And Atlanta becomes its headquarters.

## Part 5C

Then Georgia keeps surprising everyone.

In nineteen seventy-four, in Atlanta, Hank Aaron breaks Babe Ruth's home run record.

In nineteen seventy-seven, a peanut farmer from the tiny town of Plains, Jimmy Carter, becomes President of the United States.

In nineteen ninety-six, Atlanta hosts the Olympics.

And today, Atlanta's airport is the busiest in the world. Georgia makes so many movies and TV shows that people call it "Y'allywood".

## Part 5D

So how does the colony that banned slavery become one of the biggest slave states in the country?

It starts with a man whose friend died in a debtors' prison.

Oglethorpe wanted a colony of small, free farmers. No slavery. But his settlers looked across the river at South Carolina's wealth, and fought until they got it. Then the cotton gin made slavery the engine of Georgia.

And a century later, the grandchildren of the enslaved led the movement that tore down segregation. From Atlanta.

<short pause> The colony that was meant to give people a second chance... finally did.

<chuckle> And it took a soft drink and a peanut farmer to make the rest of the world notice.
