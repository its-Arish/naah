## Hook

Ben and Jerry's? <short pause> Started in an old gas station in Vermont.

Vermont makes about half of all the maple syrup in America.

Its capital, Montpelier, is the only state capital without a McDonald's. And there are no billboards anywhere in the state.

<long pause> In the nineteen-thirties, Vermont passed a law to sterilise people it called "unfit". Many of them were poor, disabled, or Abenaki.

And for fourteen years, Vermont was its own country. With its own money. And its own post office.

<short pause> So why was Vermont its own country... and why did it give that up?

<whispers> It starts with a gang called the Green Mountain Boys.

## Part 1: First Peoples

Vermont is green mountains, valleys, rivers, and the long blue Lake Champlain.

For thousands of years, it's the homeland of the Abenaki. They fish, hunt, grow corn, and tap maple trees in early spring, boiling the sap into sugar.

In sixteen-oh-nine, the French explorer Samuel de Champlain paddles down the lake and names it after himself. <chuckle> Of course.

The name Vermont comes from French, too. "Verts monts". Green mountains.

## Part 2: The Grants

Now, the gang.

In the seventeen-fifties, the governor of New Hampshire, Benning Wentworth, starts selling land grants west of the Connecticut River. Whole towns. Settlers buy them and move in.

There's one problem. New York says the land belongs to New York.

In seventeen sixty-four, the king agrees with New York. So New York tells the settlers: your land isn't really yours. Buy it again, from us.

The settlers are furious.

## Part 2B

A big, loud, hard-drinking frontiersman named Ethan Allen organises a militia. The Green Mountain Boys.

When New York sends sheriffs and surveyors, the Green Mountain Boys chase them off. They burn their cabins. They whip one man with branches. And New York puts a price on Ethan Allen's head.

<chuckle> He's basically an outlaw. And very popular.

## Part 2C

Then, the Revolution.

In May seventeen seventy-five, just weeks after Lexington and Concord, Ethan Allen and the Green Mountain Boys, with Benedict Arnold, sneak up on the British fort at Ticonderoga at dawn. The guards are asleep. They capture it without a single death.

Allen later claims he demanded the surrender "in the name of the Great Jehovah and the Continental Congress".

The fort's cannons are dragged all the way to Boston. They help drive the British out.

## Part 3: Republic of Vermont

But Vermonters don't want to be part of New York. So in January seventeen seventy-seven, they declare independence. From Britain. And from New York.

They call it the Republic of New Connecticut. Then, Vermont.

That July, they write a constitution. It does things no other constitution in America does yet. It bans adult slavery. And it lets men vote even if they don't own property.

## Part 3B

But the Continental Congress refuses to recognise Vermont. New York won't allow it.

So Vermont runs itself. For fourteen years. It has its own governor and legislature. It makes its own copper coins. It runs its own post office. It even talks with foreign countries.

And Ethan Allen and his brother Ira secretly negotiate with the British. About Vermont maybe joining Canada.

<chuckle> It might have been a trick to pressure Congress. Or it might not. Nobody's completely sure.

## Part 3C

Finally, in seventeen ninety, Vermont agrees to pay New York thirty thousand dollars to drop its claims.

And on March fourth, seventeen ninety-one, Vermont joins the United States. The fourteenth state. The first new one after the original thirteen.

## Part 4: Fourteenth State

Vermont stays a land of small farms, sheep, dairy cows and stone. Its quarries ship marble and granite across America.

## Part 4B

In October eighteen sixty-four, twenty-one Confederate soldiers in disguise, who had slipped into Canada, ride into the little town of St. Albans, Vermont. They rob three banks.

<chuckle> It's the northernmost land action of the Civil War. In Vermont.

## Part 4C

In August nineteen twenty-three, Vice President Calvin Coolidge is visiting his father's farmhouse in Plymouth Notch, Vermont. There's no electricity. No telephone.

In the middle of the night, news arrives. President Harding has died.

So, by the light of a kerosene lamp, Coolidge's father, a local notary, swears his son in as President of the United States.

## Part 4D (quiet style)

In November nineteen twenty-seven, days of heavy rain cause the worst flood in Vermont's history. Rivers sweep away homes, bridges and railroads. Eighty-four people are killed.

## Part 4E (quiet style)

In nineteen thirty-one, after a study of so-called "degenerate" families, Vermont passes a law allowing the state to sterilise people it considers "unfit".

Many of those targeted are poor, disabled, or of French-Canadian or Abenaki heritage. Some families hide who they are for generations.

In twenty twenty-one, Vermont's legislature formally apologises.

## Part 5: Today

In nineteen thirty-four, near Woodstock, skiers rig America's first ski tow, using a rope and a car engine. Vermont becomes ski country.

In nineteen sixty-eight, Vermont bans billboards, to protect its views.

In nineteen seventy-eight, two old friends, Ben Cohen and Jerry Greenfield, take a five-dollar correspondence course in ice cream making, and open a shop in a gas station in Burlington.

And in two thousand, Vermont becomes the first state to create civil unions for same-sex couples.

## Part 5B

So why was Vermont its own country... and why did it give that up?

It starts with a gang called the Green Mountain Boys.

They fought for their land against New York. They fought for America against Britain. And when nobody would make them a state, they made themselves a country, and ran it their own way. Only when New York finally gave up did Vermont agree to join.

<short pause> Vermont didn't join the United States because it had to. It joined because it finally got what it wanted.

<chuckle> And it's been doing things its own way ever since. No billboards. Lots of syrup.
