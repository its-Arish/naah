# The Entire History of Vermont

Narration script, written to be voiced with **Gemini TTS** and then animated. About 2,100 spoken words, so roughly 11 to 12 minutes when recorded (California ran 2,600 words to 13:50).

How to use it (the full rules are in `docs/PLAYBOOK.md` and `docs/HOOKS.md`):

- Each `##` heading is **one Gemini generation**. Paste only the lines under it, never the heading. Headings are paste breaks: never spoken, never shown. The film has no chapter cards.
- Paste the matching style line (table below) into Gemini's style box. Parts marked "quiet style" use the quiet line.
- Tags are angle brackets and only from Gemini's supported list: `<short pause>` `<long pause>` `<breath>` `<sigh>` `<exhales>` `<gasp>` `<chuckle>` `<laugh>` `<giggle>` `<phew>` `<tsk>` `<groan>` `<whispers>`. Never two tags side by side. CAPITALS stress a word; three dots add a hesitation; years and numbers are spelled out.

| Part | Style line for the style box |
| --- | --- |
| Hook | Quick, amused for the first three facts; slower and serious from "In the nineteen-thirties"; leaning in, almost whispering, on the last line. |
| Part 1 | Calm, woodsy, maple-season storyteller, with a wink. |
| Part 2 | Rowdy, rebellious outlaw energy. |
| Part 3 | Proud and independent, a little sly. |
| Part 4 | Plain-spoken, hardy farm voice; then grave. |
| Part 5 | Cosy and quirky, small-town proud; smile on the very last line. |
| Lettered parts | Use the style line of their number. |
| Any part marked "quiet style" | Quiet, slow and respectful. No smile in the voice. Long gaps between sentences. |

On-screen timeline labels (the only sign of a new era): First Peoples 10,000 BC to 1749 · The Grants 1749 to 1775 · Green Mountain Boys 1775 to 1777 · Republic of Vermont 1777 to 1791 · Fourteenth State 1791 to 1945 · Today 1945 to now.

## Hook

Ben and Jerry's? <short pause> Started in an old gas station in Vermont.

<chuckle> Vermont makes about half of all the maple syrup in America. Its capital, Montpelier, is famous for being the only state capital without a McDonald's. And there are no billboards anywhere in the state.

<long pause> In the nineteen-thirties, Vermont passed a law to sterilise people it called "unfit". Many of them were poor, disabled, or Abenaki.

And for fourteen years, Vermont was its own country. With its own money. And its own post office.

<short pause> So why was Vermont its own country... and why did it give that up?

<whispers> It starts with a gang called the Green Mountain Boys.

## Part 1: First Peoples

Vermont is green mountains, valleys, rivers, and the long blue Lake Champlain.

<gasp> At the end of the Ice Age, the ocean flooded in here. In eighteen forty-nine, railroad workers digging near the town of Charlotte find the bones of a whale. A beluga. Buried in a farm field, a hundred and fifty miles from the sea. <chuckle> Charlotte the Whale is now Vermont's state fossil.

<whispers> And some people say something else lives in Lake Champlain. A giant serpent called Champ. <chuckle> There are hundreds of reported sightings. And no proof.

## Part 1B

For thousands of years, Vermont is the homeland of the Abenaki. They fish, hunt, grow corn, and travel the rivers in birch-bark canoes.

<short pause> In early spring, when nights are freezing and days are warm, the sap starts running in the sugar maples. The Abenaki cut into the trees, collect the sweet sap, and boil it down into sugar. <chuckle> Settlers learn to do the same. It takes about forty gallons of sap to make one gallon of syrup.

In sixteen-oh-nine, the French explorer Samuel de Champlain paddles down the lake and names it after himself. <laugh> Of course.

The name Vermont comes from French, too. "Verts monts". Green mountains.

<sigh> For a long time, Vermont's government barely acknowledged that the Abenaki were still there. <short pause> In twenty eleven and twenty twelve, the state finally recognises four Abenaki bands.

## Part 2: The Grants

Now, the gang.

In the seventeen-fifties, the governor of New Hampshire, Benning Wentworth, starts selling land grants west of the Connecticut River. Whole towns. Settlers buy them and move in. <chuckle> He names the first one after himself. Bennington.

There's one problem. <tsk> New York says the land belongs to New York.

In seventeen sixty-four, the king agrees with New York. So New York tells the settlers: your land isn't really yours. Buy it again, from us.

<groan> The settlers are furious.

## Part 2B

A big, loud, hard-drinking frontiersman named Ethan Allen organises a militia. The Green Mountain Boys.

When New York sends sheriffs and surveyors, the Green Mountain Boys chase them off. They burn their cabins. <chuckle> They whip one man with beech branches, and call it giving him "the beech seal". New York puts a price on Ethan Allen's head.

<laugh> He's basically an outlaw. And very popular.

<tsk> It helps that Allen and his brothers own a land company, with tens of thousands of acres of New Hampshire grants. If New York wins, they lose everything. <short pause> Fighting for their neighbours is also fighting for their own fortune.

## Part 2C

Then, the Revolution.

<gasp> In May seventeen seventy-five, just weeks after Lexington and Concord, Ethan Allen and the Green Mountain Boys, with Benedict Arnold, sneak up on the British fort at Ticonderoga at dawn. <whispers> The guards are asleep. <short pause> They capture it without a single death.

Allen later claims he demanded the surrender "in the name of the Great Jehovah and the Continental Congress". <chuckle> Witnesses say he actually used ruder words.

The fort's cannons are dragged all the way to Boston, across the snowy mountains on sledges. They help drive the British out.

<sigh> A few months later, Allen tries to capture Montreal with a tiny force. He's caught, and spends almost three years as a British prisoner, much of it on prison ships.

## Part 2D

In the summer of seventeen seventy-seven, a big British army comes south from Canada. <short pause> At Hubbardton, Vermont, the only Revolutionary War battle actually fought in Vermont, the Green Mountain Boys fight a rear-guard battle so the American army can escape.

Weeks later, the British send soldiers to grab supplies stored at Bennington. <gasp> Militia from Vermont and New Hampshire smash them. The Battle of Bennington. <chuckle> Vermont still celebrates it every August, with a state holiday.

<short pause> A stone obelisk more than three hundred feet tall marks the battle. It's still the tallest structure in Vermont.

## Part 3: Republic of Vermont

But Vermonters don't want to be part of New York. So in January seventeen seventy-seven, they declare independence. From Britain. <laugh> And from New York.

They call it the Republic of New Connecticut. Then, Vermont.

<short pause> That July, they write a constitution. It does things no other constitution in America does yet. It bans adult slavery. And it lets men vote even if they don't own property.

<short pause> In the town of Rutland, a Black minister named Lemuel Haynes, who fought in the Revolution, preaches to a white congregation for thirty years. <gasp> He's believed to be the first Black man ordained as a minister by a mainstream Protestant church in America.

## Part 3B

But the Continental Congress refuses to recognise Vermont. New York won't allow it.

So Vermont runs itself. For fourteen years. It has its own governor and legislature. It makes its own copper coins, with a sun rising over the mountains. It runs its own post office. <chuckle> It even talks with foreign countries.

<whispers> And Ethan Allen and his brother Ira secretly negotiate with the British. About Vermont maybe joining Canada.

<short pause> It might have been a trick to pressure Congress. Or it might not. Nobody's completely sure.

## Part 3C

Finally, in seventeen ninety, Vermont agrees to pay New York thirty thousand dollars to drop its claims.

<gasp> And on March fourth, seventeen ninety-one, Vermont joins the United States. The fourteenth state. The first new one after the original thirteen.

## Part 4: Fourteenth State

Vermont stays a land of small farms, sheep, dairy cows and stone.

<groan> In eighteen sixteen, a volcano on the other side of the world, in Indonesia, has blasted so much ash into the sky that the summer never comes. In Vermont, it snows in June. Frost kills the crops in July and August. <short pause> People call it "Eighteen Hundred and Froze to Death". Thousands of Vermonters give up and move west.

## Part 4B

<chuckle> A lot of famous people leave Vermont. Joseph Smith, who founds the Latter-day Saint church, is born in Vermont. So is Brigham Young, who leads it to Utah. John Deere, born in Rutland, moves to Illinois and builds the steel plough that breaks open the prairie. Elisha Otis, born in Halifax, Vermont, invents a safety brake for elevators. <short pause> Without it, there might be no skyscrapers.

<short pause> In eighteen twenty-three, Alexander Twilight graduates from Middlebury College in Vermont. He's thought to be the first Black person to earn a degree from an American college. <chuckle> He later builds a huge granite schoolhouse with his own hands, and in eighteen thirty-six, becomes the first Black person elected to a state legislature.

<laugh> Then, in the eighteen-twenties and thirties, Vermont goes crazy for sheep. Fine-wooled merino sheep, from Spain. At the peak, there are about six sheep for every person in the state.

Its quarries ship marble and granite across America. Barre, Vermont, becomes the granite capital of the world, cutting gravestones and monuments.

<short pause> Stonecutters come from Italy and Scotland to carve it. <chuckle> In Barre's cemetery, their gravestones are works of art. One is a granite armchair. Another is a life-size racing car.

## Part 4C

<gasp> In eighteen forty-eight, near the town of Cavendish, a railroad foreman named Phineas Gage is packing explosives into a rock with a long iron rod. <groan> The powder goes off. The rod shoots straight through his head, and lands about eighty feet away.

<phew> He survives. He even talks to the doctor. But his personality changes. Doctors study him for the next hundred and fifty years, and learn how the front of the brain shapes who we are.

## Part 4D

In the Civil War, Vermont sends a bigger share of its men than almost any other state.

<gasp> At Gettysburg, on the third day, a brigade of Vermont farm boys, most of them in their first battle, swings out sideways and fires into the flank of Pickett's Charge. It helps break the attack.

<gasp> And in October eighteen sixty-four, twenty-one Confederate soldiers in disguise, who had slipped into Canada, ride into the little town of St. Albans, Vermont. They rob three banks, try to set the town on fire, and gallop back over the border.

<chuckle> It's the northernmost land action of the Civil War. In Vermont.

## Part 4E

<short pause> In the little town of Jericho, a farmer named Wilson Bentley spends the winters of his life photographing snowflakes. <gasp> Starting in eighteen eighty-five, he takes more than five thousand pictures of single snow crystals, through a microscope, in the freezing cold. <chuckle> He never finds two alike. People call him "Snowflake" Bentley.

<short pause> In the eighteen-nineties, a famous English writer lives in a house near Brattleboro, Vermont, with his American wife. <chuckle> There, in the snowy Vermont winters, Rudyard Kipling writes about a boy raised by wolves in the jungles of India. The Jungle Book.

## Part 4F

In August nineteen twenty-three, Vice President Calvin Coolidge is visiting his father's farmhouse in Plymouth Notch, Vermont. There's no electricity. No telephone.

<whispers> In the middle of the night, news arrives. President Harding has died.

<short pause> So, by the light of a kerosene lamp, Coolidge's father, a local notary, swears his son in as President of the United States. <chuckle> Then Coolidge goes back to bed.

## Part 4G (quiet style)

In November nineteen twenty-seven, days of heavy rain cause the worst flood in Vermont's history.

<breath> Rivers sweep away homes, bridges and railroads. The lieutenant governor himself is drowned.

<long pause> Eighty-four people are killed.

## Part 4H (quiet style)

In nineteen thirty-one, after a study of so-called "degenerate" families, Vermont passes a law allowing the state to sterilise people it considers "unfit".

<breath> Many of those targeted are poor, disabled, or of French-Canadian or Abenaki heritage. Some families hide who they are for generations.

<long pause> In twenty twenty-one, Vermont's legislature formally apologises.

## Part 5: Today

<chuckle> In nineteen thirty-four, near Woodstock, skiers rig America's first ski tow, using a long rope and the engine of a Model T Ford. Vermont becomes ski country.

<short pause> In nineteen forty-two, a singing family who fled the Nazis in Austria buys a farm near Stowe, because the mountains remind them of home. The von Trapps. Their story becomes "The Sound of Music". <chuckle> Their family still runs a lodge there.

<short pause> The poet Robert Frost spends many of his summers on a farm in Vermont. He's buried in Bennington. His gravestone says: "I had a lover's quarrel with the world."

## Part 5B

In nineteen sixty-eight, Vermont bans billboards, to protect its views.

<short pause> Two years later, it passes one of the toughest land-development laws in America, Act Two-Fifty, so big projects have to prove they won't spoil the land.

<laugh> In nineteen seventy-eight, two old friends, Ben Cohen and Jerry Greenfield, take a five-dollar correspondence course in ice cream making, and open a shop in a gas station in Burlington. <chuckle> Ben has almost no sense of smell, so they add big chunks to the ice cream, for texture.

<laugh> When a flavour is discontinued, it gets a headstone, in a little graveyard at their factory in Waterbury. The Flavour Graveyard.

<chuckle> In maple season, Vermonters pour boiling syrup onto fresh snow, where it hardens into chewy, sticky taffy. Sugar on snow. <laugh> Traditionally, it's served with a plain doughnut... and a sour pickle, to cut the sweetness.

<short pause> Montpelier, with fewer than nine thousand people, is the smallest state capital in America.

<short pause> And in nineteen fifty-seven, IBM builds a computer chip factory near Burlington. For decades, it's Vermont's biggest private employer.

And in two thousand, Vermont becomes the first state to create civil unions for same-sex couples.

## Part 5C (quiet style)

In August two thousand eleven, Tropical Storm Irene drops so much rain on Vermont that rivers tear through towns, and wash away hundreds of roads and bridges.

<breath> In July twenty twenty-three, floods fill the streets of Montpelier, the capital, again.

## Part 5D

So why was Vermont its own country... and why did it give that up?

<whispers> It starts with a gang called the Green Mountain Boys.

They fought for their land against New York. They fought for America against Britain. <short pause> And when nobody would make them a state, they made themselves a country, and ran it their own way. Only when New York finally gave up did Vermont agree to join.

<breath> Vermont didn't join the United States because it had to. It joined because it finally got what it wanted.

<chuckle> And it's been doing things its own way ever since. No billboards. Lots of syrup.
