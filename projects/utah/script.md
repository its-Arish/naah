## Hook

Utah's official state snack? <short pause> Jell-O.

Utah's state bird is the California gull. A seagull. In the desert.

The Great Salt Lake is so salty, you float on it like a cork.

<long pause> In eighteen fifty-seven, a militia in Utah killed about a hundred and twenty men, women and children travelling west in a wagon train.

And Utah asked to become a state six times... and was turned down every time.

<short pause> So how does a group of refugees, chased out of every place they lived, build a state in a desert nobody else wanted?

<whispers> It starts with a plague of crickets.

## Part 1: Ancient Utah

Utah is a land of red rock. Arches, canyons and stone towers carved by wind and water. And under it, some of the richest dinosaur bones on Earth.

Long ago, the Fremont people and the Ancestral Puebloans farm here and paint and carve thousands of figures onto the canyon walls.

Later, the land belongs to the Ute, the Shoshone, the Paiute, the Goshute and the Navajo. The state is named after the Ute.

## Part 1B

In seventeen seventy-six, the same year as the Declaration of Independence, two Spanish priests, Domínguez and Escalante, wander through Utah, looking for a route to California. They don't find it.

Later, fur trappers arrive. One of them, Jim Bridger, tastes the water of a giant lake and finds it salty. He thinks he's reached the Pacific Ocean.

<chuckle> He hasn't. It's the Great Salt Lake. The ocean is still about six hundred miles away.

## Part 2: Deseret

Now, the refugees.

In eighteen thirty, in New York, a young man named Joseph Smith founds a new church. The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Its members are often called Mormons.

Wherever they go, their neighbours turn against them. Their beliefs are unusual. They vote together. They grow fast.

They're driven out of Ohio. Then out of Missouri, where the governor orders that they be driven out of the state... or exterminated.

## Part 2B (quiet style)

They build a city in Illinois called Nauvoo. But in eighteen forty-four, a mob storms the jail where Joseph Smith is being held.

He is shot and killed.

## Part 2C

The church's new leader, Brigham Young, makes a decision. They will go west. Beyond the United States. Somewhere nobody will bother them.

In eighteen forty-six and forty-seven, thousands of them cross the plains in wagon trains.

And on July twenty-fourth, eighteen forty-seven, Young looks down from the mountains into a dry valley next to the salty lake, and says, "This is the right place."

At that moment, the valley isn't even in the United States. It's part of Mexico.

<chuckle> A year later, after the Mexican-American War, it's in the United States again. They can't escape.

## Part 3: The Crickets

They dig ditches from the mountain streams, and flood the dry ground. They plant crops.

And in the spring of eighteen forty-eight, the crops come up green. The settlers have nothing else to eat. Everything depends on this harvest.

And then, the crickets come.

Huge, black, armoured crickets. Millions of them. They swarm over the fields, eating everything. The settlers beat them with brooms, drown them in ditches, set fires. Nothing works.

## Part 3B

Then, from the lake, seagulls arrive. Thousands of them.

They land in the fields, and eat the crickets. They eat until they're full, drink water, throw up, and eat some more.

Enough of the harvest is saved. The settlers survive.

To them, it's a miracle. Today, there's a monument to the seagulls in Salt Lake City. And the California gull is Utah's state bird.

## Part 3C

After the crickets, they build. And they build fast. Irrigation canals. Hundreds of towns, each laid out on the same neat grid, with wide streets.

They call their land Deseret, a word from their scripture meaning "honeybee". A symbol of hard work together. The beehive is still on Utah's flag and highway signs.

But Congress won't make Deseret a state. It makes it a territory, and names it after the Ute. Utah.

## Part 4: Conflict

And the church openly practises polygamy. Some men have more than one wife. Much of America is outraged.

In eighteen fifty-seven, the president sends an army toward Utah. The territory prepares for war. Fear and rumours spread everywhere.

## Part 4B (quiet style)

In September eighteen fifty-seven, a wagon train of families from Arkansas, heading to California, camps at a place called Mountain Meadows.

Local militiamen, members of the church, along with some Paiute men they recruit, attack them. After a siege, the militia promises the emigrants safe passage. Then they kill about a hundred and twenty people. Only small children are spared.

Years later, only one man, John D. Lee, is ever executed for it. In two thousand seven, the church formally expressed its regret.

## Part 4C (quiet style)

The settlers also take more and more of the land and water of the Ute, the Shoshone and the Paiute. In eighteen sixty-three, at Bear River, soldiers kill hundreds of Shoshone people. One of the deadliest attacks on Native Americans in the West.

## Part 5: Railroad

On May tenth, eighteen sixty-nine, at Promontory Summit, in Utah, two railroads meet. One built from the east, by many Irish workers. One built from the west, by many Chinese workers.

A golden spike is tapped in. The first railroad across America is complete. A telegraph sends one word to the whole country. "Done."

Utah isn't isolated any more.

## Part 5B

And Utah does something surprising. In eighteen seventy, it gives women the vote. A schoolteacher named Seraph Young becomes the first woman to vote under an equal suffrage law in the United States.

But Congress, trying to crush polygamy, passes a law that takes the vote away from Utah's women again.

## Part 6: Statehood

Utah keeps asking to be a state. Six times. Six rejections. Always because of polygamy.

So in eighteen ninety, the church's president announces that the church will stop new plural marriages.

And on January fourth, eighteen ninety-six, Utah becomes the forty-fifth state. Its women get the vote back.

That same year, Utah elects the first woman state senator in America. Martha Hughes Cannon. A doctor. <chuckle> One of the people she beat in the election? Her own husband.

## Part 6B

In the twentieth century, miners dig the Bingham Canyon copper mine. It becomes one of the biggest man-made holes on Earth.

Utah's red rock country becomes national parks. Zion. Bryce Canyon. Arches. Canyonlands. Capitol Reef.

And a farm boy from Utah, Philo Farnsworth, grows up to invent the first all-electronic television.

## Part 6C (quiet style)

During the Second World War, Japanese American families are held at Topaz, a camp in the Utah desert.

## Part 6D

In two thousand two, Salt Lake City hosts the Winter Olympics.

Today, Utah is one of the fastest-growing states, with one of the youngest populations in America. Tech companies fill the valley between Salt Lake and Provo. People call it Silicon Slopes.

And in two thousand one, Utah's lawmakers made Jell-O the official state snack. <chuckle> Utahns eat a lot of it.

## Part 6E

So how does a group of refugees, chased out of every place they lived, build a state in a desert nobody else wanted?

It starts with a plague of crickets.

When the crickets came, the settlers had nowhere else to go. They had to make the desert work, or die. And when the gulls came, they took it as a sign to keep going. They dug the canals, built the towns, and did everything together, like bees in a hive.

<short pause> Nobody wanted that valley. That's exactly why they chose it.

<chuckle> And they never forgot to thank the seagulls.
