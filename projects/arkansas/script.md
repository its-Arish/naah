## Hook

Walmart? <short pause> Started in Arkansas.

In Arkansas, there's a diamond mine where anyone can dig. And whatever you find, you keep.

Arkansas even has a law about how to pronounce its own name.

<long pause> In nineteen fifty-seven, nine Black teenagers tried to go to high school in Little Rock. The governor sent soldiers to keep them out. The president sent soldiers to get them in.

<short pause> So how does one of the poorest, most rural states in America give birth to the biggest company in the world?

<whispers> It starts with a little dime store on a town square.

## Part 1: First Peoples

Arkansas has two faces. In the northwest, the green, rugged Ozark and Ouachita mountains. In the east, the flat, rich Mississippi Delta.

The Caddo build mound towns and make beautiful pottery. The Osage hunt in the Ozarks. And along the rivers live the Quapaw.

Other nations call the Quapaw a name that sounds like "Akansa". French explorers write it down. And that's where "Arkansas" comes from.

## Part 1B

In fifteen forty-one, Hernando de Soto marches through, looking for gold. In sixteen eighty-six, the French build a little trading post near where the Arkansas River meets the Mississippi. Arkansas Post. The first European settlement in the lower Mississippi Valley.

## Part 2: Arkansas Post

After the Louisiana Purchase, Arkansas becomes American. Settlers come. Hunters, trappers, and farmers carving homesteads out of the forest.

In eighteen thirty-two, Congress protects something special. In the Ouachita Mountains, hot water bubbles out of the ground at about a hundred and forty degrees. People have soaked in it for centuries.

Congress sets the springs aside as a federal reservation. Forty years before Yellowstone becomes the first national park.

Hot Springs becomes "the American Spa". Grand bathhouses line the street. And later, famous gangsters like Al Capone come to soak, gamble and hide out.

<chuckle> Baseball teams come too. Some of the very first spring training camps happen in Hot Springs.

## Part 2B

In eighteen thirty-six, Arkansas becomes the twenty-fifth state. A slave state. Cotton plantations spread across the Delta.

## Part 2C (quiet style)

Through Arkansas pass the Cherokee, the Choctaw and other nations on their forced journey west, on the Trail of Tears. Many die along the way.

## Part 3: Sharecroppers

In eighteen sixty-one, Arkansas joins the Confederacy. After the war, enslaved people are freed. But most Black families in the Delta, and many white families too, end up sharecropping. Farming someone else's land for a share of the crop. Usually ending each year in debt.

## Part 3B

And in eighteen eighty-one, Arkansas has an argument. How do you say the state's name?

Its two U.S. senators even say it differently. One says "Ar-KAN-sas". The other says "AR-kan-saw".

<chuckle> So the legislature passes a resolution. It's "AR-kan-saw". It's still the official pronunciation today.

## Part 3C (quiet style)

In nineteen-nineteen, Black sharecroppers near Elaine, Arkansas, meet in a church to form a union and demand fair prices for their cotton.

Shots are fired outside the church. Then white mobs and soldiers sweep the area.

Over the next days, more than a hundred Black people are killed. Some historians believe the number is in the hundreds. It's one of the deadliest racial massacres in American history.

## Part 4: Hard Times

In nineteen-oh-six, a farmer named John Huddleston finds two strange, shiny crystals on his land near Murfreesboro.

Diamonds. Sitting in the eroded crater of an ancient volcano.

Today, it's a state park. Crater of Diamonds. The only place in the world where the public can dig for diamonds in their original volcanic source. You pay a small fee, dig in the dirt, and keep anything you find. Visitors find hundreds of diamonds every year.

## Part 4B

But Arkansas stays poor. In nineteen twenty-seven, the Great Mississippi Flood covers much of the Delta. Then the Great Depression and drought hit.

In the nineteen-thirties, the government builds a farming colony for poor families at Dyess, Arkansas. One of those families has a little boy named J.R. Cash.

He grows up picking cotton and singing. He becomes Johnny Cash.

## Part 5: Little Rock

In nineteen fifty-four, the Supreme Court rules that segregated schools are unconstitutional.

In September nineteen fifty-seven, nine Black students try to enter Little Rock's Central High School.

Governor Orval Faubus orders the Arkansas National Guard to block them. White crowds scream and threaten them.

So President Eisenhower sends in the U.S. Army. The famous hundred-and-first Airborne Division. Soldiers escort the nine students to class every day.

They're called the Little Rock Nine. They show America that the law will be enforced.

## Part 6: Small Towns, Big Stores

Now, the dime store.

In nineteen fifty, a young man named Sam Walton buys a small variety store on the town square of Bentonville, a little town in northwest Arkansas. Fewer than three thousand people.

Big chain stores ignore towns like Bentonville. Too small. Too far away. Not worth it.

Sam Walton thinks the opposite. Small towns are everywhere. And people there want low prices too.

## Part 6B

In nineteen sixty-two, in nearby Rogers, he opens a bigger discount store. Walmart.

He builds his stores in small towns, close together, and supplies them from giant warehouses with his own trucks. He cuts prices. And keeps cutting them.

Small town by small town, Walmart spreads across America. Then the world.

Today, Walmart is the biggest company on Earth by revenue. And its headquarters is still in Bentonville.

## Part 6C

And Walmart isn't alone. In the same corner of Arkansas, Tyson Foods becomes one of the biggest meat companies in the world. And J.B. Hunt becomes one of the biggest trucking companies.

And a boy born in a little town called Hope, Arkansas, Bill Clinton, becomes governor, and then president of the United States in nineteen ninety-three.

## Part 6D

So how does one of the poorest, most rural states in America give birth to the biggest company in the world?

It starts with a little dime store on a town square.

Arkansas was full of small towns everyone else ignored. Sam Walton grew up in towns like that, and opened his store in one. He knew those customers. And he built an empire by serving them.

<short pause> Arkansas was too small and too rural for the big companies. So it built its own.

<chuckle> And if you ever find yourself in Murfreesboro... bring a shovel.
