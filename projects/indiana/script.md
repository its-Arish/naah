## Hook

The Indy 500? <short pause> The biggest single-day sporting event on Earth.

Abraham Lincoln? He grew up in Indiana. Not Illinois.

In eighteen ninety-seven, Indiana's lawmakers almost passed a law that said pi equals three point two.

There's a town in Indiana called Santa Claus. Every Christmas, kids send it thousands of letters.

<long pause> And in the nineteen-twenties, the Ku Klux Klan practically ran Indiana. Hundreds of thousands of members. Its governor. Its legislature.

<short pause> So how does a quiet farm state end up hosting the biggest one-day sporting event in the world?

<whispers> It starts with a race track paved with three million bricks.

## Part 1: First Peoples

A thousand years ago, on the Ohio River, people of the Mississippian culture build a town of mounds and wooden houses. Today it's called Angel Mounds.

Later, the forests and prairies of Indiana are home to the Miami, the Potawatomi, the Shawnee, the Lenape and others. The name "Indiana" means "land of the Indians".

## Part 1B

In seventeen thirty-two, French traders build a fort at Vincennes, on the Wabash River. During the Revolution, an American frontiersman, George Rogers Clark, leads his men through freezing floodwaters, sometimes up to their shoulders, to capture it from the British.

## Part 2: Frontier

In eighteen-eleven, the Shawnee leader Tecumseh and his brother, the Prophet, gather nations at Prophetstown, on the Wabash, to resist the Americans. While Tecumseh is away, Governor William Henry Harrison attacks. The Battle of Tippecanoe.

<chuckle> Harrison turns it into a campaign slogan, and decades later, rides it into the White House. "Tippecanoe and Tyler too!"

## Part 2B

In December eighteen sixteen, Indiana becomes the nineteenth state.

The same month, a family crosses the Ohio River from Kentucky into the deep woods of southern Indiana. The father is Thomas Lincoln. His son is seven years old. Abraham.

Abe grows up in Indiana. He clears land with an axe. He reads every book he can borrow. He spends fourteen years here, from seven to twenty-one.

## Part 2C (quiet style)

When Abe is nine, his mother, Nancy, drinks milk from a cow that has eaten a poisonous plant. It's called milk sickness. She dies.

Abe helps his father make her coffin.

## Part 2D (quiet style)

In eighteen thirty-eight, soldiers force more than eight hundred Potawatomi people out of northern Indiana at gunpoint. They are marched to Kansas. More than forty die on the way, many of them children.

The Potawatomi call it the Trail of Death.

## Part 3: Gas Boom

People from Indiana are called "Hoosiers". Nobody knows exactly why. There are dozens of theories. <chuckle> None of them are proven.

## Part 3B

In the eighteen-eighties, drillers in central Indiana hit natural gas. Huge amounts. Towns light giant gas torches day and night, just to show off. Factories rush in.

The Ball brothers move their glass jar factory to Muncie. Ball jars. In South Bend, the Studebaker brothers build wagons, then cars.

Indiana fills with car makers. Dozens of them. And they need somewhere to test their cars.

## Part 3C

Meanwhile, in eighteen ninety-seven, an amateur mathematician convinces an Indiana lawmaker to introduce a bill. Its maths would make pi equal to three point two.

The House passes it. Sixty-seven to nothing.

<chuckle> But a Purdue maths professor happens to be visiting the statehouse that day. He explains the problem. The Senate puts the bill away. Forever.

## Part 4: Brickyard

Now, the bricks.

In nineteen-oh-nine, a businessman named Carl Fisher builds a giant oval track in Indianapolis. A place for car companies to test their cars, and race them.

The first races are a disaster. The track is gravel and tar. It breaks apart. Drivers and spectators are killed.

So Fisher repaves it. With bricks. About three point two million of them.

<chuckle> Yes. Three point two. Indiana really loves that number.

People start calling it the Brickyard.

## Part 4B

On May thirtieth, nineteen eleven, the first Indianapolis five hundred is held. Five hundred miles. Forty cars. The winner averages about seventy-five miles an hour.

Today, the cars go over two hundred miles an hour. More than three hundred thousand people come to watch. It's the biggest single-day sporting event in the world.

And at the finish line, one yard of the original bricks is still there. Winners kneel down and kiss it.

## Part 5: Dark Years (quiet style)

In the nineteen-twenties, the Ku Klux Klan grows huge in Indiana. Hundreds of thousands of members. It attacks Black people, Catholics, Jews and immigrants. It helps elect the governor and many lawmakers.

Then, in nineteen twenty-five, its leader, D.C. Stephenson, is convicted of the murder of a young woman named Madge Oberholtzer.

The Klan's power in Indiana collapses.

## Part 5C

Indiana is crazy about basketball. They call it Hoosier Hysteria.

In nineteen fifty-four, a tiny high school from the town of Milan, with fewer than two hundred students, wins the state championship against a big-city school. It becomes the movie "Hoosiers".

## Part 5D

And Indiana gives the world the Jackson Five, from Gary. Purdue University trains so many astronauts, including Neil Armstrong, that it calls itself the "Cradle of Astronauts".

In the town of Santa Claus, volunteers called "elves" answer every letter.

And Indiana couldn't decide about daylight saving time for decades. Different counties, different clocks. <chuckle> The whole state only started springing forward together in two thousand six.

## Part 5E

So how does a quiet farm state end up hosting the biggest one-day sporting event in the world?

It starts with a race track paved with three million bricks.

Indiana's gas boom brought factories, and the factories brought car makers. The car makers needed a test track. A businessman built one in Indianapolis, and paved it with bricks. And the race he started there grew into the biggest party in sport.

<short pause> Indiana calls itself the Crossroads of America. And for one day every May, the whole world drives through it.

<chuckle> At about two hundred and thirty miles an hour.
