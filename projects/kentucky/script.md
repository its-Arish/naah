## Hook

Kentucky Fried Chicken? <short pause> Started at a gas station in Kentucky.

The tune of "Happy Birthday"? Written by two sisters in Kentucky.

There are more barrels of bourbon ageing in Kentucky than there are people.

And under Kentucky lies the longest known cave system on Earth.

<long pause> During the Civil War, Kentucky families sent their sons to fight and die on both sides.

And the two presidents who led that war, Abraham Lincoln and Jefferson Davis, were both born in Kentucky. Less than a year apart.

<short pause> So how does one state give birth to both sides of the Civil War?

<whispers> It starts with a gap in the mountains.

## Part 1: First Peoples

Under Kentucky, water has been eating through limestone for millions of years. It's made a maze of tunnels and caverns. Mammoth Cave. More than four hundred miles of mapped passages. And it keeps getting longer as explorers find more.

People were exploring it with cane torches four thousand years ago.

On the surface, the Shawnee, the Cherokee and other nations hunt in the forests and meadows. Few live there year round. For many, it's a hunting ground shared, and fought over.

## Part 2: The Gap

To the east, the Appalachian Mountains stand like a wall between the colonies and the West.

But there's a door. A notch in the mountains, where Virginia, Kentucky and Tennessee meet today. Animals have used it for thousands of years. Then Native trails.

The Cumberland Gap.

## Part 2B

In seventeen sixty-nine, a hunter from North Carolina named Daniel Boone goes through the gap. He spends almost two years in Kentucky, hunting. He falls in love with it.

In seventeen seventy-five, Boone and a crew of axemen cut a path through the gap. The Wilderness Road.

And then, the people come. Tens of thousands. Then hundreds of thousands. Families walking, with wagons, cattle and pigs.

Most of them come from Virginia and the Carolinas. And many bring something with them. Enslaved people.

## Part 2C

The settlers fight the Shawnee and their allies for years. Boone's own son is killed. Boone himself is captured by the Shawnee, adopted into the nation, and escapes.

In seventeen ninety-two, Kentucky becomes the fifteenth state. The first state west of the Appalachian Mountains.

## Part 2D

Kentucky's farmers grow lots of corn. Too much to carry over the mountains. So they turn it into something easier to carry, and worth more.

Whiskey.

Aged in charred oak barrels, it becomes a sweet, smoky whiskey called bourbon.

Today, around ninety-five percent of the world's bourbon is made in Kentucky.

## Part 3: Bluegrass

In the middle of Kentucky, the limestone under the soil makes grass rich in calcium. Horses raised on it grow strong bones. The Bluegrass region becomes the horse capital of America.

And Kentucky grows hemp and tobacco, worked by enslaved people.

## Part 3B (quiet style)

Kentucky is a slave state. But its north side is the Ohio River. On the other bank, Ohio, Indiana and Illinois are free.

So enslaved people in Kentucky can see freedom across the water. Many try to cross.

And slave traders in Louisville sell thousands of people to the cotton plantations of the Deep South. Down the Ohio. Down the Mississippi. It's where the phrase "sold down the river" comes from.

## Part 3C

Inside Mammoth Cave, an enslaved teenager named Stephen Bishop becomes its most famous guide. He crosses a deep pit on a ladder, discovers underground rivers and blind fish, and draws a map of the cave that's used for decades.

## Part 3D

And in a tiny log cabin in Kentucky, in eighteen-oh-nine, a boy is born. Abraham Lincoln.

Less than a year before, about a hundred miles away, another boy was born. Jefferson Davis.

Two families. Same state. Then both families leave.

Lincoln's family crosses the Ohio River north, to Indiana. A free state. Lincoln later said his father moved "partly on account of slavery".

Davis's family moves south, to Mississippi. Cotton country. Davis grows up to own a plantation, and enslaved people.

Two boys from the same state. One goes north. One goes south.

## Part 4: Divided

In eighteen sixty-one, the war comes. Lincoln leads the Union. Davis leads the Confederacy.

And Kentucky? Kentucky tries to stay neutral.

Lincoln knows how important it is. He reportedly says, "I hope to have God on my side, but I must have Kentucky."

Kentucky stays in the Union. But it keeps slavery. And its people split.

## Part 4B (quiet style)

Around a hundred thousand Kentuckians fight for the Union. Around thirty-five thousand fight for the Confederacy.

Brothers fight brothers. Even Lincoln's own wife, Mary Todd Lincoln, from Lexington, has brothers who die fighting for the South.

## Part 4C

And after the war, Kentucky refuses to ratify the Thirteenth Amendment, which ends slavery. It finally does it, symbolically... in nineteen seventy-six.

## Part 5: Horses and Coal

In eighteen seventy-five, Louisville holds its first big horse race. The Kentucky Derby.

The winning jockey of that first Derby is a Black teenager, Oliver Lewis. In fact, Black jockeys win fifteen of the first twenty-eight Derbies.

## Part 5B

In eighteen ninety-three, two sisters in Louisville, Mildred and Patty Hill, write a song for kindergarten children, called "Good Morning to All".

Later, someone changes the words. "Happy birthday to you."

<chuckle> It becomes one of the most-sung songs in the English language.

## Part 5C

In the eastern mountains, coal mining takes over. Companies build towns, own the houses and the stores, and sometimes pay miners in company money.

In the nineteen-thirties, in Harlan County, miners fight to join a union. The fighting is so violent that people call it "Bloody Harlan".

## Part 5D

In the nineteen thirties, a man named Harland Sanders runs a gas station in Corbin. He starts cooking fried chicken for travellers, with a secret mix of eleven herbs and spices.

The governor makes him an honorary Kentucky colonel. He keeps the title. And the white suit.

Kentucky Fried Chicken.

## Part 5E

In nineteen thirty-seven, the U.S. government builds a giant vault at Fort Knox, Kentucky, to store much of America's gold.

And from the hills comes a new music. Bill Monroe and his Blue Grass Boys. Bluegrass.

## Part 6: Champions

In Louisville, in nineteen fifty-four, a twelve-year-old boy's bicycle is stolen. He tells a police officer he wants to beat up the thief. The officer, who coaches boxing, tells him he'd better learn to fight first.

The boy's name is Cassius Clay. He becomes Muhammad Ali. "The Greatest".

## Part 6B (quiet style)

In December two thousand twenty-one, a long-track tornado tears across western Kentucky. The town of Mayfield is shattered. Dozens of people are killed across the state.

## Part 6C

Today, Kentucky builds Toyotas, ages millions of barrels of bourbon, and still breeds some of the fastest horses on Earth.

## Part 6D

So how does one state give birth to both sides of the Civil War?

It starts with a gap in the mountains.

Through the Cumberland Gap came families from the slave-holding South. They built a slave state, right on the edge of the free North. Kentucky was the border. The meeting place.

So when two boys were born there, one family went north, toward freedom. The other went south, toward cotton. And when they grew up, they led the two sides of the same war.

<short pause> Kentucky was the doorway. America walked through it in two different directions.

<chuckle> And on the way, it picked up some bourbon and a bucket of chicken.
